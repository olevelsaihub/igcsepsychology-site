Three mountains task (Piaget and Inhelder, 1956)

Choice

In the Three mountains task, younger children picked their own view instead of the doll's: they were egocentric. Learn the numbers: 3 mountains, 10 pictures, 100 children.

4

The method

  1. Recall the numbers

    3 mountains, 10 pictures, 100 children.

  2. State the finding

    Younger children chose their own view: egocentrism.

  3. Name the stage

    Not taking another view points to the pre-operational stage.

  4. Use the data

    For tables, state the trend with age and quote the figures.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Swapped numbers

    Options swap 3, 10 and 100. Check which one is asked.

  • ✗
    No stage

    If asked which stage, name pre-operational, not only egocentric.

  • ✗
    No figures

    A conclusion from a table needs numbers from it.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the table: 26 of 30 six-year-olds gave their own view, but only 3 of 30 ten-year-olds did.
  2. Conclusion: the ability to take another person's perspective increases with age and is mostly present by 10.
  3. Support it with the figures.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Most children appear to have lost their egocentrism by the age of 10, so they can imagine the view of someone else. 27 out of 30 ten-year-olds said someone across town would see the rectangular building nearest to them, even though from where the pupils stood it was at the back, compared with only 4 out of 30 six-year-olds.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the conclusion about perspective taking by age 10; one mark for supporting data from Table 1.
