Treating offenders: token economy and anger management · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Question 12 · part (whole) · 9 marks

*12 Bailsford Prison houses prisoners who have committed serious offences. The prison guards have noticed a significant increase in fights and assaults amongst prisoners. The managers discuss implementing a token economy programme. Behaviours such as ‘being polite to others’ and ‘not getting into a fight for a week’ will be rewarded with a token. These can then be exchanged for items from the prison shop once 10 tokens have been acquired. When informed about the programme, prisoners complain that guards have their favourite prisoners, and the prison shop does not sell anything they want to buy. Assess whether a token economy programme could help improve the behaviour of the prisoners. (9)

Worked explanation

  1. AO1: token economy rests on operant conditioning; tokens are secondary reinforcers swapped for wanted items.
  2. AO2: target behaviours (polite, no fights for a week); 10 tokens; prison shop.
  3. AO3: research support with young offenders; vague targets; favouritism; nothing worth buying; may not last after release.
  4. Judgement.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A token economy uses operant conditioning to change behaviour. Each time a person shows a chosen pro-social behaviour they receive a token straight away. The token is a secondary reinforcer: it has no value on its own but can be exchanged for things the person wants, such as food, phone calls or extra visits. Because good behaviour is followed by reward, it should be repeated.

At Bailsford, prisoners would earn a token for being polite or for going a week without fighting. After ten tokens they could buy items from the prison shop. If this worked, the rewards would make polite, calm behaviour more frequent and cut the fights and assaults the guards have noticed.

Research with young offenders has shown that rewarding cooperative behaviour with tokens increased those behaviours, which suggests it could help here. However, the prisoners' complaints point to serious problems. A reinforcer only works if it is wanted, and they say the shop sells nothing they want, so the tokens would have little value. They also believe guards have favourites, and 'being polite' is vague, so tokens may be handed out unfairly or inconsistently, which weakens the link between behaviour and reward. Needing ten tokens, with one target taking a week, means the reward is delayed, which is hard for impulsive prisoners. Even if behaviour improves, it may stop once the prisoners leave and the tokens end.

Overall, a token economy could reduce fighting at Bailsford, but only if the shop offers rewards the prisoners actually want and guards apply clear, fair rules. As planned, it is unlikely to succeed.

How the marks are earned

Level 3: correct mechanism (AO1), detailed use of the scenario's targets, 10 tokens and shop (AO2), and evaluation using research, consistency, motivation and long-term effects with a clear judgement (AO3).
