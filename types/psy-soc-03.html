Why bystanders do or do not help

Written

Whether a bystander helps depends on situational factors (other people, cost, ambiguity) and personal factors (skills, similarity, mood).

1

Will anyone help?

5 factors to find

Find three situational factors and two personal ones.

  1. Diffusion of responsibility
  2. Cost of helping
  3. Ambiguity
  4. Competence
  5. Similarity

Diffusion of responsibility

Situational
Each person assumes someone else will help.

Cost of helping

Situational
Stopping would make them late.

Ambiguity

Situational
If unsure it is an emergency, people wait.

Competence

Personal
Skills make helping more likely.

Similarity

Personal
People help those like themselves.

The method

  1. Which kind?

    Is a personal or a situational factor asked for?

  2. Name and explain

    Say in one sentence how it changes helping.

  3. Point to the scene

    Quote the detail that shows it.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong kind

    Personal = about the helper; situational = about the setting.

  • ✗
    Unexplained label

    Say how the factor works.

  • ✗
    No scenario detail

    Use crowd size, hurry, the victim.

Worked explanation

  1. Note the situational detail: many other students saw the bullying.
  2. Choose diffusion of responsibility.
  3. Explain it, then link it to Keira.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Diffusion of responsibility: when lots of people witness an incident, each person feels less personally responsible for doing something because they assume someone else will step in. Lots of other pupils saw the younger boy being insulted and tripped, so Keira may have assumed one of them, or a teacher they told, would deal with it, and walked on to her lesson.

How the marks are earned

One mark for explaining diffusion of responsibility, one for linking it to the many pupils watching Keira: 2 marks.
