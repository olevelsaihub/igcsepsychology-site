Freud's theory of dreaming · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 34 · part (whole) · 9 marks

*34 Aisha has arranged to meet up with a man she met at work. She is nervous that they may not have anything to talk about when they meet. Aisha also worries that he might not be as nice away from work as he is when they chat at work. The night before her meeting, Aisha dreamt she was in a restaurant surrounded by 30 males who all looked like her dad. They made negative comments about her outfit and hairstyle. Aisha could not reply because her mouth had vanished. Aisha dreamt she ran from the restaurant but could not find her way home until a man on a unicorn rescued her. Assess how well Freudian theory of dreaming (Freud, 1900) can explain Aisha’s dream. (9)

Worked explanation

  1. AO1: unconscious wishes, manifest, latent, dreamwork, wish fulfilment.
  2. AO2: men criticising = worry about the date; dad-like men = recent memory/father figure; rescue = wish for protection; vanished mouth = fear of having nothing to say.
  3. AO3: support (Little Hans), weakness (subjective, untestable), alternative (activation synthesis), possible benefit.
  4. Judgement.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Freud (1900) believed dreams let repressed, unconscious thoughts reach awareness in a disguised form. The manifest content is the dream as the person remembers it, often built from recent events and people, while the latent content is its hidden meaning, usually an unconscious wish or fear. Dreamwork, through processes such as symbolisation and displacement, turns the disturbing latent content into the safer manifest story.

Aisha's dream can be read this way. The 30 men criticising her outfit and hair could stand for her hidden fear that the man she is meeting will not like her, or will be less pleasant away from work. Her vanished mouth may symbolise her fear that she will have nothing to say. The men looking like her dad could be drawn from recent memories of her father, or show displacement of feelings about men onto a father figure. Being rescued by a man on a unicorn could be wish fulfilment: her latent wish that the date will go well and that she will find a man who protects her.

Freud supported his theory with case studies such as Little Hans, whose dreams seemed to reveal his fears, and the theory makes sense of how Aisha's dream so closely matches her anxieties the night before the meeting. Dreamwork could even be helpful, letting her process her worry safely. However, interpretations like these are subjective: a different analyst could read the unicorn or the father figures quite differently, and the theory cannot be scientifically tested. Activation Synthesis Theory offers an alternative, suggesting the strange details, like a unicorn, are just the brain making sense of random activity in REM sleep, so the dream may have no hidden meaning at all.

Overall, Freud's theory gives a convincing account of why Aisha's dream reflects her nerves about the meeting, but because its interpretations cannot be tested and a biological explanation can also account for the bizarre content, it explains her dream only partly.

How the marks are earned

Level 3: thorough knowledge of manifest/latent content and dreamwork (AO1); sustained interpretation of the dream's details (AO2); evaluation using supporting evidence, a weakness and a competing theory, ending in a judgement (AO3).
