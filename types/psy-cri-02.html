The method

  1. Find the model

    Who is watched, and why: same sex, similar age or high status.

  2. Name the process

    Identification, vicarious reinforcement or observational learning.

  3. Show the copy

    Point to the act reproduced later.

  4. Use Bandura

    Quote a finding that fits, e.g. boys and physical aggression.

  5. Weigh it up

    In long answers, add other evidence and a conclusion.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Vicarious vs direct

    Vicarious means seeing someone else rewarded.

  • ✗
    Wrong sex finding

    Boys, not girls, copied more physical aggression.

  • ✗
    Study only

    Always link the study back to the person.

Worked explanation

  1. AO1: recall the procedure and key findings of the Bobo doll study.
  2. AO2: link each finding to Delun (male hero, a boy, copying fighting).
  3. AO3: give support (controlled lab) and challenge (Charlton, biology, artificial doll), plus note Delun copies kind acts too.
  4. Conclude with a judgement.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Bandura, Ross and Ross (1961) studied 72 nursery children aged around three to five. Some watched an adult hit, kick and shout at an inflatable Bobo doll, some watched a calm adult and a control group saw no model. Children who saw the aggressive adult later copied many of the same physical and verbal attacks, while the other groups showed far fewer. Boys imitated more of the physical attacks than girls did, and the male adult was imitated more than the female one.

This fits Delun well. Delun admires Captain Michael, a male character, so like the children in the study he is likely to pay attention to him and reproduce what he sees. Delun jumped on and hit a boy, which is physical aggression of the kind boys copied most in the study. He even told the teacher he wanted to be Captain Michael, showing identification with the model. Captain Michael is also kind, and Delun was protecting a younger child, so he may be copying both the helpful and the violent sides of the model.

However, there are limits. The study was a tightly controlled lab experiment, which gives confidence that the model caused the copying, but the children hit a doll that bounces back rather than a real child, so it may not show that real fights are learned this way. The children saw a live adult, while Delun saw a TV character, so the situations differ. When TV arrived on St Helena, Charlton et al. (2000) reported that children did not become more aggressive, suggesting screen violence does not always lead to copying. Biological factors such as hormones or genes may also explain why boys are more physically aggressive.

Overall, the study explains Delun's behaviour quite well because he is a young boy copying a male role model he identifies with, but it cannot fully explain it, since the evidence comes from an artificial setting and other research and biological explanations suggest TV models are only part of the story.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (7-9): accurate study knowledge (AO1), repeated links to Delun's details (AO2), and balanced evaluation using control, ecological validity, Charlton and biology ending in a judgement (AO3).
