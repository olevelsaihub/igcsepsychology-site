Knowledge card

What shapes the self: temperament and experience

Written

Self-esteem is shaped from inside by temperament (an inborn style) and from outside by experience: how others react and who we compare ourselves with.

The method

  1. Inside or outside?

    Temperament is internal; others’ reactions and comparison are external.

  2. Define it

    Say what the influence is.

  3. Show the effect

    Explain how it raises or lowers this person’s self-esteem.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Learned temperament

    Temperament is inborn.

  • ✗
    No effect

    Always say what happens to self-esteem.

  • ✗
    Same detail twice

    Two ways need two different details.

18 Daniel is worried about his mathematics test. He enjoys mathematics but is getting low scores in his homework. He does not understand why, as he feels he is working hard. Daniel speaks to his teacher, who criticises his work, suggesting that he does not work hard enough outside of school. Daniel believes that he will never be good enough at mathematics, especially as his twin sister, Zoe, always gets high scores. Even after speaking to his teacher, Daniel’s scores on his mathematics test and homework remain low. Explain two ways external influences on the self could account for Daniel’s continued low scores in mathematics. 1 ................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................ 2 ................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................

Worked explanation

  1. Way 1: teacher's criticism (reaction of others) lowers belief in his ability.
  2. Way 2: comparing himself with Zoe (social comparison) gives a poor self-image.
  3. Link each to continued low scores.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 Daniel's teacher criticised him and said he does not work hard enough. Argyle argued that how other people respond to us shapes our self-esteem, so this criticism may have lowered Daniel's confidence in his maths, making him less able to improve. 2 Daniel compares himself with his twin Zoe, who always scores highly. Comparing ourselves with people who do better can leave us with a negative self-image, so Daniel believes he will never be good at maths and this lack of belief keeps his scores low.

How the marks are earned

One mark per way identified from the scenario and one per justification using external-influence theory.
