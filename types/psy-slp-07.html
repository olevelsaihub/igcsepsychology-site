Freud's Little Hans case study · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Question 33 · part (whole) · 9 marks

*33 Joel is five years old and suffers with anxiety. He has recently developed a phobia of police officers. Joel has a recurring dream of being in a shop alone. He is searching for his mother, but he cannot find her. Joel then sees many police officers, all wearing glasses, coming towards him in the shop and he runs away. Joel sees his mother getting into a car with a police officer. Joel’s father remembers an incident where Joel witnessed a police officer chase a criminal. The criminal ran past, knocking his father’s glasses off. The police officer caught the criminal and drove them away in a police car. Assess how well the case study of ‘Little Hans’ (Freud, 1909) can account for Joel’s phobia of police officers. (9)

Worked explanation

  1. AO1: horse phobia, blinkers as father's glasses (displacement), Oedipus complex, dreams of losing mother.
  2. AO2: police in glasses = father; dream of losing mother; mother driven away by police officer.
  3. AO3: similarities support; alternative association explanation from the chase incident; single case.
  4. Judgement.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Freud (1909) studied Little Hans, a young boy who developed a phobia of horses, particularly horses wearing blinkers. Freud argued that the horses stood for Hans's father, who wore glasses, so Hans's fear of his father had been displaced onto horses. He explained this through the Oedipus complex: Hans wanted his mother's attention for himself and unconsciously feared his father as a rival. Hans's phobia also appeared around the time he was having recurring dreams in which he lost his mother.

Joel's case has clear parallels. He is five, the same age Hans was, and the police officers in his dream all wear glasses, like his father, so his fear of police could be displaced fear of his father. His recurring dream of searching for his mother in a shop suggests anxiety about losing her, as Hans had. Seeing his mother get into a car with a police officer fits the Oedipal idea of the father figure taking the mother away from him.

These similarities in age, anxiety, dreams and the glasses detail make the case study a strong match for Joel. However, there is a simpler explanation: Joel watched a police officer chase a criminal who knocked his father's glasses off, so his fear could have been learnt by association with that frightening event, just as Hans had seen a horse fall in the street. Little Hans is also a single case, largely reported through his father, so his experiences may be unique and the Oedipal interpretation is subjective and cannot be tested.

Overall, the Little Hans case can partly account for Joel's phobia because the parallels are striking, but the frightening incident Joel witnessed gives a more direct explanation, so Freud's account is not the most convincing one.

How the marks are earned

Level 3: accurate detail of the case (AO1); sustained mapping of Joel's dream, age and glasses onto Hans (AO2); evaluation weighing similarities against association and single-case limits, ending in a judgement (AO3).
