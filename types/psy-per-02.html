The method

  1. Two images

    Each eye gets a slightly different image.

  2. Brain merges

    The brain fuses them into one 3D image.

  3. Apply

    Say which image is missing for the person and what happens to their depth judgement.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Only one eye

    Stereopsis always needs two eyes.

  • ✗
    Forgetting "different"

    The difference between the images is what gives depth.

  • ✗
    No effect stated

    End with what happens to depth perception.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain stereopsis: two slightly different images merged for 3D.
  2. Apply: Katie's damaged eye cannot line up with the other, so the brain lacks a matching second image.
  3. Conclude: her depth judgement suffers.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Stereopsis is a binocular cue: each eye receives a slightly different view of the scene and the brain fuses the two views into one image that has depth. Because nerve damage limits how one of Katie's eyes moves, that eye cannot focus with the other on the same object, so her brain does not get a usable second image and she struggles to perceive depth.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1 for the two-eye fusion explanation of stereopsis; mark 2 for linking Katie's damaged eye to losing the second image and so depth.
