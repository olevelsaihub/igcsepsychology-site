Piliavin et al. (1969): helping on the New York subway

Written

Piliavin et al. (1969) staged collapses on a New York subway. A cane victim got help far more often than a drunk one, and no diffusion of responsibility was found.

Field experiment

Strength
A real setting, so behaviour is natural.

Weakness
No consent was given.

Who did what

Roles
Victim collapsed; model helped if no one else did; observers recorded.

Why
Tested if a helper prompts others.

4 details to find

Find the result that rules out diffusion of responsibility.

  1. Cane: almost always
  2. Drunk: half the time
  3. Mostly men helped
  4. Bigger groups no slower

Cane: almost always

Why
Low cost, deserving victim.

Drunk: half the time

Why
Higher cost: possible mess or danger.

Mostly men helped

Use it
Predict men will step in first.

Bigger groups no slower

Explanation
A cost-reward model of helping.

The method

  1. Learn the numbers

    Trials: 65 cane, 38 drunk. Model at 70 or 150 s. Helpers 90% male.

  2. Pick the finding

    Choose the result that fits the person in the question.

  3. Apply it

    Predict what will happen in the scenario.

  4. Evaluate

    A strength or weakness, linked to the scenario.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Cane vs drunk

    Cane got more help.

  • ✗
    Diffusion

    It was not found here.

  • ✗
    Wrong study

    Latané and Darley were lab studies; Piliavin was a field one.

Piliavin et al. (1969): helping on the New York subway · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 25 · part (whole) · 2 marks

25 Justin is investigating how people respond in the presence of others in crowded situations. He recruits confederates to stand in a queue in a crowded shopping centre, with one real participant in the queue. One of the confederates steals the purse from another confederate’s backpack, in full view of the real participant, and then walks off with the purse. The real participant can see that all of the confederates have seen what has happened. Justin repeated the scenario with the real participant being male or female on many occasions during the course of a day. Explain what Justin is likely to find regarding the real participant’s reaction to the theft of the purse in the crowded shopping centre. You should refer to Piliavin et al. (1969) in your answer.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Spot the variable that matches Piliavin: the real participant is sometimes male, sometimes female.
  2. Recall the gender finding.
  3. Predict the result for Justin.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Piliavin et al. found that around 90% of the people who first helped the collapsed man were male, so men intervened far more readily than women. Justin should therefore observe that male real participants react to the theft (for example by challenging the thief or raising the alarm) more often than female participants do.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the accurate Piliavin gender finding and one for applying it to predict the male/female difference in Justin's study: 2 marks.
