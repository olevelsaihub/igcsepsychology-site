Genes and depression: Caspi et al. (2003)

Written

Genes can make someone vulnerable. Caspi et al. (2003) found the short 5-HTT allele raised depression risk when combined with many stressful life events.

  2. Serotonin transporter gene

Longitudinal sample

Detail
Dunedin cohort of 1 037 followed from age 3; tested at 26.

Serotonin transporter gene

Detail
Each person has two alleles, short or long.

Gene-environment interaction

Write it as
One or two short alleles plus many stressful events raised the risk.

Exam tipAll groups had similar numbers of life events. The gene changed how much stress mattered.

The method

  1. Recall or apply?

    Decide if it wants study details or a link to a person.

  2. State the finding

    Short 5-HTT allele plus many stressful events raises risk.

  3. Find both clues

    Point to family history and to the stressful events.

  4. Evaluate with a reason

    Give a strength or weakness and tie it to the person.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    "The depression gene"

    Genes do not act alone; include life events.

  • ✗
    Wrong allele

    It is the short allele that raises risk.

  • ✗
    Shared homes

    Families share environments too, so a family link is not proof of genes.

Genes and depression: Caspi et al. (2003) · June 2022 · Paper 1 · Question 15 · part (whole) · 2 marks

15 Bob has two children. One of his children has been diagnosed with cancer twice but recovered both times. His father, who suffered from depression, has recently died and Bob also lost his job in the recruitment industry. Explain how likely Bob is to develop depression. You should refer to Caspi et al. (2003) in your answer.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Recall Caspi's main finding about the short 5-HTT allele and stressful life events.
  2. Find the inherited clue (depression in Bob's father).
  3. Find the stressful events (job loss, father's death, child's illness).
  4. Combine them into a prediction.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Caspi et al. (2003) found that people who carried at least one short 5-HTT allele and went through many stressful life events had a higher risk of depression than people with two long alleles. Depression ran in Bob's family through his father, so Bob may have inherited a short allele, and he has had several stressful events (losing his job, his father dying and his child's cancer), so he is quite likely to develop depression.

How the marks are earned

One mark for an accurate statement of Caspi's finding; one for applying it to Bob's inherited risk and life events.
