Knowledge card

Why people obey - and how to stop blind obedience

Written

Obedience is following a direct order from someone with legitimate authority. The situation and your personality change how likely you are to obey.

1

What changes obedience?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 factors to find

Find the versions that lowered and raised obedience.

  1. The baseline
  2. Distant authority
  3. Disobedient role models
  4. Less responsibility

The baseline

Finding
Milgram (1963): 65% of 40 men went to 450 volts.

Distant authority

Why
With the authority far away, people obey less.

Disobedient role models

Why
Seeing others refuse makes refusing easier.

Less responsibility

Why
Not pressing the switch felt less responsible.

Exam tipMake people feel responsible to cut obedience.

0 of 4 found

3 factors to find

Find who is more and less likely to obey.

  1. Uniform
  2. Authoritarian personality
  3. Internal locus of control

Uniform

Evidence
Bickman (1974): people obeyed a guard's uniform more.

Authoritarian personality

Evidence
Adorno et al. (1950): respects authority, so obeys.

Internal locus of control

Effect
Feels responsible for her actions, so resists.

The method

  1. Find the authority

    Who gives the order, and what is it?

  2. Name the factor

    One factor or one way to prevent obedience.

  3. Back it up

    A study and its finding, or a theory.

  4. Link to the people

    Use their names from the question.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Conformity mix-up

    Obedience follows an order; conformity follows a group.

  • ✗
    Same idea twice

    "Rebel" and "disobey" are one point.

  • ✗
    Wrong figures

    65% original, 10% with peers refusing.

21 A football team is in the final match of its season and is trying to win a trophy. The team is not as good as the other team it is playing. The manager of the football team asks his players to kick and hurt the opposition players during the match. He tells them to shout insults at them and push them over in an effort to win the match. Explain two ways to prevent blind obedience to the football manager. Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Spot the authority figure: the football manager ordering players to hurt opponents.
  2. Choose two different prevention methods: disobedient peers and making players personally responsible.
  3. Back each with a Milgram variation and its result.
  4. Link each back to the players and the match.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. Some players could openly refuse to foul the other team, giving the rest disobedient role models. In Milgram's (1974) variation where two fellow teachers refused to go on, only 10% of participants continued to the maximum shock, compared with 65% in the original, so seeing teammates refuse should make the others far less likely to obey the manager. 2. The players could be reminded by the referee or captain that they alone will be punished (sent off or banned) for any fouls, so they cannot blame the manager. Milgram found obedience rose to 92.5% when participants only had to tell someone else to press the switch, showing that feeling less responsible increases obedience; making players feel fully responsible should therefore reduce their blind obedience.

How the marks are earned

Each numbered point earns one mark for the prevention method and one mark for the linked Milgram evidence explaining why it would work: 4 marks.
