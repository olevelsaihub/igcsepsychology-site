Knowledge card

Crowds, deindividuation, pro-social and anti-social behaviour

Written

Deindividuation is losing your sense of identity in a group or through anonymity. Crowds can be pro-social or anti-social, depending on their norms and leaders.

Crowds, deindividuation, pro-social and anti-social behaviour · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 24 · part (whole) · 2 marks

24 A crowd of fans who support a football club has gathered outside the stadium. The fans are protesting at what they feel is an unfair situation about the ownership of their football club. The organiser of the crowd is using a loudspeaker to encourage everyone to protest calmly and frequently reminds members of the crowd they are responsible for their own actions. Most of the members of the crowd are silent and are waving signs with messages on them. Explain one reason why the crowd is acting peacefully.

Worked explanation

  1. Spot the organiser with a loudspeaker telling people to stay calm and be responsible.
  2. Choose the factor: a leader/authority figure and personal accountability reduce anti-social crowd behaviour.
  3. Link to the silent fans waving signs.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Crowds tend to follow the lead of someone they see as in charge, and when individuals are reminded they are personally accountable they are less likely to become deindividuated. The organiser is acting as an authority figure, using the loudspeaker to tell the fans to protest calmly and that they are responsible for their own actions, so the fans keep their individual identity and follow his lead by protesting quietly with signs.

How the marks are earned

One mark for understanding a factor affecting crowd behaviour, one for applying it to the organiser and fans: 2 marks.
