Eysenck's criminal personality · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 13 · part (whole) · 9 marks

*13 Bailey was arrested for stealing a car and driving it at high speed on a motorway. He showed no remorse for his behaviour and became aggressive when the police arrested him, saying they were spoiling his fun. Bailey has already been in prison for burglary and selling drugs. Bailey’s solicitor asked why he had stolen the car. Bailey said he had just been bored and wanted a laugh with his friends. The solicitor told Bailey that he may face a custodial sentence for his actions. Bailey said he did not care and that it would give him extra time to see his dad, who was already in prison for assault. Assess how well personality types (Eysenck, 1964) can explain Bailey’s behaviour. (9)

Worked explanation

  1. AO1: outline E, N and P and the high-PEN criminal profile.
  2. AO2: boredom and wanting a laugh = extraversion; no remorse and aggression = psychoticism; repeated offending despite prison = poor conditioning.
  3. AO3: research support versus dad as a role model; questionnaires and self-report.
  4. Judgement.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Eysenck (1964) argued that personality is mostly inherited and can be described on three dimensions. Extraverts are outgoing and impulsive and crave excitement because their nervous system is under-aroused; neurotic people are anxious and emotionally unstable; and people high in psychoticism are cold, hostile and uncaring. He claimed that criminals tend to score highly on all three, and that extraverts are harder to condition, so punishment teaches them less.

Bailey fits this pattern. He stole a car and drove at high speed because he was bored and wanted a laugh, which suggests the thrill-seeking of a high extravert. He showed no remorse and turned aggressive with the police, which matches the coldness and hostility of high psychoticism. He has already been to prison for burglary and drug dealing yet keeps offending, which fits Eysenck's idea that extraverts do not learn well from punishment.

There is evidence for the theory, as studies comparing young offenders with non-offenders have found the offenders score higher on psychoticism, extraversion and neuroticism. However, Bailey's dad is serving a sentence for assault, so Bailey may have watched and copied his father's behaviour, which social learning theory would explain without any inherited personality. The theory also relies on personality questionnaires, and offenders may not answer honestly. Not everyone with a high-PEN profile commits crime, so personality cannot be the whole cause.

Overall, Eysenck explains Bailey's thrill-seeking, lack of remorse and failure to respond to prison quite well, but because his father may have acted as a criminal role model, a mix of inherited personality and learning gives a fuller explanation.

How the marks are earned

Level 3: accurate PEN knowledge (AO1), each dimension tied to Bailey's actions (AO2), research support and SLT alternative leading to a balanced judgement (AO3).
