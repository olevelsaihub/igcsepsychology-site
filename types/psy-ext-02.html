Issues and debates essays (9 marks) · June 2025 · Paper 1 · Question 22 · part (whole) · 9 marks

*22 Depression is a common mental health issue. Researchers have identified that both nature and nurture factors are involved in how it is caused. As an individual, the symptoms and treatment of depression can affect the ability to cope, from completing basic day-to-day activities to employment. It can also put a strain on family relationships. In wider society, depression can have an impact on many different areas including employment, health services, emergency services and public funding. Assess the importance of whether depression is caused by nature or nurture factors. (9)

Worked explanation

  1. Define nature and nurture causes of depression (AO1).
  2. Apply to the individual and society points in the stem (AO2).
  3. Evaluate with drug therapy, CBT and Caspi (AO3).
  4. Judge which matters and why the question is important.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Nature explanations say depression comes from inside the person, for example inherited genes or low levels of the neurotransmitter serotonin. Nurture explanations say it comes from the environment, such as stressful life events or learned negative thinking; Beck's cognitive theory argues that a negative view of the self, the world and the future keeps people depressed. A combined (diathesis-stress) view says a genetic vulnerability only leads to depression when it is triggered by stress.

This debate matters for individuals because, as the stem says, depression can stop people coping with everyday tasks and work and strains families. If depression is seen as biological, the person may feel less blamed and less stigmatised. For society, depression costs a great deal through lost work, health care and benefits, so knowing the causes helps decide where public money goes: on drug treatment, on therapy, or on preventing stressful conditions such as unemployment.

Evidence supports nature: antidepressants such as SSRIs, which raise serotonin levels, reduce symptoms for many patients more than placebos do, which suggests a biological cause. However, drugs do not work for everyone and symptoms can return when they stop, so biology cannot be the whole story. Evidence also supports nurture: cognitive behavioural therapy, which challenges irrational thoughts, is effective for many depressed people, suggesting learned thinking patterns matter. The strongest evidence is Caspi et al. (2003), who followed a large New Zealand sample and found that people carrying a short allele of the serotonin transporter (5-HTT) gene became depressed more often only if they had experienced several stressful life events, showing genes and environment working together.

Overall, the evidence suggests depression is caused by an interaction of nature and nurture rather than either alone. Understanding this is important because it supports combining treatments (drugs and CBT) and reducing life stress, which could help individuals recover and reduce the large cost to society.

How the marks are earned

Level 3: AO1 defines nature, nurture and the interaction; AO2 applies to individual coping, stigma and the societal cost from the stem; AO3 evaluates with SSRIs, CBT and Caspi, giving balance and a supported conclusion. Both nature and nurture are considered, so no cap applies.
