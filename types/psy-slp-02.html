Bodily rhythms: circadian and ultradian

Written

Circadian rhythms last about 24 hours, like the sleep-wake cycle. Ultradian rhythms repeat more than once a day, like the 90-minute sleep cycle.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Circadian vs ultradian

    Circadian is about 24 hours; ultradian is shorter.

  • ✗
    Same point twice

    "Tired" twice is one way; trough and daylight are two.

  • ✗
    No application

    Mention the worker or traveller every time.

Bodily rhythms: circadian and ultradian · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Question 32 · part (whole) · 4 marks

32 Deandre is a nurse at a local hospital. He has started working a week of day shifts followed by a week of night shifts but is having issues adjusting to his new work schedule. Deandre feels really tired during his first night shift and struggles to remain alert throughout the night. When Deandre gets home after his night shift, he tries to fall asleep but wakes up every 30 minutes. Deandre only feels tired when he is due to go back to work that night. Deandre worries that his lack of sleep will affect his performance at work. Explain two ways circadian rhythms can account for the issues Deandre is having when working night shifts. 1 ................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................ 2 ................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................

Worked explanation

  1. Way 1: working through the circadian trough, when alertness is naturally lowest.
  2. Way 2: sleeping in daylight conflicts with the light cue that resets the body clock.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 Deandre's body clock is still on a daytime pattern, so during his night shift he is awake through the circadian trough in the early hours, when body temperature and alertness naturally drop to their lowest. This is why he feels so tired and struggles to stay alert at work during the night. 2 When he gets home after the night shift it is daylight, and light is the main external cue that tells the circadian rhythm it is time to be awake. Because his sleep-wake cycle is still matched to light, his body signals wakefulness during the day, so he keeps waking every 30 minutes and only feels sleepy again in the evening when the cycle swings back towards sleep.

How the marks are earned

Each way: 1 mark for the circadian point identified, 1 for linking it to Deandre's tiredness or broken sleep.
