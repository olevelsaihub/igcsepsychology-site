Knowledge card

Conformity to majority influence

Written

Conformity is changing what you do or think to match the majority. A unanimous group, a hard task and an external locus of control all raise it.

The method

  1. What form?

    A definition, a factor or a conclusion from data?

  2. Name the factor

    Unanimity, group size, task difficulty or locus of control.

  3. Say how

    Does it raise or lower conformity, and why?

  4. Use the figures

    Compare two conditions with numbers.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Obedience mix-up

    No order is given in conformity.

  • ✗
    Single number

    Compare two conditions.

  • ✗
    Describing the study

    Give the factor, not the procedure.

Conformity to majority influence · June 2025 · Paper 1 · Question 19 · part (whole) · 4 marks

19 Callum wanted to investigate conformity to majority influence in his students. He gave his students two quizzes. One was an easy general knowledge quiz and the other was a hard general knowledge quiz. To test for conformity, Callum used two classes of students: • Class one: 85% of the students were confederates / stooges, who were told by Callum to give the same wrong answer to all the questions in the two quizzes. Class two: 55% of the students were confederates / stooges, who were told to • give the same wrong answer to most of the questions in the two quizzes, but sometimes a different wrong answer. Callum recorded the number of times the other students in the two classes gave the same wrong answer as the confederates / stooges. Callum’s findings are shown in Table 3. Worked explanation

  1. Compare class one with class two to see the effect of majority size and unanimity.
  2. Compare easy with hard quiz to see the effect of task difficulty.
  3. Write each conclusion as a conformity factor, then quote the difference in percentages.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. A larger, unanimous majority produced more conformity. On the hard quiz, 50% of the real students in class one (85% stooges, always the same wrong answer) copied the wrong answer, compared with 30% in class two (55% stooges, not always consistent), a difference of 20 percentage points. 2. Conformity was greater when the task was harder. In class one, copying the wrong answer rose from 15% on the easy quiz to 50% on the hard quiz, an increase of 35 percentage points, suggesting students looked to the majority when they were unsure.

How the marks are earned

Each conclusion earns one mark for the conformity pattern and one mark for the supporting comparison from Table 3: 4 marks.
