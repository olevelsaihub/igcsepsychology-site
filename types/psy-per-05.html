Gregory's constructivist theory of perception

Written

Gregory's constructivist theory says perception is top-down: the brain uses prior knowledge to form a perceptual hypothesis, a best guess about unclear input.

Worked explanation

  1. Strength point: theory explains the flip as two equally likely hypotheses.
  2. Justify: the brain can hold only one hypothesis at a time, so it switches.
  3. Weakness point: the figure is an artificial 2D drawing.
  4. Justify: Gibson argues real scenes are rich enough that hypothesis testing is unnecessary.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Strength: The theory explains why Rhonda keeps switching between the rabbit and the duck, because both are equally sensible readings of the drawing based on her past experience of both animals. Gregory (1970) says the brain forms perceptual hypotheses about sensory input, and when two hypotheses fit equally well it cannot choose, so perception flips from one to the other and only one is seen at a time. Weakness: The rabbit-duck picture is an artificial, flat drawing with very little context, so it may not show how perception works normally. Gibson (1966) argued that in the real world the light reaching our eyes contains plenty of information, so we would not need to test hypotheses; if Rhonda saw a real animal, her perception would stay fixed on one interpretation.

How the marks are earned

1 mark each for identifying the strength and the weakness; 1 mark each for justifying them (hypotheses and flipping; Gibson's rich-information argument).
