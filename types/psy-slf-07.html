Knowledge card

Measuring personality: traits, scales and the 16PF

Written

Personality is measured with scales (a score on each trait) or types (categories). Allport ranked traits in three levels; Cattell’s 16PF scores 16 factors.

Measuring personality: traits, scales and the 16PF · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 18 · part (whole) · 2 marks

18 Patrick was in a performance review at work with his boss. His boss commented that he was tense, too sensitive and impersonal to others. Patrick did not agree with his boss’s comments, so he used Cattell’s (1946) 16PF personality factor assessment to measure his personality. The results suggested Patrick was relaxed, tough and caring. Explain one reason why using a personality factor assessment may have measured Patrick’s personality incorrectly. You must use Cattell (1946) to justify your answer.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Reason: Patrick disagreed with his boss, so may have answered to look relaxed, tough and caring.
  2. Justify: the 16PF relies on self-report answers placed on factor scales (e.g. warmth), so dishonest answers shift his scores.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Patrick may have answered in a socially desirable way, choosing responses that made him look relaxed, tough and caring because he wanted to prove his boss wrong. Cattell's 16PF is a self-report questionnaire that places a person on scales for each factor, such as from reserved to warm, so if Patrick's answers were not honest his scores on those scales would be inaccurate.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the reason (socially desirable/false answers); one for justifying through the 16PF's self-report factor scales.
