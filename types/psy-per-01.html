4

The method

  1. Spot the cue

    Match the clue in the picture to one cue.

  2. Define it

    Say in one sentence how the cue signals distance.

  3. Apply it

    Name the objects: which looks nearer, which further.

  4. Join with "so"

    Link the cue to what the person perceives.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Definition only

    Without the objects in the scenario, the second mark is lost.

  • ✗
    Size mix-up

    Relative size is a depth cue; size constancy is different.

  • ✗
    Two eyes

    Stereopsis needs both eyes, so it is not monocular.

23 Jimmy was shown a painting of people wearing formal clothes, walking along a street with square stones. The painting is shown in Figure 2. Jimmy knows that the people in the painting are closer than the building. Explain, using texture gradient, why Jimmy knows that the people in the painting are closer than the building.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Define texture gradient: detail fades as distance grows.
  2. Spot the clues: people and stones are crisp; the building and path are blurred and smooth.
  3. Conclude: the crisp items must be nearer.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Texture gradient is a one-eye cue to depth: surfaces close to us show clear, coarse detail, while surfaces far away look finer and less distinct. Jimmy can see the details of the people's clothes and the separate square stones, but the building is blurred and the path near it looks smooth, so he perceives the people as nearer and the building as further away.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1 for explaining that less detail signals more distance; mark 2 for applying that to the clear people and stones versus the blurred building and smooth path.
