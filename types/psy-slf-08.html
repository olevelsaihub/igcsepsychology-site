Van Houtte & Jarvis (1995): pets and children · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Question 19 · part (whole) · 9 marks

*19 Sammie is 10 years old and in the fifth grade at school. Her mother, Joanna, notices that she has few friends at school and always says that the girls in her class are more intelligent than her. Sammie does not like being away from her mother for long. Joanna is worried because Sammie is starting a new school in September. She does not think Sammie will cope. Joanna reads an article about the benefits of owning a pet, so buys Sammie a rabbit. After a few months looking after her rabbit, Sammie seems more self-confident. She recently went to play at a friend’s house for the very first time. Assess how well Van Houtte and Jarvis (1995) can explain Sammie’s behaviour. (9)

Worked explanation

  1. AO1: sample, cat/dog definition, autonomy and fifth/sixth-grade self-esteem findings.
  2. AO2: Sammie is 10 (fifth grade), more confident and independent after the rabbit.
  3. AO3: rabbit not counted as a pet in the study; self-report and cause; pet-therapy support.
  4. Judgement.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Van Houtte and Jarvis (1995) compared 130 American children who did or did not own pets, using questionnaires on self-esteem, autonomy and life satisfaction. Only children with a cat or dog counted as pet owners. Pet owners showed higher autonomy than non-owners, and among fifth- and sixth-graders pet owners also had higher self-esteem.

Sammie is ten and in fifth grade, the age group in which pets were linked to higher self-esteem. Before the rabbit she compared herself badly with other girls and had few friends, suggesting low self-esteem, but after caring for it she seems more self-confident. She also did not like being away from her mother, yet she has now visited a friend's house alone for the first time, which shows greater autonomy, as the study found.

However, Sammie's pet is a rabbit, and in the study only cats and dogs counted, so under its definition she would be a non-owner, meaning the findings cannot strictly be applied to her. The study also measured autonomy through how children viewed their parents rather than real independence, and it relied on self-report, so it cannot show that a pet caused the change. Sammie may simply have grown more confident with age or support from her mother. On the other hand, animals are used successfully in therapy to boost self-esteem in children and adults, which supports the idea that caring for a pet helped Sammie.

Overall, the study explains the rise in Sammie's confidence and independence fairly well, but because a rabbit falls outside its definition of a pet and it cannot prove cause and effect, it only partly explains her behaviour.

How the marks are earned

Level 3: accurate findings (AO1), sustained links to Sammie's age, confidence and independence (AO2), evaluation of pet definition, autonomy measure, causation and therapy support with a judgement (AO3).
