Knowledge card

Operant conditioning: rewards and punishments that shape crime

Written

Operant conditioning says behaviour is shaped by its consequences: reinforcement makes crime more likely to be repeated, punishment makes it less likely.

Find the consequences that make Jay more likely to steal again.

  1. Positive reinforcement
  2. Negative reinforcement
  3. Positive punishment
  4. Negative punishment

Positive reinforcement

Why
Something pleasant (praise) is added.
Effect
Stealing is repeated.

Negative reinforcement

Why
Something unpleasant (hunger) is removed.
Effect
Stealing is still repeated.

Exam tipNegative reinforcement is not a punishment: it still makes the behaviour more likely.

Positive punishment

Why
Something unpleasant (a fine) is added.
Effect
Stealing becomes less likely.

Negative punishment

Why
Something he likes (his bike) is taken away.
Effect
Stealing becomes less likely.

  1. Primary reinforcer

Primary reinforcer

Why
Meets a biological need directly.

Secondary reinforcer

Why
No value itself, but can be swapped for primary rewards.

4

The method

  1. Added or removed?

    Positive means added; negative means removed.

  2. Pleasant or unpleasant?

    Decide what was added or removed.

  3. Define the term

    One exact sentence.

  4. Apply it

    Use a detail about the named person.

  5. Say the effect

    Will the behaviour be repeated or stop?

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Positive = good

    Positive only means something is added.

  • ✗
    Token as primary

    Tokens and money are secondary reinforcers.

  • ✗
    No person

    A correct definition alone misses the application mark.

A month after the course, she was caught driving over the speed limit again when she was nearly involved in a road traffic accident. Explain how negative punishment could be used to stop Tamara from driving over the speed limit in the future.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Negative punishment = taking away something the person likes.
  2. What does Tamara value here? Being able to drive.
  3. Apply: remove her right to drive for a while.
  4. Say the effect: without driving she cannot speed, and losing something she wants makes speeding less likely later.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Negative punishment means removing something a person wants after they behave badly, so the behaviour becomes less likely. Tamara could have her driving licence taken away for several months, so she loses the freedom to drive that she values and is less likely to speed again in the future.

How the marks are earned

One mark for correctly defining negative punishment as removing something desired; one mark for applying it to Tamara by taking away her licence or ability to drive.
