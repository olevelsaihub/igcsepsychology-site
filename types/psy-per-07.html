Haber and Levin (2001): judging size and distance · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 25 · part (whole) · 2 marks

25 Colin was shopping for new curtains for his living room window. He found some curtains that he liked, but there were lots of different sizes to select from. Colin estimated how big the window was and bought curtains he believed would fit. When he returned home, the window was larger than he thought, so the curtains were too small. Explain one reason why Colin found it difficult to estimate the correct size of the window. You must use Haber and Levin (2001) to justify your answer.

Worked explanation

  1. Notice windows come in many sizes.
  2. Classify as token variant.
  3. Use the finding that size estimates were least accurate for such objects.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Windows are not a fixed size, so Colin had no reliable remembered size to compare with and underestimated how big his window was. In Haber and Levin's (2001) study, people judged the size of token-variant objects (familiar things that come in many sizes) much less accurately than objects that always have the same size, because memory of the category does not tell them the actual size.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1 for the reason (windows vary in size so Colin misjudged); mark 2 for justifying with Haber and Levin's token-variance finding.
