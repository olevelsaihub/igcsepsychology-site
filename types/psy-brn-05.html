Visual agnosia, prosopagnosia and pre-frontal damage

Written

Visual agnosia: eyesight works but objects are not recognised. Prosopagnosia: familiar faces are not recognised. Pre-frontal damage brings impulsive, poor decisions.

The method

  1. Faces or objects?

    Look at what the person can and cannot recognise.

  2. Define it

    Name the condition and say what it means.

  3. Link the evidence

    Use the test results in the scenario.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    "Blind"

    In agnosia the eyes work; recognition fails.

  • ✗
    Swapping names

    Prosopagnosia is faces; agnosia is objects.

  • ✗
    Vague damage

    Say impulsive, poor decisions, not "brain stops".

Worked explanation

  1. Patient could not name objects but did recognise the face.
  2. Objects not recognised = visual agnosia.
  3. Define and apply.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The results suggest the patient has visual agnosia, where someone's eyesight works but they cannot recognise what they are looking at. The patient could not name everyday objects like the keys or the games console, but did recognise the familiar person in the photograph, so the problem is with recognising objects rather than faces.

How the marks are earned

One mark for an accurate definition of visual agnosia; one for linking it to the patient's results.
