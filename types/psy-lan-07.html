The method

  1. Who is from where?

    Spot the Eastern and the Western person.

  2. Which cue?

    Eyes for Japanese, mouth for American, and what each concludes.

  3. Justify

    State the Yuki finding accurately.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Reversed finding

    Americans: mouth. Japanese: eyes.

  • ✗
    Study, no result

    Always give the eyes/mouth result.

  • ✗
    No link

    Name the people in the question.

Yuki et al. (2007): eyes or mouth? · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Question 38 · part (whole) · 2 marks

38 Callum and Emma spend the evening with their friend, Itsuki. Itsuki recently arrived back at university in England after spending the summer at home in Japan. Callum noticed that Emma did not smile very much during the evening. He wondered whether she was upset. Itsuki tells Callum not to worry, as Emma seemed happy to him. Explain one reason why Itsuki and Callum may have had different opinions about whether Emma was happy during their evening together. You must use Yuki et al. (2007) to justify your answer.

Worked explanation

  1. Callum (England) reads the mouth; Emma did not smile.
  2. Itsuki (Japan) reads the eyes; her eyes may have looked happy.
  3. Justify with Yuki.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Callum probably judged Emma by her mouth, and because she was not smiling he thought she was upset, whereas Itsuki looked at her eyes, which may have looked happy. The study by Yuki et al. (2007) showed American students relied mainly on the mouth to judge emotion while Japanese participants relied more on the eyes, so the two men noticed different cues.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the reason (different cues), one for the Yuki justification.
