Observations and case studies · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 6 · part (whole) · 12 marks

*6 Rosenhan (1973) studied the way in which patients were treated in mental health hospitals. Eight confederate observers were able to get themselves admitted to different psychiatric wards in different hospitals using fake symptoms of mental health. They then conducted participant observations on the hospital wards. The confederate observers took part in the daily activities on the ward to fit in with the actual patient routines. They also watched how the staff interacted with patients, making detailed notes in diaries of the events they observed. These notes included examples about patient behaviours that had been misinterpreted by the staff. They also recorded how little time the nurses and doctors spent on the ward interacting with patients. None of the staff or patients on the wards were aware that the observations were taking place. When the confederate observers were taking notes and writing in their diaries in public, their behaviour was interpreted as part of their mental health condition. Only the other patients on the ward showed any suspicion about the notes being taken by the confederate observers. Evaluate the use of an observation to investigate human behaviour. (12)

Worked explanation

  1. AO1: participant and covert observation; qualitative records.
  2. AO2: apply to Rosenhan's confederates and diaries.
  3. AO3 strengths: genuine behaviour, rich detail.
  4. AO3 weaknesses: consent and withdrawal, observer bias, replication, overt alternative.
  5. Conclusion.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

An observation is a method in which researchers watch and record behaviour. In a participant observation the observer joins the group being studied, and in a covert observation the people being watched do not know they are being studied. Observations often produce detailed qualitative records of behaviour as it naturally happens. In Rosenhan's study, eight confederates got themselves admitted to psychiatric wards by faking symptoms, joined in the ward routine as patients, and kept diaries of how staff treated patients, without staff or patients knowing.

A strength is that behaviour is seen in its real setting. Because the staff did not know they were being watched, they behaved as they normally would, so the observers recorded genuine behaviour, such as how little time doctors and nurses spent with patients. This gives high validity. The diaries also gave rich, detailed qualitative data, including examples of normal behaviour, like note-taking, being treated as a symptom, which a questionnaire could not capture.

However, covert observation raises serious ethical problems. Staff and patients did not give informed consent and could not use their right to withdraw because they did not know the study was happening, and the hospitals were deceived. Observations are also open to observer bias: the confederates knew the aim, so they may have noticed and written down the incidents that fitted it. Because each observer saw different events in different hospitals, the observation cannot be repeated exactly, which makes reliability hard to check. If an observation is overt instead, people may change their behaviour, which would lower validity.

Overall, observations such as Rosenhan's are valuable because they show how people really behave in real settings, which can reveal problems other methods would miss. However, the ethical cost of covert observation and the risk of observer bias mean the findings should be treated with care and, where possible, checked by more than one observer.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked; knowledge, application to Rosenhan and balanced evaluation with conclusion are all sustained.

Observations and case studies · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Question 7 · part (whole) · 12 marks

7 Ainsworth et al. (1978) investigated the emotional bond of attachment. They * conducted structured observations of infant responses to their mother and a stranger. A room was designed for the infant and mother to use, which contained toys for the infant to play with during the observation. Each mother was aware that they were taking part in an eight-phase standardised procedure during the observation. The behaviour of the infant was written down during each of these eight phases without the infant knowing. An example of the type of behaviour recorded was whether the infant was distressed when the mother left the infant alone with a stranger. Evaluate the use of the observation research method to investigate human behaviour. (12)

Observations and case studies · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (whole) · 12 marks

*5 H.M. underwent an experimental brain operation in 1953 where doctors removed most of his hippocampus and amygdala. It became clear that as a result of the operation H.M. had an impaired long-term memory, but his short-term memory and some other skills were largely intact. H.M. was studied from the day of his operation until he died in 2008. He took part in experimental tasks, some of these he repeated regularly. H.M. was observed by multiple researchers over his lifetime, and his amnesia was compared to that of other amnesiac patients to see if memory loss was similar. His case provided psychologists with a range of information about memory processes that may otherwise not have been understood. Evaluate the use of case studies to investigate human behaviour. (12)
