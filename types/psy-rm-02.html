Knowledge card

Extraneous variables and how to control them

Written

An extraneous variable is anything other than the IV that could change the DV. Participant variables come from the people; situational variables come from the setting.

1

What else could change the results?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 problems to find

Find three things, apart from the IV, that could change the scores.

  1. Time of day differs
  2. Noise differs
  3. Instructions differ

Time of day differs

Type
Situational variable.
Fix
Test both groups at the same time.

Noise differs

Type
Situational variable.
Fix
Use the same quiet room for all.

Instructions differ

Fix
A standardised procedure: same script for all.

Exam tipMood, age or sleep would be participant variables: about the people.

3 problems to find

What problem does each control stop?

  1. Stops guessing the aim
  2. Stops researcher bias
  3. Stops order effects

Stops guessing the aim

Why
People cannot change behaviour to fit the aim.

Stops researcher bias

Why
The researcher cannot give hints, even by accident.

Stops order effects

Use in
Repeated measures: the same people do both conditions.

4

The method

  1. Name the term

    Say in one clause what the variable or control means.

  2. Pick an example

    Choose one that fits this study, e.g. age in an obedience study.

  3. Link to the DV

    Say how it could change the results, or how the control stops that.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Giving the IV

    The IV is never an extraneous variable.

  • ✗
    People v setting

    Participant = about the people; situational = about the setting.

  • ✗
    Wrong design

    Different people in each group is independent measures, not repeated.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain the term: a feature of the people that could affect the DV.
  2. Choose one that fits obedience: gender or age, or personality.
  3. Explain how it could affect the number who obeyed.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Participant variables are personal characteristics of the people in the study, apart from the IV, that might change the results. Thomas might have thought about the age of his participants, because older people might be more used to following orders from someone in uniform and so obey more, whatever condition they were in.

How the marks are earned

One mark for showing what a participant variable is; one for applying a sensible example to Thomas' obedience study.
