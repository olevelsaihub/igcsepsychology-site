Tables, graphs and distributions

Written

Pick the right graph for the data, then label each axis with units and use an even scale. A distribution's tail names its skew.

1

Check the graph

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 points to find

Find what is right and what will lose marks.

  1. Right graph type
  2. Even scale
  3. Missing label
  4. Uneven scale

Right graph type

Why
Two co-variables need a scatter diagram: one point each.

Even scale

Why
Equal steps of 2 earn the scale mark.

Missing label

Fix it
Label it with units, e.g. "Memory score (out of 30)".

Uneven scale

Why
15 is missing, so the steps are not equal.

3 points to find

For each shape, find where the scores bunch or where the tail points.

  1. Symmetrical bell
  2. Tail right: positive
  3. Tail left: negative

Symmetrical bell

Why
Mean, median and mode are the same.

Tail right: positive

Example
A very hard test: most people score low.

Tail left: negative

Example
A very easy test: most people score high.

Does it score?

Name the type of graph that should be used to show the relationship between hours of sleep and memory score. (1 mark)

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

4

The method

  1. Choose the graph

    Categories: bar chart. Two co-variables: scatter diagram.

  2. Label the axes

    Name each variable and give its units.

  3. Use an even scale

    Equal steps that fit the biggest value.

  4. Plot accurately

    Read each value from the table, one at a time.

  5. Read the shape

    Where scores bunch and where the tail points.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    No units

    An axis label without units can lose the mark.

  • ✗
    x and y swapped

    Plot each point as (x, y) the right way round.

  • ✗
    Skew mix-up

    Name the skew by the tail, not by the bunch.

Worked explanation

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Label the x-axis 'Average time spent on homework each week (minutes)' with an even scale from 0 to 80 in steps of 10. Plot participant C at 30 minutes and a score of 15, and participant E at 55 minutes and a score of 25.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correctly plotted point and one for the x-axis label with an even scale.
