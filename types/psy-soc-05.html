The Stanford prison study (Haney, Banks and Zimbardo)

Written

Haney, Banks and Zimbardo made students into guards and prisoners. The roles and uniforms, through deindividuation, changed behaviour so fast the study stopped after six days.

The method

  1. Know the numbers

    75 volunteers, 24 chosen, two weeks planned, six days run.

  2. Recall the finding

    Roles and uniforms changed behaviour.

  3. Apply it

    How will the uniform or role change the people in the question?

  4. Weakness + why

    Give the flaw and why it limits the study.

Do not lose marks here

The Stanford prison study (Haney, Banks and Zimbardo) · June 2022 · Paper 1 · Question 26 · part (whole) · 2 marks

26 Casey is investigating the influence of wearing a uniform on participant behaviour. She goes to a local business sales office where every staff member is wearing a smart suit. Casey asks the staff to make a phone call to a customer and records them. Later that week, Casey asks the same staff members to come to the office wearing casual clothes. She asks them to make another phone call to a customer and records them. Casey listens to both sets of recordings of the sales calls from the workers. Explain what Casey is likely to find regarding the sales calls by the staff members when wearing smart suits and when wearing casual clothes. You should refer to Haney, Banks, and Zimbardo (1973) in your answer.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Recall that guards and prisoners took on their roles once given uniforms.
  2. Notice the scenario contrast: smart suits versus casual clothes.
  3. Predict how the role linked to the suit changes the calls.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

In Haney, Banks and Zimbardo's study, ordinary students given guard or prisoner uniforms quickly behaved in line with those roles (guards became bossy and aggressive, prisoners passive), showing that uniforms and roles can override individual identity. Casey should hear that in smart suits the staff sound more formal and businesslike on the phone, acting out the role of a sales professional, while in casual clothes their calls are more relaxed and chatty.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the accurate finding about roles and uniforms, one for applying it to predict the difference in Casey's recordings: 2 marks.
