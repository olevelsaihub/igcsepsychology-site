Learning theory explanation of addiction

Written

Learning theory says addiction is learned: we copy role models, behaviour that is reinforced is repeated, and cues linked with use trigger cravings.

The method

  1. Pick the mechanism

    Modelling, reinforcement or a conditioned cue: whichever fits.

  2. Use the scenario

    Name the model or reward and show how it led to use.

  3. Evaluate with reasons

    Bandura as support, biology as a limit, each tied to the person.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Positive vs negative

    Positive adds a pleasure; negative removes something unpleasant.

  • ✗
    Bare "nurture"

    Explain the learning process, not just the label.

  • ✗
    Unlinked weakness

    Say how biology might explain this person.

Worked explanation

  1. Strength: Bandura's research on imitation of role models.
  2. Link to Jeremie watching and copying his father.
  3. Weakness: biological factors are ignored.
  4. Link to a possible inherited vulnerability from his father.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Strength: Bandura et al. (1961) showed that children imitate the behaviour of adult role models, as they copied an adult hitting a Bobo doll. This supports the idea that Jeremie learned his behaviour by watching his father enjoy winning online auctions and then copying him when he was old enough. Weakness: The theory leaves out biology, such as genes or dopamine. Jeremie might have inherited a vulnerability to addiction from his father, which could explain his addiction instead of, or as well as, imitation.

One mark for each identified strength/weakness and one for each justification linked to Jeremie.
