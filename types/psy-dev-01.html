Piaget's stages of cognitive development

Written

Piaget said children's thinking moves through four stages in a fixed order. Spot the clue in what the child does, name the stage, then link it to the child.

4 stages to find

Find the clue that shows each child's stage.

  1. Sensorimotor
  2. Pre-operational
  3. Concrete operational
  4. Formal operational

Sensorimotor

Clue
Object permanence: the toy still exists when hidden.

Pre-operational

Clue
She cannot conserve yet; she goes by how it looks.

Concrete operational

Clue
Conservation is reached, from about 7.

Formal operational

Clue
Abstract, hypothetical thinking, from about 11.

3 stages to find

Find assimilation, accommodation and equilibrium.

  1. Assimilation
  2. Accommodation
  3. Equilibrium

Assimilation

Means
New information fits an existing schema.

Accommodation

Means
The schema changes to fit the new information.

Exam tipAssimilation fits in; accommodation changes. Do not swap them.

Equilibrium

Means
Balance once the new information makes sense.

4

The method

  1. Spot the clue

    Hidden toy, sweets in a row, future thinking or a wrong label.

  2. Name the stage

    Give the stage and its key feature, with the age if it helps.

  3. Link to the child

    Say what this child did or would say, using names from the question.

  4. Evaluate with a reason

    For a strength or weakness, tie the reason to the child.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong ages

    Conservation belongs to the concrete operational stage, not before.

  • ✗
    No link

    A stage with no link to the child earns only one mark.

  • ✗
    Swapping terms

    Assimilation fits in; accommodation changes the schema.

What a complete response includes

Two-mark items give one mark for an accurate Piagetian idea and one for applying it to the child; one-mark definitions need age plus key ability; four-mark evaluations give a mark for each point and a mark for each linked justification.

Worked explanation

  1. Clue: the children are asked to imagine a person 20 years from now, which is hypothetical thinking.
  2. State the feature of the formal operational stage: reasoning about abstract or possible situations.
  3. Apply: give a sensible prediction that follows logically from the facts in the stem.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

In the formal operational stage, from about 11 years old, people can think in an abstract way and reason about hypothetical situations that have not happened yet. So a child in this stage could use the clues given and predict that because the 14-year-old works hard at school and eats healthily, in 20 years they may be healthy, have good qualifications and a well-paid job.

How the marks are earned

One mark for correctly describing formal operational thinking as hypothetical or abstract; one mark for a prediction about the 14-year-old's future that uses the details in the stem.
