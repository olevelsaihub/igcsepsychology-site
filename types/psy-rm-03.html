Knowledge card

Sampling methods

Written

A sample is the people studied from the target population. Random and stratified samples represent it better; volunteer and opportunity samples are quicker but biased.

1

Name the sampling method

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 methods to find

Name the sampling method each researcher used.

  1. Opportunity sample
  2. Random sample
  3. Volunteer sample
  4. Stratified sample

Opportunity sample

Why
Whoever is there and willing at the time.

Random sample

Why
Every student had an equal chance of being picked.

Volunteer sample

Why
People chose to take part by replying.

Stratified sample

Why
Subgroups are sampled in proportion to their size.

Exam tipAsking people "at random" in a corridor is still opportunity sampling.

4

The method

  1. Define it

    Say in one clause what the method is.

  2. Apply it

    Describe the steps this researcher takes, with the study's people and place.

  3. Stay consistent

    Random must give everyone an equal chance; stratified needs proportions.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Random v opportunity

    Stopping people in the street is opportunity, not random.

  • ✗
    No application

    A definition alone misses the mark for using it.

  • ✗
    No proportions

    Stratified means each subgroup in proportion to its size.

Worked explanation

  1. Define opportunity sampling: people who happen to be available.
  2. Apply to Jenna: university students around her at the time.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Opportunity sampling means using people who are available and willing at the time the research is done. Jenna could go to her university library one afternoon and ask the first ten students she sees whether they would take part in her memory study.

How the marks are earned

One mark for knowing the method, one for a realistic application to Jenna's university.
