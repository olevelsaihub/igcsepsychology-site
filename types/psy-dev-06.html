Stages of moral development

Written

Morality is our sense of right and wrong. Children move from judging by punishment, to laws and approval, to their own principles.

The method

  1. Find the stage

    See which stage or stages the question names.

  2. State the basis

    Punishment, laws and approval, or own principles.

  3. Link or contrast

    Judge the dilemma, or use 'whereas' for a difference.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    One side only

    A difference needs both stages described.

  • ✗
    Good reason

    Accepting theft for a good reason is post-conventional.

  • ✗
    Pre vs conventional

    Punishment is pre-conventional; laws are conventional.

Stages of moral development · June 2023 · Paper 1 · Question 3 · part (whole) · 2 marks

3 Javier is trying to find out if his son is in the conventional stage of morality. He tells his son about a woman who is trying to steal food for her mother, because they cannot afford to pay for it. Javier asks his son if the woman is right to steal the food or not. Explain what Javier’s son would say about the woman’s actions if he is in the conventional stage of morality. You should refer to the conventional stage of morality in your answer.

Hint for part (whole)

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Conventional stage: judgements are based on laws, rules and keeping society orderly.
  2. So the son will judge stealing as wrong whatever the reason.
  3. Give the reason in his own words.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

In the conventional stage, people judge right and wrong by following laws and authority so that society stays orderly, and they want others to see them as a good citizen. So Javier's son would say the woman was wrong to steal the food, even to help her mother, because stealing breaks the law and if everyone did it there would be no order in society.

How the marks are earned

One mark for describing conventional morality as following laws and social order; one mark for predicting that the son says the theft is wrong because it breaks the rules.
