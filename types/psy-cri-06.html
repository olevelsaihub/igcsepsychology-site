Charlton et al. (2000): TV comes to St Helena

Written

When TV reached St Helena, Charlton et al. (2000) found children’s behaviour barely changed. A close, watchful community may have protected them.

Natural experiment

Why
TV arrival was not set up by the researchers.

No TV effect

Write it as
Antisocial behaviour did not rise after TV.

Exam tipDo not say TV made them more aggressive.

Boys vs girls

Why it matters
This gap was not caused by TV.

Community

Why it matters
Watchful adults kept children in check.

The method

  1. Pick the finding

    Community, gender, no TV effect, or procedure.

  2. Point to the detail

    Village life, or being a boy.

  3. Justify

    Back it with the matching Charlton finding.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    TV caused aggression

    Charlton found little change.

  • ✗
    Gender from TV

    Boys were more antisocial, but not because of TV.

  • ✗
    Bandura mix-up

    Charlton was a natural study, not a lab.

Simone and her friend, Paul, enjoy going to watch action films. Their parents are concerned that any violence in the films will affect their behaviour. Simone’s parents do not notice any changes in her behaviour. Paul, however, has recently been in trouble at college, for the first time. Explain two ways that Charlton et al. (2000) could account for the behaviour of Simone and Paul. 1 ................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................ 2 ................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Way 1: Simone lives where everyone knows each other, so she is watched; justify with the community supervision finding.
  2. Way 2: Paul is a boy; justify with the finding that boys showed far more antisocial acts, unrelated to TV.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 Simone's behaviour has not changed because in her small village her parents and neighbours always know what she is doing. Charlton et al. found that the close, watchful community on St Helena, where adults kept an eye on children, helped explain why children did not become more antisocial after TV arrived. 2 Paul's trouble at college may be because he is male rather than because of the films. Charlton et al. found that boys carried out far more antisocial acts than girls, a difference that was there regardless of television.

How the marks are earned

Two scenario points (one mark each) and two linked justifications from the study's findings (one mark each).
