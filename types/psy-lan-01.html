How language and thinking develop: Piaget vs Vygotsky

Piaget's view that thought precedes language and Vygotsky's separate systems (pre-linguistic thought, pre-intellectual language).

Type 6 10 Language, thought and communication (optional) PSY-LAN-01

★★★★★ · 7 of 14 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

A scenario about a baby or toddler babbling, naming objects, pointing or failing to name something new, with Piaget (1950) or Vygotsky (1981) named, or a 'State what is meant by' question on one of their key terms.

How language and thinking develop: Piaget vs Vygotsky

Written

Piaget: thinking comes first and language follows. Vygotsky: thought and language start as two separate systems that join at about 2-3 years.

4

The method

  1. Spot the theory

    Thought first is Piaget; separate systems is Vygotsky.

  2. Quote the child

    Use the age and what the child does from the question.

  3. Name the term

    Schema, representational thinking, pre-linguistic thought or pre-intellectual language.

  4. Two ways, two behaviours

    Use a different child or action for each way.

  5. Long answers

    Weigh it against the other theory, then give a verdict.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixing the theories

    Separate systems belong to Vygotsky, not Piaget.

  • ✗
    Behaviour, no term

    Always name the theory's term.

  • ✗
    Term, no child

    Link every term to the child in the question.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Spot that Frances (7 months) has no words but clearly wants something: thinking without language.
  2. Name it: pre-linguistic thought.
  3. Spot that Tamina (18 months) names cup and spoon while walking off, not talking to anyone.
  4. Name it: pre-intellectual language.
  5. Write each way as point (child) + justification (Vygotsky's term explained).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. Frances cannot yet speak but shows she does not want the food by closing her mouth, shaking her head and knocking the spoon away. Vygotsky (1981) calls this pre-linguistic thought: babies can think and solve problems before they have any words, because thought and language develop separately at first. 2. Tamina says 'cup' and 'spoon' as she walks away, so she is labelling things she can see rather than talking to her parents. Vygotsky (1981) calls this pre-intellectual language, where a young child uses words to name objects around them without the words carrying thought or being used to communicate.

How the marks are earned

Each numbered way gets one mark for describing what the child does and one for the linked Vygotsky concept that explains it (2 + 2).
