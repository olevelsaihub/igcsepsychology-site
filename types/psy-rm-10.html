Knowledge card

Drawing conclusions from data

Written

A conclusion says what the results show about behaviour. Back it with data: quote figures, differences or a pattern, and do not claim more than the data show.

1

What do the results show?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 clues to find

Find the evidence for a conclusion, and one limit.

  1. Every rating fell
  2. Biggest drop: 7 to 4
  3. Mean fell 6.5 to 4.5
  4. Only four people

Every rating fell

Conclusion
The ban made attitudes more negative.

Biggest drop: 7 to 4

Use it
Quote one person's figures as evidence.

Mean fell 6.5 to 4.5

Use it
Compare the two means.

Only four people

Limit
Too few to generalise widely.
0 of 4 found

2 clues to find

What conclusion fits when results are this close?

  1. Gap of only 0.2
  2. Little or no effect

Gap of only 0.2

Why
Too small to show a real difference.

Little or no effect

Write it as
Music had little effect on recall: 12.1 v 12.3.
0 of 2 found

Does it score?

Exam question

Explain one conclusion from the junk food ratings before and after the ban. (2 marks)

Decide first: would this answer earn the mark? Then tap it to check.

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

4

The method

  1. Find the pattern

    Look for the biggest or clearest difference.

  2. State the conclusion

    Say what it shows about behaviour, in the study's terms.

  3. Add figures

    Quote numbers, means or differences from the table.

  4. Two conclusions

    Use two different patterns, not one twice.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Data, no conclusion

    Numbers alone do not say what they mean.

  • ✗
    Conclusion, no data

    Always quote figures as evidence.

  • ✗
    Wrong column

    Check which row and column is which.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Compare each participant's before and after ratings.
  2. Every rating fell after the ban (by 1 to 4 points).
  3. Conclude about attitude change and quote figures.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The ban made young people's attitudes towards eating unhealthy food more negative, because every one of the ten participants gave a lower rating after the ban than before, for example Participant F dropped from 9 to 5, and the average fell from 7.3 to 4.6.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the conclusion and one for data that justify it.
