The method

  1. Pick a signal

    Choose one behaviour from the question for each way.

  2. Give its job

    Warning, threat, focusing or getting care.

  3. Link to Darwin

    Innate, inherited, helped survival.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    "Learned from parents"

    Darwin says inherited, not learned.

  • ✗
    No purpose

    Always say how the signal helps survival.

  • ✗
    Same sentence twice

    Link each justification to its own behaviour.

Darwin: why non-verbal communication evolved · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 39 · part (whole) · 4 marks

39 George and Edward were arguing about who was the best at sport. George instinctively narrowed his eyes at Edward and said he was the best sportsman. Edward creased his eyebrows, frowned at George, and then called him a liar. George unfolded his arms and bared his teeth. Edward froze, clenched his jaw, and glared at George. Both George and Edward tensed their muscles, and their breathing rate became faster. Explain two ways that Darwin’s (1872) theory of evolution can account for the non‑verbal communication during the argument. 1 ................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................ 2 ................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................

Worked explanation

  1. Choose narrowed eyes (George) and bared teeth (George).
  2. State the function of each.
  3. Justify each with Darwin's evolution idea.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. George narrowed his eyes automatically, which cuts out what is happening at the edges of his vision so he can concentrate on Edward. Darwin (1872) argued such expressions are inherited reactions rather than learned, explaining why George did it without thinking. 2. George bared his teeth, which signals a threat and shows Edward he is ready to fight. Darwin (1872) saw this as a serviceable habit: animals bare teeth before biting, and those who warned rivals off were more likely to survive and pass this on.

How the marks are earned

Each way: one mark for the behaviour and its purpose, one for the Darwin justification (2 + 2).
