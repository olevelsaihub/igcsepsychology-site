Personal space and proxemics

Written

Personal space is the gap we like to keep around us. Proxemics (Hall, 1966) shows the gap shrinks as relationships get closer.

The method

  1. Define it

    Say what personal space or proxemics is, with a rough distance.

  2. Who are they?

    Strangers, new friends or close friends?

  3. Explain the move

    Use the right zone for that relationship.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixed-up distances

    Learn the zones in order.

  • ✗
    No relationship

    Say how well the people know each other.

  • ✗
    Feelings only

    Link the discomfort to distance.

Personal space and proxemics · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 38 · part (whole) · 2 marks

38 Mark attended a welcome evening on his first day at his new university. He had never met any of the other students before and wanted to get to know the people he would be studying with. At the start of the evening, Mark felt nervous about being there and stood at quite a distance from the others. By the end of the evening, he felt comfortable standing close to several other students while they all chatted. Explain how proxemics may have influenced Mark’s behaviour.

Worked explanation

  1. Proxemics: distance depends on how close the relationship is.
  2. At first Mark is with strangers so keeps a social distance.
  3. Later they are new friends so he moves into personal distance.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Proxemics suggests that the amount of space we need from others gets smaller as we get to know them. At first the students were strangers, so Mark kept a social distance of more than about 4 feet, but once he had made friends he felt comfortable moving into personal distance and stood close to them.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the proxemics idea, one for applying it to Mark moving closer as friendships formed.
