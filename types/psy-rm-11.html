Improving a study: who and where

Suggesting a change to who or where a study samples so that findings are more representative and generalisable.

Type 16 11 Research methods PSY-RM-11

★☆☆☆☆ · 6 of 14 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question asks 'Explain one/two improvement(s)' and the study uses a narrow sample (one patient, one gender, one place, one culture).

Knowledge card

Improving a study: who and where

Written

If a study uses one person, one gender, one place or one culture, its findings may not generalise. Change who or where, then explain how that makes it more representative.

4

The method

  1. Spot the narrow part

    One person, gender, place or culture?

  2. Suggest a change

    Say exactly who or where to add.

  3. Justify it

    Explain how it makes findings more representative, in context.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    No reason

    "Use more people" needs a reason.

  • ✗
    Unlinked reason

    The reason must match the change.

  • ✗
    Same change twice

    Two improvements must be different changes.

Written-answer check

Worked explanation

  1. Sandy tested one female patient only.
  2. Improve: test several patients with Region G damage, both male and female.
  3. Justify: findings about Region G would apply more widely.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Sandy could have given the same tests to several patients with damage to Region G, including both males and females, so she could be more confident that poor colour naming is caused by damage to Region G in people generally and not just something unique to her one female patient.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the improvement and one for the linked justification about generalisability.
