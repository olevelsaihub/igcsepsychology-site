Cognitive explanation of depression

Written

Cognitive theory says depression comes from faulty, negative thinking: Beck's cognitive triad of a negative outlook on yourself, your future and the world around you.

The method

  1. State the idea

    Name the triad, a schema or a bias.

  2. Label the thoughts

    Match each of the person's thoughts to self, world or future.

  3. Evaluate with a reason

    Give a strength or weakness, then justify it for the person.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Describing CBT

    CBT is the treatment. Explain the cause.

  • ✗
    Bare "reductionist"

    Say what is left out, such as genes.

  • ✗
    Generic point

    Every justification needs the person's details.

Worked explanation

  1. Choose a strength: research evidence (Evans et al., 2005).
  2. Justify it by linking negative self-beliefs to Maddie's thoughts.
  3. Choose a weakness: biological factors ignored.
  4. Justify it with what else could explain Maddie's depression.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Strength: Research supports the theory. Evans et al. (2005) found that women holding the most negative views of themselves had a higher chance of developing depression later on, which fits Maddie, who thinks she will always be a failure and will never be promoted, so her negative thinking could have led to her depression. Weakness: The theory ignores biology, such as genes or low serotonin. Maddie does not seem to have had a major negative event to trigger her thinking, so an inherited vulnerability or a neurotransmitter imbalance might explain her depression better than her thought patterns.

How the marks are earned

One mark for identifying the strength, one for its justification; one for identifying the weakness, one for its justification.
