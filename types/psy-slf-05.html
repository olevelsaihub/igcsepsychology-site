Free will: Baumeister and Vohs & Schooler · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 20 · part (whole) · 9 marks

*20 George’s class have a mock examination in two weeks. He has done no revision for this and believes it is too late for any revision to make a difference. Instead of trying to complete some revision, George blames the teacher for not helping him enough. Suzie has completed lots of revision, and she offers to help George so that he can pass his mock examination. George is rude to Suzie and calls her a ‘teacher’s pet’. He says she will only do well because she is clever, and there is no point in revision as he does not care about the mock examination anyway. Assess how well a belief in free will (Baumeister, 2008) can explain George’s behaviour. (9)

Worked explanation

  1. AO1: Baumeister: free will = self-control and rational choice enabling prosocial action; disbelief linked to harmful behaviour.
  2. AO2: George thinks the result is fixed, blames the teacher, is rude to Suzie.
  3. AO3: Vohs & Schooler support; determinism may be realistic for George; other explanations (low self-esteem); lab-based evidence.
  4. Judgement.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Baumeister (2008) describes free will as a highly developed form of self-control. It lets people think rationally and choose actions that benefit themselves and society, even when those choices go against selfish impulses. He suggests that when people stop believing in free will they act more impulsively and are more likely to behave in ways that harm themselves or others.

George seems to lack this belief. He thinks it is already too late for revision to help, treating his result as fixed rather than something he can change. Instead of controlling his behaviour and revising, he blames the teacher, which shows he does not take responsibility for his choices. He is rude to Suzie when she offers to help, a selfish rather than social response, and claims not to care, possibly to protect himself from the upset of failing.

There is evidence to support this. In Vohs and Schooler (2008), students who read a passage claiming free will is an illusion cheated more on a maths task than those who read a neutral passage, showing that weakening belief in free will makes people less likely to behave well. Other research by Baumeister has linked disbelief in free will to people being less helpful and more aggressive, which matches George's rudeness. However, these studies took place in laboratories with students, so they may not reflect real classroom behaviour. George's behaviour could also be explained by low self-esteem or fear of failure, and he may partly be right that two weeks is not long enough, so a deterministic view might be realistic rather than just a mistaken belief.

Overall, Baumeister's ideas explain George's blaming, rudeness and refusal to revise quite well, but other factors such as his self-esteem may also be involved, so free will only partly explains his behaviour.

How the marks are earned

Level 3: accurate theory (AO1), sustained links to George's blaming, rudeness and giving up (AO2), evaluation with Vohs and Schooler, other research, lab limits and alternatives ending in a judgement (AO3).
