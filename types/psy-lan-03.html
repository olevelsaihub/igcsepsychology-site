Human language vs animal communication

Written

Aitchison (1983) listed features of language. Animals share some, like turn-taking; only humans have four, including displacement and semanticity.

The method

  1. Pick a feature

    Displacement or semanticity for a difference; turn-taking or signals for a similarity.

  2. Say who has it

    Humans, animals or both.

  3. Link to the example

    Use what the person says or the animal does.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    "Animals can't communicate"

    They can: sounds, faces and body signals.

  • ✗
    Just a label

    Explain what the feature means.

  • ✗
    Wrong kind

    Check if a similarity or a difference is asked.

Human language vs animal communication · June 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 39 · part (whole) · 2 marks

39 Harry was planning to take his dog, Betty, for a walk, but it was raining. He told Betty that they would go for a walk in the woods when the rain stopped. Harry said they could look for a big stick for her to play with, and maybe they would see some squirrels. Betty wagged her tail while Harry spoke to her. Explain one reason why Betty would not understand Harry’s plan for the walk. You must use Aitchison (1983) to justify your answer.

Worked explanation

  1. Notice Harry talks about a future walk, the rain stopping, a stick and squirrels.
  2. Recognise this is talking about things not present: displacement.
  3. Explain Betty only reacts to his voice.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Betty is only reacting to the sound of Harry's voice by wagging her tail; she cannot follow a plan about something that will happen later. Aitchison (1983) said displacement, the ability to talk about events that are not happening here and now such as future or imagined events, is a feature only humans have, so a dog cannot understand plans about a future walk.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the reason linked to Betty, one for the Aitchison justification (displacement unique to humans).
