4

The method

  1. Use the age

    The age points to the stage.

  2. State the conflict

    Name both sides, e.g. autonomy v shame and doubt.

  3. Link the behaviour

    Show how the behaviour fits the conflict.

  4. Judge if asked

    Strength: whole lifespan. Weakness: descriptive, ages approximate.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong stage

    Check the age before naming a stage.

  • ✗
    Stage, no conflict

    Always give the conflict.

  • ✗
    Generativity vs integrity

    Generativity is middle age; integrity is old age.

Kim insists on putting on her own shoes and her mother lets her do this. Kim struggles and does not always put her shoes on the correct feet. Worked explanation

  1. Kim is 2: autonomy v shame and doubt.
  2. Strength: explains the 'no' and doing her own shoes, helping her mother understand.
  3. Weakness: describes behaviour but gives no practical advice on helping her through the stage.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Strength: Erikson places a two-year-old in the autonomy versus shame and doubt stage, which explains why Kim refuses requests and insists on putting on her own shoes, as she is trying to be independent. Knowing this, her mother can see the behaviour as normal and give Kim safe chances to do things for herself. Weakness: Erikson mainly describes what children do at each age rather than explaining how to help them succeed. It tells Kim's mother that independence matters, but not how to handle the refusals or the shoe struggles, so it is of limited practical use.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for identifying the strength and weakness; one each for justifying them using Kim's behaviour.
