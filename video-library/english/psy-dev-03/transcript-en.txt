Idea. You fail a maths test. One friend thinks, I am just bad at maths. Another thinks, I can improve if I practise. Carol Dweck said these are two mindsets. A mindset is a belief about your own ability. A fixed mindset says ability is set and effort will not change it. A growth mindset says ability can grow with effort. Praise shapes mindset. Process praise praises effort and strategy, such as, you must have tried hard. It encourages a growth mindset. Person praise praises talent, such as, you are so smart. It encourages a fixed mindset. Mueller and Dweck, in nineteen ninety-eight, tested this. Children praised for being clever put in less effort and did worse after a failure than children praised for effort. Gunderson and colleagues, in twenty thirteen, filmed parents with their children at fourteen, twenty-six and thirty-eight months. Process praise early on predicted a growth mindset at seven to eight years old. Boys got more process praise than girls. Praise was only about three per cent of what parents said. A question shows a child's behaviour or some praise. You decide whether it is a fixed or growth mindset, state what that means, and link it to the person.

Method. Step one: decide whether the behaviour or praise fits a fixed or a growth mindset. Step two: state what that mindset means. Step three: apply it, by quoting what the person did, or by giving an example of praise. Use clues. Saying, I will never understand, and giving up, is fixed. Keeping going after failing is growth. Praise for effort is growth. Praise for being clever is fixed. The trap is swapping the two. To encourage growth, praise effort and strategy. Never talent.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Emily is stuck on a maths puzzle. Martha, a teaching assistant, wants to encourage a growth mindset. We explain one way Martha could praise Emily. Two marks. Step one: link growth mindset to praise. It develops when a child is praised for effort and strategies, not for being naturally clever. Step two: give words Martha could say. For example, I can see how hard you are working on this puzzle. Keep trying different ways. Step three: say why it helps. Emily learns she can improve if she keeps going, so she is more likely to carry on. A tempting wrong route is to say, well done, you are so clever. Reject it. That praises talent, so it builds a fixed mindset. Now write it out. The idea, then the example. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is swapping fixed and growth. Praise for talent leads to a fixed mindset. Praise for effort leads to a growth mindset. Check which one the question asks for.

Your turn. Now you try. Sara says there is no point reading because she will never understand the words. Later she stops digging when it gets hard. Explain what this shows about her mindset, using Dweck. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Sara shows a fixed mindset. A fixed mindset means believing ability cannot improve with effort. She says she will never understand the words, and she gives up when digging gets hard. So she avoids the challenge. The common wrong answer says Sara is lazy. That does not use Dweck's theory, so it loses the mark.
