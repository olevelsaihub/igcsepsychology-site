Idea. Why are dreams so strange? One minute you are in class, the next you are flying. A dream is the story your sleeping brain makes. Hobson and McCarley said, in nineteen seventy-seven, that dreams come from the brain's own activity, not from hidden meanings. This is Activation Synthesis Theory. Dreaming happens mostly in REM sleep, which means rapid eye movement sleep. Brain activity is high. Activation means that in REM the brainstem, at the base of the brain, fires random signals up to the cortex, the thinking layer on the outside. Synthesis means putting parts together. The cortex tries to make sense of the random signals. It weaves memories and images into a story. That is why dreams are often bizarre, which means very odd. Two more parts. Sensory blockade means the brain blocks most information from the outside world, so sounds and sights do not shape the dream. Movement inhibition means signals to the muscles are blocked, so the body is paralysed and we do not act out dreams. A question gives you a person asleep, or a strange dream, and asks for one reason. You must use the theory to justify your answer.

Method. Step one: identify the reason in the scenario. High brain activity points to random firing in REM. A strange dream points to a mix of random memories. Step two: justify with the theory. Say what Hobson and McCarley say, using the key word. The traps are giving Freud's hidden meaning, not mentioning REM sleep, and forgetting movement inhibition when muscles are in the question.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Belinda is at a sleep clinic. Electrodes record her brain waves and muscle movement. While asleep, her brain activity is high, but she hardly moves. We explain one reason, using the theory. Two marks. Step one: identify the reason. High brain waves with little movement is what REM sleep looks like. The brain fires randomly, and the muscles are still. Step two: justify with the theory. Activation: in REM the brainstem sends random signals to the cortex, which turns them into a dream. Movement inhibition: signals to the muscles are blocked, so the body stays still. A tempting wrong route is to say Belinda is dreaming about a hidden fear. That is Freud's idea, and the question demands this theory. Reject it. The exam answer. Reason, then theory. One mark for the reason in REM sleep. One mark for justifying it with the theory. Common mistake. The most common mistake is to give a reason that is not from this theory, or to leave out REM sleep. Without the theory's words, you cannot earn the justification mark.
