Idea. Your friends all change their profile picture to the same photo. So you do too. That is conformity. Conformity means changing your behaviour or opinions so they match the majority of a group. The majority is simply most of the people in the group. In nineteen fifty-one, Asch tested this. Male students judged the lengths of lines. Confederates, who are people secretly working with the researcher, gave wrong answers on purpose. About a third of the critical answers conformed. About three quarters of students conformed at least once. Some things raise conformity. A unanimous majority, where everyone agrees, raises it. One person who disagrees lowers it sharply. A harder task raises it, because people look to the group for the answer. And a bigger group helps, up to three or four people. Personality matters too. People with an external locus of control, who believe luck and others control their lives, conform more. People with an internal locus, who believe they control their own lives, conform less. So do people with high self-esteem. Questions ask for a definition, a factor, or conclusions from data.

Method. Step one: decide if you need a definition, a factor or conclusions from data. Step two: name the factor exactly, such as unanimity or task difficulty. Step three: say how it changes conformity. Step four: link it to the person, or quote numbers from the table. The traps are calling it obedience, and quoting one number instead of comparing two conditions.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Callum gave his students an easy quiz and a hard quiz. In class one, eighty-five per cent were confederates giving the same wrong answer. In class two, fifty-five per cent were. He counted how often the other students copied the wrong answer. We write two conclusions about conformity from his table. Four marks. Here is Table three. It shows the percentage of the other students who copied the wrong answer. Class one had eighty-five per cent confederates. Class two had fifty-five per cent. Conclusion one. Compare the classes on the hard quiz. Class one copied fifty per cent, class two thirty per cent. That is twenty percentage points more. So a larger, unanimous majority produced more conformity. Conclusion two. Compare quizzes in class one. Copying rose from fifteen per cent to fifty per cent. That is thirty-five percentage points more. So conformity was greater when the task was harder. A tempting wrong route is to write, class one conformed fifty per cent. That is one number, not a conclusion. Compare two figures, and name the factor. The exam answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to confuse conformity with obedience. Obedience follows an order from an authority. Conformity follows a group, with no order at all.

Your turn. Now you try. Leonora goes to see a film that her friends chose. Explain why, using a factor affecting conformity. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The factor is a unanimous majority. All of Leonora's friends wanted car racing, and nobody agreed with her. With no dissenter to support her, the group pull was strong, so she conformed and went along with them. A common wrong answer is, peer pressure. That is vague. It names no factor, so it earns no knowledge mark. Say unanimous majority, then link it to her friends. So name the factor, say how it changes conformity, and link it.
