Spotting the IV and the DV

Idea. Imagine you test whether music helps students revise. One group revises with music. Another group revises in silence. Then both groups sit the same test. You are changing one thing, and measuring another. The thing you change or compare is the independent variable, or IV. The thing you measure at the end is the dependent variable, or DV. It depends on the IV. Here, the IV is music or silence. The DV is the test score. Sometimes the researcher cannot change the IV. If a study compares boys and girls, the IV is still the group difference, gender. The researcher did not create it, but they compare it. The DV is still the measurement. Things kept the same for everyone are controls. Time allowed, the task and the room are controls, not the IV or DV. A good answer also operationalises the variable. That means it says exactly how it was changed or measured, such as number of words recalled out of twenty. So this question type gives you a study. You pick out or name the IV or the DV.

Method. Step one: find what is different between the groups or conditions. That is the IV. Step two: find what the researcher counted, timed or scored at the end. That is the DV. Step three: write it using the details of the study, or pick the option that matches. The trap is picking a control, such as time allowed, or swapping the IV and DV. Ask: did the researcher change it, or measure it, or keep it the same?

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Thomas tests obedience. Thirty people are told to stay silent by a man in a uniform. Another thirty are told by a man in jeans and a t-shirt. Thomas records how many obey. We must pick the dependent variable from four options. It is worth one mark. Step one. Find the IV. The two conditions differ in what the man wore. That is option A. It is the thing Thomas changed, so it is the IV, not the DV. Step two. Find the controls. The time is forty minutes in both. The task is the same too. So options B and D describe controls, not the DV. Step three. What did Thomas record at the end? How many participants obeyed. That is option C, the number who stayed silent. It is measured, so it is the DV. A tempting wrong route is option A, because the uniform is the first thing you notice. But Thomas changed it. He did not measure it. So A is the IV. So the answer is C. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is writing a vague DV, such as memory. Say exactly what was measured. Write number of words recalled out of twenty.

Your turn. Now you try. Winston puts a mobile phone on a seat near a police station. He records how male and female passers-by respond. Name the independent variable. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. What differs between the people? Some are male and some are female. Winston compares them. So the IV is whether the passers-by were male or female, which is gender. He did not create it, but he compares it. A common wrong answer is how passers-by responded to the phone. Winston recorded that. It is measured, so it is the DV. Giving it as the IV swaps the two, so it loses the mark. So find what differs. Find what is measured. Use the words of the study.
