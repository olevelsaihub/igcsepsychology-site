Idea. Before you were born, your brain grew from a tiny tube. About three weeks after conception, this neural tube forms three swellings. They are the forebrain at the front, the midbrain in the middle and the hindbrain at the back. The forebrain becomes the cerebrum. The cerebrum is used for thinking, planning and language. The midbrain links parts of the brain and helps with reflexes to sights and sounds. The hindbrain develops into several structures. Two matter most. The cerebellum coordinates movement, balance and posture, which helps skills like walking. The medulla controls automatic body functions, such as breathing and heart rate. The medulla begins to form around weeks six to seven of pregnancy. It also connects the brain to the spinal cord. So if it is damaged, signals to the body can be disrupted. A question names a brain part. You give its main job, then a second detail. That could be when it develops, an example, or how damage would affect the person.

Method. Step one: identify the named part and recall where it comes from. Step two: give its main job. Step three: add a second detail, such as when it develops, or how damage would affect the person in the question. Learn the four pairs. Forebrain, cerebrum and thinking. Midbrain, linking and reflexes. Cerebellum, movement and balance. Medulla, breathing and heart rate. The trap is mixing up the cerebellum with the cerebrum. The cerebellum is for coordination. The cerebrum is for thinking. And the medulla is automatic, not thinking.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. It tells us the cerebellum develops during early brain development. We must describe its role during development. It is two marks, so we need two separate facts. Fact one: where it comes from and its job. The cerebellum develops from the hindbrain. It coordinates movement, balance and posture. Fact two: what this lets a child do. As the cerebellum develops, a baby can learn motor skills, which are skills of movement, such as sitting up, crawling and walking. A tempting wrong route is to say the cerebellum controls breathing and heart rate. Reject it. That is the medulla. The cerebellum is about movement. Now write it out. The job first, then what it lets a child do. One mark for coordinating movement and balance. One mark for the motor skills it allows. Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing up the cerebellum and the cerebrum. The cerebellum coordinates movement and balance. The cerebrum does the thinking.

Your turn. Now you try. Finlay likes to dance and run. At eighteen months he fell and damaged his medulla. Explain one way Finlay could be affected, using the role of the medulla. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The medulla controls automatic functions such as breathing and heart rate. So Finlay could have trouble breathing, or his heart rate could be affected, because the damaged medulla cannot control them properly. The common wrong answer says he would struggle to dance and run.
