Idea. Ask yourself, who am I? You might say, I am fourteen, I am good at football, and I am a bit shy. All of those answers together make up your self-concept. Lewis said the self develops in two steps. The existential self comes first in babies. It is the awareness that you exist as a person separate from others. The categorical self comes next. It is describing yourself by categories, such as age, gender, size or skills. Three more terms. Self-image is how you see yourself. Self-esteem is how much you value yourself. Ideal self is the person you would like to be. Rogers said what matters is the match between self-image and ideal self. If they match, that is congruence. If they do not match, that is incongruence, and self-esteem falls. Self-actualisation, reaching your full potential, is easier when you are congruent. A question names one of these terms, or describes someone who feels not good enough. You name the idea, find the evidence, and justify with the theory.

Method. Step one: name the concept that fits and define it exactly. Step two: find the evidence in the scenario. What does the person think they are, and what do they want to be? Step three: justify with Rogers or Lewis. Explain the match or the gap, and its effect on self-esteem. The trap is mixing up self-image and self-esteem.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Talia has always wanted to be a doctor, but she finds it hard to talk to patients and staff, and feels not good enough. We must explain one reason, using incongruence. First, find the two selves in the story. Her ideal self is a caring doctor. Her self-image is someone who struggles to talk to patients. Those two do not match. Second, name the gap. Rogers calls a mismatch between ideal self and self-image incongruence. Incongruence lowers self-esteem, so Talia feels she is not good enough. A tempting wrong route is to just repeat that she feels not good enough. Reject it. That is the question, not a reason. You need the mismatch. Now write it out. Reason, then theory. One mark is for the reason from the story. One mark is for the theory. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to mix up self-image and self-esteem. Self-image is how you see yourself. Self-esteem is how much you value yourself. Incongruence is a gap that lowers self-esteem.

Answer. Lewis said the categorical self is describing yourself by categories, such as age, skills and size. Toby gives his age, three, says he is good at running, and says he is bigger than his sister. So the teacher knows he has a categorical self. The common wrong answer says Toby shows the existential self. But the existential self is only knowing you exist as separate from others.
