Idea. Two students are always tired. One lies awake all night and cannot sleep. The other falls asleep in the middle of lessons. Both have a sleep problem, but it is not the same one. A sleep disorder is a problem with the amount or quality of sleep that disrupts daily life. This topic covers two. They are insomnia and narcolepsy. Insomnia means difficulty getting to sleep, waking in the night, or waking too early, and not feeling refreshed. The effects are tiredness, irritability, poor concentration and memory, and mistakes at work. Narcolepsy means excessive sleepiness in the day, and sudden attacks of sleep at the wrong times. It may include cataplexy, which is a sudden loss of muscle control. It is linked to low levels of a brain chemical called hypocretin. So insomnia means cannot sleep. Narcolepsy means cannot stay awake. The question gives symptoms, and you pick the right disorder.

Method. Step one: decide which disorder fits. Awake at night means insomnia. Sudden sleep in the day means narcolepsy. Step two: define it by its symptoms. Step three: match the symptoms to the person and explain the result, such as tiredness or errors. The trap is listing symptoms without linking them. Use clue words. Awake at night, or cannot get back to sleep, means insomnia. Falls asleep suddenly, or collapses, means narcolepsy.

Worked example. Here is a real two mark question. Dennis is always tired. He goes to bed early and falls asleep quickly. But he wakes an hour later and cannot get back to sleep. He struggles at work and makes errors. We explain why, using one sleep disorder. Find the clue. He falls asleep, but wakes up and cannot get back to sleep. That is a problem staying asleep, so the disorder is insomnia. Mark one defines insomnia. It is difficulty getting to sleep, or waking up and not getting back to sleep, so the person has too little sleep. Mark two links it to Dennis. He loses most of his night's sleep. So he is tired all day, cannot concentrate, and makes errors at work. A tempting wrong route is to say narcolepsy, because he is always tired. But narcolepsy means sudden sleep attacks in the day. Dennis has none. Here is the answer. Insomnia is difficulty getting to sleep or staying asleep, so a person gets too little sleep. Dennis wakes after an hour and cannot get back to sleep, so he is tired and makes errors. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The classic slip is confusing the two disorders. Insomnia is not being able to sleep. Narcolepsy is sudden sleep in the day. Mixing them up loses both marks.

Your turn. Now you try. Duncan sometimes falls asleep suddenly in the day. He feels very drowsy and needs naps. He sometimes loses control of his muscles and collapses. Explain one sleep disorder that can account for this. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The disorder is narcolepsy. Narcolepsy is excessive sleepiness in the day, with sudden attacks of sleep. Duncan falls asleep suddenly and feels very drowsy, so he needs naps. Narcolepsy can also include cataplexy, a sudden loss of muscle control. That explains why Duncan can collapse. The common wrong answer says insomnia, because Duncan is tired. But insomnia is trouble sleeping. Duncan does not struggle to sleep. He falls asleep suddenly. So pick the disorder from the clues. Define it. Link it to the person.
