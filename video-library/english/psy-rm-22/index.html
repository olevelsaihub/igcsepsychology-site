Idea. Your teacher says your science experiment was unfair, because some groups got more time. What would you change? You would give every group the same time. That change is an improvement. In psychology, an improvement is a change to how a study is run or measured that fixes a weakness. Here are three ways to improve how a study is run. Standardise: give everyone the same task, instructions and time. Add a control group, a group that gets no treatment to compare with. Use a baseline, a score taken before the start, to measure change. Here are three ways to improve the design or the measure. Repeated measures means the same people do both conditions, so differences between people cannot spoil the result. A better measure uses more items, or more familiar ones. An independent rater is someone who scores the results without knowing the aim, which removes bias. The question says, explain one improvement. Two marks. One mark for a specific change, and one for what it fixes.

Method. Step one: find the flaw in the task, the measure or the controls. Step two: suggest one specific change to this study. Step three: explain what the change fixes and why that makes the result better. The trap is changing the sample when the flaw is in the task, or the reverse. Always match the change to the flaw.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Matthew tests a patient who has had a brain injury. We explain one improvement to his assessment. Two marks. Step one, the flaw. The patient could not name the objects. But a boat's anchor is something many people rarely see. So the patient may fail for that reason, not because of the injury. Step two, the change. Use pictures of very common objects, such as a cup, a spoon or a toothbrush. Then the reason: if the patient still cannot name them, the injury is the likely cause. A tempting wrong route is to test more patients. That is a sample change. The flaw here is in the objects, so it does not fix the flaw. The exam answer. One mark for the change and one for the reason. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is a reason like, so it is more reliable. That is a label with no explanation. Say what changes in the results.

Your turn. Now you try. Lucía's partner may have anterograde amnesia, which means being unable to make new memories after an injury. Explain one improvement to the test. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The flaw is that the partner may never have seen those news events on social media. So not recalling them would not prove amnesia. One improvement is to show the events to the partner during the test, then ask for recall after a delay. Now failing to recall shows they cannot make new memories. A common wrong answer is, use more events, or test more people. It does not fix the flaw, which is whether the events were seen. It loses the marks. So find the flaw, make one specific change, and say what it fixes.
