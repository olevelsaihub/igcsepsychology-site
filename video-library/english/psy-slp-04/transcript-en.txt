Idea. Imagine a room with no windows, no clock and no phone for a long time. Soon you would lose track of the time. You might sleep and wake at strange hours. Outside signals that keep us on time are called zeitgebers. The word means time givers. A zeitgeber is an external cue that resets the body clock. Light is the most important. Meal times and social routines are others. Light enters the eye and signals the body clock, the SCN. This keeps the sleep-wake cycle in line with the twenty-four hour day. Light is an external cue, not an internal one. Siffre, nineteen seventy-five, tested this. He lived alone in a cave for about six months, with no daylight, clocks or calendars. His sleep-wake cycle free-ran, which means nothing reset it. It varied from about eighteen to fifty-one and three quarter hours. He lost the date, and his memory and hand control got worse. Siffre was one man, and his lamp may itself have acted as a zeitgeber. Questions give you a person with cues present or missing. Explain it using zeitgebers or Siffre.

Method. Step one: say which external cue is present or missing. Step two: explain how that cue, or its absence, resets or upsets the sleep-wake cycle, or give Siffre's matching finding. Step three: for four marks, give two different cues or findings. The trap is calling light internal. Match the problem to a finding. No daylight means a drifting cycle and lost days. Isolation means worse memory and dexterity. Siffre's cycle was never exactly twenty-four hours.

Worked example. Here is a real four mark question. Leonardo, an astronaut in training, spends four months in a lab with no natural light. He loses track of days and struggles to know when to sleep. A crossword and sewing buttons get harder. We explain two ways Siffre's findings account for this. Match each problem to a finding. Losing track of days matches Siffre losing track of the date. Worse tasks match his falling memory and dexterity. Way one. With no sunlight, Leonardo has no external cue to keep his sleep-wake cycle to twenty-four hours. Siffre's cycle drifted from about eighteen to fifty-one and three quarter hours without daylight, and he lost track of days too. Way two. Leonardo finds crosswords and sewing harder. Siffre's memory and fine hand control also got worse during his long isolation. So the finding matches. A tempting wrong route says Siffre's cycle stayed at twenty-four hours, so Leonardo would be fine. But without daylight, Siffre's cycle drifted. That is the opposite of the finding. Here is the answer. Way one: with no sunlight Leonardo has no cue for a twenty-four hour day. Siffre's cycle drifted too. Way two: Siffre's memory and dexterity got worse in isolation, like Leonardo's tasks. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The classic slip is calling light an internal influence. Light comes from outside the body. It is an external zeitgeber. The body clock is the internal part.

Your turn. Now you try. Nick works nights. His friend David suggests dark curtains in the day and a daylight lamp at night. Explain two reasons why this may help, referring to zeitgebers. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Reason one: dark curtains block daylight, the zeitgeber that tells the body clock to be awake. So Nick can sleep in the day. Reason two: the daylight lamp is a light cue at night. It tells his body clock to be awake, so he stays alert. The common wrong answer says the curtains help because dark rooms are relaxing. That uses no zeitgeber, so it earns no marks. So name the cue. Explain how it resets the clock. Make each way different.
