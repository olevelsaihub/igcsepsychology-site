Idea. In the evening the lights go dim and you start to feel sleepy. Then you check a bright phone, and you feel awake again. Light seems to switch your sleepiness on and off. A hormone is a chemical messenger in the blood. The pineal gland is a small gland in the brain. In the dark it releases a hormone called melatonin. Melatonin makes us feel sleepy. Light information reaches it from the eyes, through the body clock. In darkness the pineal gland releases more melatonin, so we feel sleepy. In light it releases less melatonin, so we feel alert. This is why bright screens at night cause trouble. Bright artificial light cuts melatonin, so sleep is delayed. Melatonin supplements are sometimes used to help sleep problems and jet lag. The question asks you to describe the hormone chain, or explain how it affects a person's sleep, or give a strength and a weakness.

Method. Step one: describe the chain. Light level goes to the pineal gland, which makes more or less melatonin. Step two: say what that does. More melatonin means sleepy and less means alert. Then link it to the person. The traps are saying melatonin keeps you awake, or that light increases it. For strength and weakness, justify each point. A strength is that melatonin levels can be measured, so the evidence is scientific. A weakness is that hormones alone ignore psychological causes of poor sleep, such as stress.
