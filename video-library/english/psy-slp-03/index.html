Read the narration

Idea. In the evening the lights go dim and you start to feel sleepy. Then you check a bright phone, and you feel awake again. Light seems to switch your sleepiness on and off. A hormone is a chemical messenger in the blood. The pineal gland is a small gland in the brain. In the dark it releases a hormone called melatonin. Melatonin makes us feel sleepy. Light information reaches it from the eyes, through the body clock. In darkness the pineal gland releases more melatonin, so we feel sleepy. In light it releases less melatonin, so we feel alert. This is why bright screens at night cause trouble. Bright artificial light cuts melatonin, so sleep is delayed. Melatonin supplements are sometimes used to help sleep problems and jet lag. The question asks you to describe the hormone chain, or explain how it affects a person's sleep, or give a strength and a weakness.

Method. Step one: describe the chain. Light level goes to the pineal gland, which makes more or less melatonin. Step two: say what that does. More melatonin means sleepy and less means alert. Then link it to the person. The traps are saying melatonin keeps you awake, or that light increases it. For strength and weakness, justify each point. A strength is that melatonin levels can be measured, so the evidence is scientific. A weakness is that hormones alone ignore psychological causes of poor sleep, such as stress.

Worked example. Here is a real two mark question. It asks us to describe how the pineal gland and melatonin influence a person's sleep. It is a recall question, so no scenario is needed. Mark one is about the pineal gland. It receives signals about the light level, passed on from the body clock in the brain. Mark two is about melatonin. When it gets dark, the pineal gland releases more melatonin. This makes the person feel drowsy and ready for sleep. In bright light it releases less, so they feel awake. A tempting wrong route is to say the pineal gland is the hormone, or that melatonin keeps you awake. The gland makes the hormone, and melatonin makes you sleepy. Here is the answer. The pineal gland receives information about light from the body clock. In the dark it releases more melatonin, which makes a person drowsy and ready for sleep. In bright light it releases less, so they feel awake. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The classic slip is getting it backwards. Light does not increase melatonin. It reduces it. Melatonin does not keep you awake. It makes you sleepy.

Your turn. Now you try. Pragya struggles to wake in the mornings and feels sleepy all day. She is too tired to see her friends. Her doctor thinks there may be a problem with her pineal gland. Explain how it may be affecting her sleep. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The pineal gland releases melatonin, the hormone that makes us sleepy. Normally, morning light cuts melatonin release, so we wake up. Pragya's pineal gland may release too much melatonin, or keep releasing it into the morning and the day. So she struggles to wake and feels sleepy all day. The common wrong answer says her pineal gland is a hormone that makes her tired. The gland is not the hormone, and the answer never says how. Name melatonin and say what goes wrong with it. So give the chain. Light, pineal gland, melatonin. Then link it to the person.
