Idea. Imagine failing one test and thinking, I am useless, I will always fail. Thoughts like that can drag your mood down. The cognitive explanation says depression is caused by faulty thinking. Cognitive means to do with thinking. Irrational means not based on facts. Beck described three negative views, called the cognitive triad. A negative view of yourself, I am a failure. A negative view of the world, nobody helps. And a negative view of the future, it will never get better. A schema is a mental framework that organises what we know. Negative schemas often form in childhood, and stress switches them on. Cognitive biases then twist new information. One is overgeneralising, which means one bad event feels like proof of everything. How good is this explanation? A strength is research. Evans and colleagues found pregnant women with more negative beliefs about themselves had a higher chance of depression later. It also led to CBT, a talking therapy. A weakness is that it ignores biology, such as genes. And it is unclear whether negative thinking causes depression or is a result of it. A question gives a person and asks how this explanation accounts for their depression, or for a strength and weakness. Use their own words.

Method. Step one: state the cognitive idea, such as the triad or a negative schema. Step two: quote the person's thoughts, and label them self, world or future. For evaluation, give a strength and a weakness, and justify each for the person. The traps are describing CBT instead of the theory, and giving a generic strength with no link to the person. Saying the theory is too simple earns nothing unless you say what it leaves out.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Maddie has depression. She says she will always be a failure and will never get a promotion. We explain one strength and one weakness of using cognitive theory to account for her depression. Four marks. Strength first. Research supports the theory. Evans found women with more negative views of themselves were more likely to become depressed. That fits Maddie, who thinks she will always be a failure. Now a weakness. The theory ignores biology, such as genes or low serotonin, a brain chemical. So an inherited vulnerability or a chemical imbalance could explain Maddie's depression too. A tempting wrong route is to say the theory is too simple. That is vague. You must say what it leaves out, and link that to Maddie. The exam answer. Strength and justification. Weakness and justification. One mark for each point and one for each justification. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is a generic strength. Saying it has research support gets one mark at most. You must name the evidence and link it to the person.

Your turn. Now you try. Explain how cognitive theory could account for Maddie's depression. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. State the idea. Cognitive theory says depression is caused by negative, irrational thinking. Then quote Maddie. She says she will always be a failure, which is a negative view of herself. And she thinks she will never get a promotion or a partner, which is a negative view of her future. A common wrong answer only describes her symptoms. She feels sad and stopped going to work. That is not the theory. No negative thinking is named, so no mark. So state the idea, quote their thoughts, and label them.
