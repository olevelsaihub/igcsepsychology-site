Idea. Think of a football match crowd. A crowd is a large group of people in one place. Thousands of them, all dressed the same. Behaviour in a crowd can be kind or harmful. Pro-social behaviour is positive behaviour that helps others or society, such as helping, sharing or comforting. Anti-social behaviour is behaviour that harms others, or goes against social norms, causing alarm or distress. Examples are vandalism, or loud noise at night. It does not mean being shy. Deindividuation is when a person loses their sense of personal identity in a group, or through anonymity, and behaves differently from usual. Anonymity, such as masks, uniforms or darkness, and a large crowd raise it. Crowds can be pro-social, like a peaceful protest, or anti-social, like a riot. The norms of the group, and its leaders, shape which. A leader who calls for calm, and reminds people they are accountable for their own actions, can keep a crowd peaceful.

Method. Step one: spot whether you need a definition, a contrast, or an explanation of crowd behaviour. Step two: give an accurate definition, or name the crowd factor. Step three: add an example, or apply the factor to the crowd described. Read the scene for clues, such as a leader, masks or uniforms. The traps are defining anti-social behaviour as not being social, and giving a factor with no link to the crowd described.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Football fans protest outside the stadium, mostly in silence. The organiser uses a loudspeaker to tell them to stay calm. We explain one reason why the crowd is peaceful. Two marks. Step one: spot the detail. The organiser uses a loudspeaker, asks for calm, and reminds people they are responsible. That is a leader, an authority figure, and a reminder of accountability. Step two: explain the factor. Crowds follow someone they see as in charge. And when people are reminded they are personally accountable, they are less likely to become deindividuated. Step three: link it. The fans keep their individual identity and follow the organiser's lead, by protesting quietly with signs. A tempting wrong route is to say the crowd is peaceful because the fans are calm people. That just repeats the question. Give a reason: the leader and accountability. The exam answer. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing up deindividuation with diffusion of responsibility. Deindividuation is losing your personal identity in a crowd. Diffusion of responsibility is not helping because others are present.

Answer. Pro-social behaviour is positive behaviour that helps others or society, such as stopping to help someone. Anti-social behaviour is behaviour that harms others or breaks social norms, such as vandalism. One mark for each, and an example makes each clear. A common wrong answer is, anti-social means shy, or not social. It does not. Anti-social behaviour harms others or goes against norms, and causes alarm or distress. So give a precise definition, add an example, and link it to the scene.
