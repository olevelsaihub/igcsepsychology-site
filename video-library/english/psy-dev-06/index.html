Idea. Is it ever right to steal? A young child says no, because I will be punished. An older child says no, because it is against the law. Morality is a person's sense of what is right and wrong. Morals are the principles that guide their behaviour. For example, believing that stealing is wrong is a moral. The stages of morality come from Kohlberg's work and link to cognitive development. As thinking matures, children move from the pre-conventional stage, to the conventional stage, and possibly to the post-conventional stage. In the pre-conventional stage, right and wrong are judged by rewards and punishments. A child thinks, I should not steal because I will be punished. In the conventional stage, right and wrong are judged by rules, laws and what others approve of. Keeping order and obeying authority matter. In the post-conventional stage, a person uses their own ethical principles, even if these go against the law. A question may ask you to define morality, describe the difference between two stages, or predict what someone in a stage would say about a dilemma, which is a hard moral choice.

Method. Step one: identify which stage the question names. Step two: state what that stage uses to judge right and wrong: punishment, laws and approval, or personal principles. Step three depends on the question. For a difference, describe one stage, say whereas, then describe the other. For a dilemma, predict the judgement and give the reason from that stage. The trap is saying a conventional thinker accepts stealing for a good reason. That is post-conventional reasoning. A conventional thinker still says stealing is wrong.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Javier tells his son about a woman who steals food for her mother because they cannot afford it. He asks if she was right. We explain what the son would say if he is in the conventional stage. Two marks. Step one: describe the stage. In the conventional stage, people judge right and wrong by laws, rules and authority, so that society stays orderly. Step two: predict the judgement. The son would say the woman was wrong to steal the food, even to help her mother. A tempting wrong route is to say he would excuse her because her mother needs food. Reject it. That is post-conventional thinking, which puts personal principles above the law. Now write it out. The stage, then the judgement and reason. One mark for the stage. One mark for the judgement and reason. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake in a difference question is describing only one stage. The question wants both. Use the word whereas to bring them together.

Your turn. Now you try. Describe the difference between the conventional and post-conventional stages of morality. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. In the conventional stage, right and wrong are judged by laws, rules and what others approve of. Whereas in the post-conventional stage, a person uses their own ethical principles, which may go against the law. The common wrong answer describes only the conventional stage. It never describes the post-conventional stage, so it loses the second mark. So identify the stage, state its basis for judging, and use whereas for a difference.
