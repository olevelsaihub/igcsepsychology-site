Retrograde and anterograde amnesia

Idea. Imagine meeting someone, and five minutes later you have completely forgotten them. Or imagine not recognising your own childhood photos. This is amnesia. Amnesia means memory loss. It can be caused by brain damage, illness, surgery or drugs. The key idea is onset. Onset is the moment the amnesia begins. Memories can be lost from before that moment, or from after it. Retrograde amnesia means being unable to recall information or events from before the onset. Retro means backwards, so it reaches back into the past. The person can usually still learn new information. Anterograde amnesia means being unable to form new long-term memories after the onset. The person can usually still recall events from before. A famous case is HM, who could not form new long-term memories after brain surgery in nineteen fifty-three. A question describes a test. You decide whether it is about the past or about new learning, define the type of amnesia, and predict the result on that test.

Method. Step one: decide whether the test is about the past, before onset, or about new learning after onset. Step two: define the type of amnesia in the question. Step three: predict the result on the test, using its details. The clue is when the information was learned. Old information from before the onset is a problem in retrograde amnesia. New information since the onset is a problem in anterograde amnesia. The trap is saying the person forgets everything. Only one direction is lost. A retrograde patient can still learn. An anterograde patient still remembers the past.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Ivana tests a patient who may have retrograde amnesia from the last six months. She shows twenty photographs of famous people, who were in the news for five years before the test. We explain what she is likely to find. Two marks. First the clue. The faces were in the news for five years before the test. The amnesia started six months ago. So most faces were learned before the onset. Step one: define it. Retrograde amnesia means losing memories of information and events from before the amnesia began. Step two: predict. Ivana is likely to find the patient fails to recognise many of the twenty faces, especially those known before the amnesia began. A tempting wrong route is to say the patient cannot learn new faces. Reject it. That is anterograde amnesia. This test is about old memories. Now write it out. The definition, then the prediction. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is swapping the two types. Retrograde is losing the past. Anterograde is being unable to make new memories.

Your turn. Now you try. Lucía's partner hit their head and has been confused since. A psychologist gives them ten news events from social media since the fall, and asks them to recall what they can. Explain what the psychologist is likely to find if the partner has anterograde amnesia. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Anterograde amnesia means being unable to form new long-term memories after the onset. The news events all happened after the fall. So the partner will recall little or nothing about them. The common wrong answer says the partner will forget events from before the fall. That describes retrograde amnesia, so it loses the mark. So decide past or new, define the type, and predict the result on the named test.
