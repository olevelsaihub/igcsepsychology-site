Idea. Your friend sleeps eight hours and scores higher in a test than you. You might say: sleep helps test scores. That is a conclusion. A conclusion is a general statement about what the results show about the behaviour being studied. A conclusion needs proof. The proof is the data. Quote numbers. Compare the groups. Say which is higher or lower, or how many times bigger. Do not claim too much. If two groups score almost the same, the conclusion may be that the independent variable had little or no effect. The independent variable is the thing the researcher changes. A small sample also limits how far the findings apply. This question type gives a table or a short result. It asks you to explain one conclusion, or to give one way the findings support a conclusion.

Method. Step one: find the clearest difference or pattern. Step two: write a conclusion about the behaviour, using the words of the study. Step three: back it with figures. If the question says two conclusions, use two different patterns. The trap is describing the data without drawing a conclusion. Eighteen and five are just numbers. You must say what they mean about the behaviour.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Yolanda watches children play at a day care for four hours. She counts how many children played with vehicles. We must explain one conclusion about children's play from her results. It is worth two marks. Step one. Find the clearest pattern. Eighteen males played with vehicles. Only five females did. Step two. Write the conclusion about play. Males play with vehicles more than females. Step three. Add figures. Eighteen minus five is thirteen more males. And eighteen divided by five is three point six. So over three times as many males played with vehicles. A tempting wrong route is to say males are born to prefer vehicles. The data cannot show why. It shows only what these children did on one Monday. Stay with what the results show. Now the exam-ready answer. Males play with vehicles more than females, because 18 males played with vehicles compared with only 5 females. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is a conclusion with no figures. It sounds right, but the second mark is for evidence. Always add because, and then quote the numbers from the table.

Your turn. Now you try. Andrew showed people twenty trigrams. A trigram is a meaningless group of three letters. He counted how many each person recalled. Explain one conclusion about short-term memory. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. First add the males and females in each group. One person recalled one to four trigrams. Seven recalled five to eight. Eleven recalled nine to twelve. One recalled thirteen to sixteen. None recalled more. The pattern is clear. Eighteen of the twenty people recalled between five and twelve. So the conclusion is that short-term memory has a limited capacity. A common wrong answer says females have better short-term memory, because eight females recalled nine to twelve and only three males did. But there were fifteen females and only five males. Three out of five males is sixty per cent. Eight out of fifteen females is about fifty three per cent. So the raw counts mislead, and the answer loses the mark. So find the pattern. Write the conclusion about behaviour. Back it with figures.
