Idea. Think of a computer. You type something in. The computer works on it. Then it shows you an answer. Memory can be described in the same way. Information processing has three parts: input, processing and output. Input is information going in. Processing is what the brain does with it. Output is the response. Memory itself has three stages. They are encoding, storage and retrieval. Think of a photo on your phone. Saving it in the right form is encoding. Keeping it in the gallery is storage. Opening it later is retrieval. Information can be encoded as pictures, sounds or meaning. These are visual, acoustic and semantic. Retrieval can be recall, where you bring it back yourself, or recognition, where you spot it when you see it. You can forget at any stage. The information may never have been encoded. It may be lost from storage. Or you may fail to retrieve it. A question shows a diagram with a missing box, or asks you to define one stage. First check which sequence the diagram shows. Then choose the right term.

Method. Step one: recall the two sequences. Input, processing, output. And encoding, storage, retrieval. Most questions fill a gap in one of them. Step two: look at the boxes either side of the gap. They tell you which sequence is shown. Step three: write the term that fits. For a definition, say in one sentence what happens to information at that stage. The trap is confusing storage with retrieval. Storage is keeping information. Retrieval is getting it back out.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Figure 3 shows the three stages of the memory process, but one stage is missing. We complete the figure with the missing stage. One mark. Here is Figure 3. The first box is empty. The second box says storage. The third box says retrieval. Step one: storage and retrieval belong to the sequence encoding, storage, retrieval. So the empty box is its first stage. Step two: fit the term. Information must first be changed into a storable form. That is encoding. Then it is kept. Then it is retrieved. A tempting wrong answer is input. Reject it. Input belongs to the other sequence, input, processing, output. The boxes show the memory stages. Now the exam answer. One word in the empty box. One mark for writing encoding. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing the two sequences. Encoding does not go between input and output. Between those, the stage is processing.

Your turn. Now you try. Define what is meant by retrieval as part of the process of memory. It is one mark. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Retrieval means getting stored information back out of memory when it is needed. For example, you retrieve a friend's name when you see them. It is the last stage, after encoding and storage. The common wrong answer says retrieval is keeping information in memory. That describes storage, not retrieval, so it loses the mark. So recall both sequences, check the boxes either side, and write the term that fits. For a definition, say in one sentence what happens to the information.
