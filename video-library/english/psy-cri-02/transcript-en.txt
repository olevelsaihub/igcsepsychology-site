Idea. Think of a little brother who copies the way his big brother walks and talks. Nobody taught him. He just watched, remembered and copied. This is social learning theory. Bandura said we learn behaviour, including crime, by watching and copying others. The person we copy is a model. We copy models more when we identify with them, which means we feel similar to them or look up to them. Vicarious reinforcement is when you see someone else rewarded for a behaviour, so you become more likely to copy it. Direct reinforcement is when you are rewarded yourself. Now the famous study. Bandura, Ross and Ross, nineteen sixty-one, used seventy-two nursery children aged about three to five. Some watched an adult hit and shout at a Bobo doll. Some watched a calm adult. Some saw no model. The children who watched the aggression copied it most. Boys copied more physical aggression, and the male adult was copied more. A question describes someone who watched another person and then behaved the same way. You explain it using the theory, or a finding from the study.

Method. Step one: find the model and say why they are watched. Step two: name the process, such as observational learning, which is learning by watching, or vicarious reinforcement. Step three: show the copying later in the scenario. Step four: add a matching finding from the study. Longer assess questions work the same way, in three layers. Describe the study, apply it to the person, then judge it with strengths, weaknesses and other explanations.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Kevin and Chloe watched two girls fight. A week later one of them challenged them, but they said no and walked away. We must explain two ways the findings of Bandura, Ross and Ross account for this. Way one is about Kevin. His name suggests he is a boy, and the fighters were girls. Bandura found children copy a model of their own sex more, and boys copied the male adult most. So Kevin may not identify with the girls, and he does not copy them. Way two is about Chloe. Her name suggests she is a girl. Bandura found girls copied less physical aggression than boys. So Chloe is less likely to fight physically, and she walks away. A tempting wrong route is to say they saw a fight, so they will copy it. But Bandura found copying depends on who the model is, so that answer ignores the finding. Now write it out, one way at a time. Each way has a scenario point and a finding. That is two marks for the points and two for the findings. Common mistake. A common mistake is to describe the Bobo doll study but never link it to the people in the question. Always use a detail about the named people.

Your turn. Now you try. Jayne watched Harriet fight, and two days later fought her sister. Explain one reason why, using social learning theory. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Jayne watched Harriet punch and swear, so Harriet was a model for her. Social learning theory calls this observational learning: Jayne paid attention, remembered the fighting, and reproduced it two days later with her sister. A common wrong answer says Jayne saw Harriet rewarded, so it was vicarious reinforcement. But Harriet was excluded, which is not a reward. That answer does not fit the scenario, so it loses the mark. So find the model. Name the process. Show the copying. Add a finding from the study if asked.
