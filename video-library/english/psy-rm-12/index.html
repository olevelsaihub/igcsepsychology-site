Calculating percentages

Idea. You score eighteen out of twenty in a quiz. Your friend scores forty out of fifty. Who did better? To compare, we change both to a percentage. Percent means out of one hundred. Eighteen out of twenty is ninety per cent. Forty out of fifty is eighty per cent. The rule is simple. Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by one hundred. The part is the group you want. The whole is the total that group belongs to. Tables often give the pieces but not the total. Then you add the cells to find the whole. The part may need adding too. Always check which cells the question means. A percentage change uses the original value as the whole. A score falls from five to three. The change is two. Two divided by five is point four. That is a forty per cent decrease. This question type says calculate the percentage.

Method. Step one: find the part, adding cells if you need to. Step two: find the whole, the total the question names. Step three: divide part by whole and multiply by one hundred. Step four: round as asked and write the per cent sign. The trap is the wrong total. If the question says all participants, add every cell. Do not divide by one row or one column.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Thomas tests whether a man's clothes affect obedience. He records how many people obeyed an instruction to stay silent. We calculate the percentage who obeyed, out of all participants. It is worth two marks. Here is Table 1 again. We want everyone who obeyed. That is the obeyed column. Twenty two in Condition A and eleven in Condition B. Step one. Find the part. Twenty two plus eleven is thirty three people obeyed. Step two. Find the whole. All participants means every cell. Twenty two plus eight plus eleven plus nineteen is sixty. That matches thirty plus thirty. Step three. Divide thirty three by sixty, then multiply by one hundred. That gives fifty five per cent. A tempting wrong route is to divide by thirty, because each condition has thirty people. But the question says all participants. Thirty three divided by thirty is a hundred and ten per cent. That is impossible. Now the exam-ready answer. Total equals twenty two plus eight plus eleven plus nineteen, which is sixty. Obeyed equals twenty two plus eleven, which is thirty three. Thirty three over sixty times one hundred is fifty five per cent. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is percentage change using the new value. The score fell from five to three. The change is two. Divide by the original, five. Dividing by three gives the wrong answer.

Your turn. Now you try. Andrew showed twenty people twenty trigrams. A trigram is a meaningless group of three letters. The table shows how many trigrams they recalled. Calculate the percentage of all participants who recalled between nine and twelve. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The part is everyone who recalled nine to twelve. That is three males plus eight females, so eleven. The whole is every participant. Add all ten cells: the total is twenty. Now divide eleven by twenty, which is point five five. Multiply by one hundred. The answer is fifty five per cent. A common wrong answer counts only the females, eight, and divides by twenty. That gives forty per cent. But the question asks about all participants, so males count in the part too. Leaving them out loses the marks. So find the part. Find the whole. Divide and multiply by one hundred. Round at the end.
