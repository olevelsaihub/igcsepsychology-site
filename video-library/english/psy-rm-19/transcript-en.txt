Idea. Big numbers are awkward to write. A study might collect thirty nine million, nine hundred and eighty thousand responses. Standard form is a short way to write it. A number in standard form is a number from one up to, but not including, ten, times ten to a power. Here that is three point nine nine eight times ten to the power seven. An estimate means round the numbers first, then calculate. Round each to one or two significant figures. Four point eight times nine point seven becomes five times ten, which is fifty. Decimal places count digits after the decimal point. Significant figures count from the first digit that is not zero. Take nought point nought four five six seven. To two decimal places it is nought point nought five. To two significant figures it is nought point nought four six. To round, look at the next digit. Five or more rounds up. This question type may ask for a total, a difference, an estimate, standard form, or a rounded answer.

Method. Step one: spot the form the answer must take, such as standard form, an estimate, or two decimal places. Step two: do the calculation with exact numbers, or rounded numbers for an estimate. Step three: round at the end, to the stated precision. The trap is calculating exactly when the question says estimate. An estimate uses rounded numbers.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. A patient drinks twelve point eight one nine two four units of alcohol. We give that number to two decimal places. It is worth one mark. Step one. Spot the form. Two decimal places means we keep two digits after the point. That keeps twelve point eight one. Step two. Look at the next digit. It is nine. Nine is five or more, so we round up. Step three. Round up the last kept digit. One becomes two. So twelve point eight one becomes twelve point eight two. A tempting wrong route is to cut the number off after two decimal places. That gives twelve point eight one. Cutting ignores the next digit. Rounding does not. Now the exam-ready answer. Twelve point eight two units. Common mistake. A common mistake in standard form is a first number of ten or more. Thirty nine point nine eight times ten to the power six has the right value, but thirty nine is more than ten. Move the point once more to get three point nine nine eight times ten to the power seven.
