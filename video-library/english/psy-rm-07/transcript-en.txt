Idea. A teacher watches the playground to see who plays alone. She does not ask anyone. She just watches. That is an observation. An observation is a method where the researcher watches and records behaviour. It can be overt, where people know they are watched, or covert, where they do not. In a participant observation, the observer joins in. In a non-participant observation, the observer stays separate. The big strength is natural behaviour, so high ecological validity, which means findings true to real life. Weaknesses are observer bias, where the observer sees what they expect, and ethical issues if covert. A case study is an in-depth study of one person or a small group, often over a long time, using several methods such as interviews, tests and observation. A famous case is H.M. Brain surgery in nineteen fifty-three left him unable to form new long-term memories. The strength of a case study is rich detail, and insight into rare cases. The weaknesses are that you cannot generalise, which means apply the findings to everyone, and that researcher bias and replication are problems. This type of question asks you to define, describe or evaluate an observation or a case study, using the study in the question.

Method. Step one: name the method and its key feature. For observation, it is natural behaviour. For a case study, it is one person in depth. Step two: say why it suits, or does not suit, this study. Every point should build from what the method is. For a twelve-mark evaluation, weigh validity and detail against bias, ethics and generalising, using the study. End with a justified judgement. The trap is defining a case study as a study of a case. That says nothing.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Kamira wants to find out how likely people are to return lost items. She sits in a food court and watches what passers-by do when someone leaves an item behind. We describe why she may have used an observation. It is worth two marks. Step one. Name the feature. An observation records what people really do, in a real setting, without asking them. Step two. Say why that suits Kamira. If she asked people would you return a lost item, most would say yes. They want to look good. Watching shows what they really do. A tempting wrong route is to say observation was used because people always answer honestly. That is not true. The real reason is that watching avoids what people say, and records what they do. Now the exam-ready answer. Observation records people's real behaviour in a real setting. Kamira could watch what passers-by really do with a lost item, rather than rely on what they say they would do, so her findings are more valid. Common mistake. A common mistake is listing strengths of observation without linking them to the study. Always say what it lets this researcher do.

Your turn. Now you try. Rocio ran a case study on her patient, who had an addiction to alcohol. She worked with the patient for six months. Define what is meant by a case study. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. A case study is an in-depth study of one person or a small group. It often lasts a long time and uses several methods. For Rocio, that was one patient over six months. A common wrong answer says a case study is a study of a case. That repeats the words without explaining them, so it loses the mark. So name the feature. Say why it suits the study. Use the study's details.
