Idea. Think of a relay race. One runner carries the baton to the end of their stretch, then hands it on to the next runner. Your nerves work like that. A neuron is a nerve cell that carries electrical messages. One neuron hands its message to the next one. The handover happens at a synapse. A synapse is the tiny gap between two neurons. The impulse cannot jump the gap. Instead, little bags called vesicles release chemical messengers into the gap. These are neurotransmitters, such as serotonin and dopamine. Receptors are the parts on the next neuron that catch the neurotransmitters. They fit like a lock and key. Only the matching neurotransmitter fits. Once it binds, the next neuron can pass the message on. Now the bigger picture. The central nervous system, or CNS, is the brain and the spinal cord. The spinal cord carries messages between the body and the brain. If it is damaged, messages from the body cannot reach the brain. This question type asks you to state or describe how neurons and synapses work, or to apply the CNS to a person.

Method. Step one: use the exact terms: impulse, vesicle, neurotransmitter, synaptic gap, receptor. Marks go to accurate terms. Step two: if you describe the synapse, put the steps in order. Out of order shows weak understanding. Step three: for a person with a symptom, say the message travels along the spinal cord to or from the brain, then link it to the symptom. The trap is saying neurotransmitters travel along the neuron. They cross the gap.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Serinda has had a motorbike accident. The doctor puts hot and cold pads on her arms and legs. She cannot feel them on her legs. The doctor says her central nervous system is damaged. We explain one reason why, using the role of the CNS. Step one. State the role. The CNS is the brain and spinal cord. The spinal cord carries messages from the body up to the brain. Step two. Apply it. Serinda cannot feel the pads on her legs. So the messages from her legs are not reaching her brain. That suggests the spinal cord is damaged. A tempting wrong route is to say her legs are broken, so they cannot feel. But the doctor concluded the central nervous system is damaged. Your answer must be about the CNS, not the legs. Now the exam-ready answer. The CNS is the brain and spinal cord, and the spinal cord carries messages from the body to the brain. Serinda cannot feel the pads on her legs, so the messages are not reaching her brain. This suggests her spinal cord is damaged. That is one mark for the role and one for the application. Common mistake. A common mistake is saying the neurotransmitters travel along the neuron. They are released into the synaptic gap and cross it. They bind to receptors, not to the synapse.

Your turn. Now you try. State two processes that happen in synaptic functioning. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Process one: neurotransmitters are released from vesicles into the synaptic gap. Process two: the neurotransmitters bind to receptors on the next neuron, like a lock and key. Each process earns one mark. A common wrong answer says the neurotransmitters travel along the neuron, or that receptors bind to the synapse. Both mix up where things happen, so they lose the mark. So use the exact terms. Put the steps in order. Say what each part does.
