Read the narration

Idea. In eighteen forty-eight, a railway worker called Phineas Gage had an iron rod blasted through his head. He survived. But friends said he was a different man, rude, impulsive and unreliable. Scientists wanted to know which part of his brain was damaged. In nineteen ninety-four, Damasio and colleagues worked it out. Their aim was to find which areas were damaged, using modern technology. They photographed and measured Gage's skull. They built a three-D computer model of a similar brain. Then they worked out the possible paths of the rod. The damage was to the ventromedial region of both frontal lobes, more on the left. The frontal lobes are the front part of the brain. The ventromedial region is the lower middle part of them. Broca's area, which helps produce speech, and the motor cortex, which controls movement, were not damaged. Their conclusion: damage to the ventromedial region makes rational decision-making harder and affects how emotion is processed. This fits Gage's behaviour, and patients today with similar damage. This type of question gives you a person with a brain injury. You compare their damage with Gage's, then explain or evaluate.

Method. Step one: recall the detail you need, such as the aim, the procedure or the damaged areas. Step two: compare the person's damage with Gage's. Ventromedial is a match. Broca's area is not. Step three: predict or evaluate. A match means similar problems. Extra damage means effects Damasio cannot predict. For strength and weakness questions, give a strength where the damage matches Gage. Give a weakness where it does not. Justify each one using the person. The trap is saying Broca's area was damaged in Gage. It was not.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Marvin teaches languages. After an accident, his frontal lobes are damaged, including the ventromedial region and Broca's area. We explain one strength and one weakness of using Damasio to account for the outcome. It is worth four marks. Compare the damage. Marvin's ventromedial region is damaged, like Gage's. That is the strength. Broca's area is damaged in Marvin, but it was intact in Gage. That is the weakness. Strength. The same area is damaged, and Damasio linked it to poor decisions. So it is reasonable to predict Marvin will have similar problems. Weakness. Broca's area was intact in Gage, so Damasio says nothing about it. It affects speech, so Damasio cannot predict how it will change Marvin's teaching. A tempting wrong route is to say Broca's area was damaged in Gage too. It was not. Using that would make your weakness wrong. Now the exam-ready answer. Write one strength and one weakness, each with its reason. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is describing Gage's accident without linking it to the person. Always point to the person's own damage.

Your turn. Now you try. Holly asks ten patients with pre-frontal cortex damage about their personality before and after. Explain what she is likely to find, referring to a study. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Use Damasio. Gage had ventromedial damage, and his personality changed. He became rude and impulsive. So Holly is likely to find that her patients say their personality has changed, for example they now make poor decisions or act on impulse. A common wrong answer says the patients will have no memory of before the damage, or cannot speak. Damasio did not find that. Broca's area and movement were not damaged. So that loses the mark. So recall the study. Compare the damage with Gage's. Then predict or evaluate.
