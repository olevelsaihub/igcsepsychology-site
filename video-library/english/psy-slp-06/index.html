Idea. You dream you are at school in your pyjamas. Some people think a dream like this has a hidden meaning. That is the idea behind Freud's theory of dreaming. Freud wrote about dreams in nineteen hundred. He said dreams are the royal road to the unconscious, which means thoughts we are not aware of. Dreams show repressed wishes and fears, pushed out of awareness, in disguised form. A wish shown in a dream is called wish fulfilment. The dream we remember is the manifest content. It often uses people and events from recent days. The hidden meaning underneath is the latent content. Do not swap them. Dreamwork is the unconscious process that turns latent content into the manifest dream. Condensation joins several ideas into one image. Displacement moves a feeling onto another person or thing. Symbolisation shows an idea as a symbol. Secondary elaboration tidies the dream into a story. Freud used case studies, which are detailed studies of one person, such as Little Hans. But interpretations are subjective and cannot be tested, and dream recall is unreliable. Activation synthesis, a biological theory, says dreams are random brain activity. Questions ask you to define, interpret or evaluate.

Method. Step one: define the term the question asks for, such as manifest, latent or dreamwork. Step two: interpret the dream details as disguised wishes or worries from the person's life. Step three: evaluate with evidence or an alternative theory, then judge. The trap is swapping manifest and latent. Nine mark questions use three skills. Describe the theory, apply it to the dream, then evaluate with a strength, a weakness and an alternative theory. Finish with a judgement.

Worked example. Here is a real four mark question. Marcel is a therapist using Freud's theory. A client dreams of being chased, avoiding gaps and falling objects. We explain two weaknesses of Marcel using Freud's theory, justified with concepts or research. Choose two clear weaknesses. One is that interpretations are subjective. The other is that a biological theory offers a different explanation. Weakness one. Marcel's reading of the chase is subjective. He might say the gaps are fears, but another therapist could read it differently. Freud's ideas cannot be tested, so no one can prove which reading is right. Weakness two. Activation synthesis theory, from Hobson and McCarley, says that in REM sleep the brain fires random signals, and then builds them into a story. Random activity in movement areas could explain the running and falling objects. So the chase may have no hidden meaning for Marcel to find. A tempting wrong route is to say Freud is wrong because dreams are not real. That is an opinion. Use a concept or evidence, such as subjectivity or another theory. One: Marcel's interpretation is subjective, and Freud's ideas cannot be tested. Two: activation synthesis says the brain builds dreams from random signals in REM sleep, so the chase may mean nothing. Common mistake. The classic slip is swapping manifest and latent. Manifest is the dream as remembered. Latent is the hidden meaning.

Your turn. Describe how dreamwork is used to uncover the latent content of dreams. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Dreamwork is the unconscious process that disguises the hidden latent content as the manifest dream, using condensation, displacement, symbolisation and secondary elaboration. To uncover the latent content, an analyst works backwards. They interpret the symbols in the remembered dream to find the hidden wish or fear. The common wrong answer says dreamwork is the hard work of dreaming, or that it is the latent content itself. It must name the disguise and the manifest dream. So define the term. Interpret using the person's life. Evaluate when asked.
