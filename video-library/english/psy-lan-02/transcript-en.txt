Idea. Does the language you speak change what you notice? Linguistic relativism says it influences how you think about the world. It is the weak version of the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis. A hypothesis is an idea that can be tested. Linguistic determinism is the strong version. It says language decides what you can think. In two thousand and one, Boroditsky compared English and Mandarin speakers on time. English speakers mostly talk about time horizontally, with words like ahead and behind. Mandarin speakers also use vertical words, up for earlier and down for later. Her test used spatial primes. A prime is a picture statement shown first, horizontal or vertical. Then people answered true or false questions about time. Mandarin speakers were faster after vertical primes. English speakers were faster after horizontal primes. Everett found the Piraha tribe have words only for one, two and many. They struggled to match larger amounts. This supports determinism. But people can learn new words, and can think of things without a word. That weakens determinism. A question gives a person and an effect of language, and asks you to explain it, using one version.

Method. Step one: check which version the question names. Step two: define it. Step three: use the exact words or behaviour in the stem. Step four: back it with a study. For a nine-mark question, these steps grow into a longer answer. Define and contrast. Apply to the stem. Weigh evidence and the other version. End with a judgement. Today we practise the core on a two-mark part.

Worked example. Here is a real two-mark question. Ivan's art teacher suggests shades of blue: aqua, turquoise, azure and sky. Ivan does not know them, because he thinks of blue as light or dark. We must explain why, using linguistic relativism. Step one: define relativism. The language we use influences how we see the world. Step two: use the stem. Ivan only uses two blue words, light blue and dark blue. So he groups every shade into those two. A tempting wrong route is to say Ivan cannot see aqua at all, because he has no word. That is determinism, the strong version. The question says relativism. Reject it. The exam answer. Define. Then link to Ivan. One mark for the definition. One mark for linking it to Ivan's two blue words. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is swapping the versions. Students say relativism means language decides what you can think. That is determinism. Relativism only says language influences.

Answer. Language influences how we think. So speakers of different languages may understand the same thing differently. For example, Boroditsky found English speakers use horizontal words for time, but Mandarin speakers also use vertical words. So the two groups may picture time running in different directions.
