Idea. Imagine a friend who thinks, if I miss one message, everyone will hate me. Helping them change that thought is what CBT does. CBT is cognitive behavioural therapy. It is a talking therapy that changes negative or irrational thoughts, and the behaviour linked to them. In CBT, the client and the therapist spot irrational thoughts together. They challenge them by testing them against evidence. Then the client learns to replace them with more realistic thoughts. Between sessions, clients do homework. They practise new ways of thinking and acting. One example is a thought diary, where they write what happened, what they thought, and the evidence against the thought. For addiction, CBT helps the person spot their triggers, the things that set off the urge. It helps them manage cravings, and build coping strategies. These prevent relapse, which means starting to use again. A question gives a person and asks how CBT could help them. Name one specific technique, then show it in the person's own problem. Never just say, talk about problems.

Method. Step one: name one specific technique, such as challenging irrational thoughts, homework or a thought diary. Step two: show what it would look like for this person, using details from the scenario. Both steps earn a mark. The traps are vague talk, such as talking about problems, not naming a technique, describing drug treatment, and repeating the same idea as both marks. For evaluation, a weakness could be that CBT needs motivation, and takes time.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Jeremie has an addiction to online auctions. We explain one way CBT could help him. Two marks. Step one: name the technique. Identifying and challenging irrational thoughts. The therapist helps Jeremie spot a thought, and question it using evidence. Step two: apply it to Jeremie. He may think, I must keep bidding to feel good. The therapist can ask how many of his hundreds of items he really needs. That challenges the thought, so he buys less. A tempting wrong route is to say the therapist talks to Jeremie about his problems. That is vague, and not a CBT technique. Reject it. The exam answer. Technique, then Jeremie. One mark for explaining the technique. One mark for applying it to Jeremie's auction buying. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is not applying the technique. Saying CBT changes thoughts is only half the answer. You must show what it looks like for this person.

Your turn. Now you try. Explain one way CBT could be used to help Kesia's addiction. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Name the technique. CBT helps Kesia identify her triggers, such as the daily trip to the shopping centre after work. Then apply it. The therapist helps her challenge the thought that she must shop to cope, and plan something else to do. Her buying, and her debt, can fall. A common wrong answer says Kesia would talk to a therapist about her debts. That is vague. It names no CBT technique, and it loses both marks. So name one technique, then show it in the person's problem.
