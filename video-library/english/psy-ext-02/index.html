Idea. Is your football skill from your genes, or from your practice? In psychology, a debate is a big question where psychologists disagree about the cause of behaviour. Each compulsory topic has one debate, and the nine-mark essays are about them. Nature means behaviour comes from inherited, biological factors, such as genes, or serotonin, a chemical messenger in the brain. Nurture means it comes from the environment and experience, such as upbringing or stressful life events. Another debate is morality, your sense of right and wrong. Kohlberg described three stages. Pre-conventional: right is what avoids punishment. Conventional: right is what others approve of, or what the law says. Post-conventional: right comes from your own principles. Each essay gives three marks for knowledge, three for applying it to the stem, the text before the question, and three for evaluation. Ignore the named debate, and you cannot go above Level two.

Method. Step one: name the debate, and define both sides. Step two: apply each side to the stem. Step three: use research as evidence for each side, with limits. Step four: reach a judgement on the exact wording. The traps are never using the debate words, arguing only one side, and listing studies without saying what they show.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Depression has nature and nurture causes, and it affects people and society. We assess the importance of nature or nurture as its cause. Nine marks, so we plan it. Step one: define both sides. Nature: genes, or low serotonin. Nurture: stressful life events, or negative thinking learned from experience. Diathesis-stress says both: an inherited weakness, the diathesis, is triggered by stress. Step two: use the stem. For the person, a biological cause may mean less blame. For society, depression costs jobs, health services and public money, so the cause decides where money goes. Step three: evidence. Drugs called SSRIs raise serotonin and reduce symptoms for many people, which supports nature, but they do not work for everyone. CBT, a talking therapy that challenges negative thoughts, also works for many, which supports nurture. The strongest evidence is Caspi and colleagues, in two thousand and three. People with a short version of a serotonin gene who also had several stressful life events became depressed more often. A tempting wrong route is to argue only nature, because genes sound convincing. Assess means weighing up both sides. Step four, the judgement. Nature and nurture work together. That matters, because it supports combining drugs with CBT and reducing stress, which helps people and society. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is writing about the topic, but never using the debate words. A depression essay that never says nature or nurture stays at Level two. Name the debate, and keep using it.

Your turn. Now you try. Three men judge a woman who steals to help her brother. You do not need to write the whole essay: plan it. Match each man to a stage, then evaluate and judge. Write your plan, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Man B says she might get in trouble: avoiding punishment, so pre-conventional. Man C says her family might disapprove: seeking approval, so conventional. Man A says everyone is equal: his own principle, so post-conventional. Evaluate. The stages fit all three reasons, and they link to cognitive development, as moral thinking matures with thinking. But the men only said what they think, which may not be what they do. Judgement: the stages explain their reasons well, but not their actions. A common wrong answer just names the stages, with no reason from each man. Each stage must be linked to what that man said. So name the debate, define both sides, and use the debate words in every paragraph.
