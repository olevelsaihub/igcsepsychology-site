Idea. Everyone feels sad sometimes, or gets hooked on a phone game. A mental health problem is when feelings or behaviour become so strong that they harm daily life. Doctors use a list called the ICD, the International Classification of Diseases. A symptom is a sign that a problem is there. Depression is a low mood problem. The main symptom is low mood or loss of interest, most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks. Others are tiredness, poor concentration, feeling worthless or guilty, feeling hopeless about the future, and changes in sleep and appetite. Addiction is called dependence in the ICD. The person cannot control their use of a substance or behaviour, like gambling. Use comes before other duties, and carries on despite harm. Tolerance means needing more for the same effect. Withdrawal means unpleasant symptoms when use stops. Mental health problems affect the person and society. A person may struggle with work or school, relationships, health, money and self-care. Society pays for treatment, loses working days, and families carry the pressure. Addiction can also link to crime. A question names depression or addiction, and asks you to state symptoms, describe an effect, or explain one for a person. Check which problem, and whether it is the person or society.

Method. Step one: decide the problem and who is affected. Step two: use the exact ICD term, such as impaired control or withdrawal. Step three: give a different point for each mark. Step four: in a scenario, show where the symptom appears in the person. Two versions of the same symptom earn only one mark. Giving a cause, such as genes, instead of a symptom earns nothing. And do not mix up depression and addiction.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Two marks. It is a state question, so we write two short, different features of addiction. Step one: the question says addiction, not depression. So we ignore low mood, tiredness and the rest. Step two: recall the features. Impaired control. Use more important than other things. Carrying on despite harm. Tolerance. Withdrawal. We pick two that are clearly different. A tempting wrong route is to write a cause, like genes or stress, or a depression symptom like low mood. Those are not features of addiction. Reject them. The exam answer. Two lines, one feature each. One mark for each different feature. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is repeating one feature in different words. Cannot stop, and cannot control how much, are both impaired control. That earns one mark, not two.

Your turn. Now you try. Name one symptom of addiction, then say how it affects Jeremie. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Pick one symptom: impaired control. Jeremie cannot limit his buying, so he keeps buying hundreds of items. Then say the effect. He may spend money he does not have, and fall into debt. Symptom, then effect on Jeremie. A common wrong answer says Jeremie is addicted, so he buys a lot. That repeats the question. It names no symptom and gives no effect. So decide the problem, use the exact term, then show the effect on the person.
