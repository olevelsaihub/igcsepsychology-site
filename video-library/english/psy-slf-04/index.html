Idea. A two-year-old shouts no and wants to do everything alone. A teenager cannot decide who they want to be. A lonely adult wants close friends. Each age seems to have its own struggle. Erikson, nineteen fifty-nine, proposed eight stages across the whole lifespan. Each stage has a conflict to resolve, which means a struggle between two outcomes. The first four stages are in childhood. The other four stages cover adolescence and adult life. Identity versus role confusion is in adolescence. Intimacy versus isolation is young adulthood. Generativity versus stagnation is middle age. Integrity versus despair is old age. A question gives an age and a behaviour, and asks which stage explains it, or how well Erikson explains it.

Method. Step one: use the age in the scenario to choose the stage. Step two: state the conflict of that stage in full. The trap is choosing the wrong stage, or naming it without its conflict. Step three: link the behaviour to the conflict. If asked, judge Erikson. A strength is that it covers the whole life. A weakness is that ages are approximate, and it describes but does not give practical advice.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Kim is two years old. She says no to bedtime and vegetables, and insists on putting on her own shoes. We must give one strength and one weakness of using Erikson to explain Kim. First, the age. Kim is two, so the stage is autonomy versus shame and doubt. Autonomy means independence. Kim is trying to be independent. The strength: it explains why Kim says no and insists on her own shoes. Her mother can see this as normal behaviour and give Kim safe chances to do things herself. The weakness: Erikson describes what children do at each age, but does not say how to help them. It does not tell Kim's mother how to handle the refusals or the shoe struggles. A tempting wrong route is to name a different stage, such as trust versus mistrust, because Kim is small. Reject it. Trust is infancy. At two, the stage is autonomy. Now write it out. Strength with a reason. Weakness with a reason. Two points and two reasons make four marks. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to mix up generativity and integrity, or to name a stage without its conflict. Learn each stage as a pair, with the age.

Your turn. Now you try. Maria is sixteen. She did better in her GCSEs than expected and cannot decide which college to choose. Explain how her stage of identity development could be the reason. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Maria is sixteen, which is adolescence. The stage is identity versus role confusion. She is working out who she is and what she wants to be. That makes choosing a college hard, because she is unsure which path fits her. The common wrong answer picks intimacy versus isolation, because she is nearly an adult. But that stage is about close relationships, from about eighteen. It does not fit her choice, so it loses the mark. So use the age. State the conflict. Link the behaviour.
