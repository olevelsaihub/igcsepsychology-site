Idea. Your brain is like a school with departments, and each department has a job. The cortex is the wrinkled outer layer. It is split into four lobes, which are large sections. They are the frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobes. Each lobe comes in a pair, one on each side of the brain. Below them, at the back, sits the cerebellum. The frontal lobes handle planning, decisions, personality, and voluntary movement, which is movement you choose. They hold Broca's area, which produces speech. The temporal lobes handle hearing, understanding language, and memory. The parietal lobes handle touch, pain, temperature and pressure, and spatial awareness, which means knowing where things are. The occipital lobes, at the very back, handle vision: colour, shape and movement. The cerebellum controls balance, coordination, and fine control of movement, like threading a needle. If a region is damaged, the person has problems with that region's function. That is how we work out which region is damaged. A question describes tests a person does well or badly on. You decide which region is damaged, and explain using its function.

Method. Step one: find the task they do badly on, and the tasks they do well on. Step two: match the poor ability to the lobe or the cerebellum responsible. Step three: explain, using the task details, why that region must be damaged. The traps are swapping parietal and occipital, naming a region without its function, ignoring the tasks done well, and calling the cerebellum a lobe.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Sandy tests a patient with damage to Region G. She does well on recall and pressure, but badly on naming a colour. We explain which region is damaged, and refer to its function. Two marks. Step one: sort the results. Colour naming was poor. Recall and pressure were fine. Step two: match. Seeing colour is vision, and the occipital lobes handle vision. Pressure was fine, so the parietal lobes work. Recall was fine, so the temporal lobes work. A tempting wrong route is to say parietal, because the colour test uses the senses. But the pressure test was done well, and touch is parietal. Colour is vision, which is occipital. Reject parietal. The exam answer. Region and function, then the link to the tests. One mark for knowing the occipital lobes handle vision. One mark for linking the poor colour naming to the damage. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is swapping the lobes. Touch and pain are parietal. Vision is occipital. Mixing them loses the knowledge mark.

Answer. State the function. The temporal lobes handle hearing and memory. Then predict. Participants with damage may not hear the song well, or remember it. So they will sing it worse than the group without damage. A common wrong answer says only that the damaged group will sing worse. It gives no function, so it loses the knowledge mark. So find the poor task, match it to a region, and explain with its function.
