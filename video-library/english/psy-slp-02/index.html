Idea. You feel sleepy at about the same time every night, even when there is no clock in the room. Your body seems to keep its own time. These natural cycles are called bodily rhythms. A bodily rhythm is a biological cycle that controls things like sleep, body temperature and hormone release. Circadian rhythms repeat about every twenty-four hours. Ultradian rhythms repeat more than once in twenty-four hours. The body clock is a small area in the brain called the suprachiasmatic nucleus, or SCN. It is an internal pacemaker, so it works from inside the body. Light from the outside world resets it each day. The circadian trough is the low point in the early hours of the morning. Body temperature and alertness are lowest then. Shift work and jet lag put the body clock out of step with the outside day. The result is tiredness and poor sleep. The question gives a shift worker or a traveller. You explain their problem using circadian rhythms, usually in two different ways.

Method. Step one: name the rhythm and its key feature, such as the twenty-four hour cycle or the trough. Step two: show how being out of step causes the problem in the story. Step three: for four marks, give two different ways. The trap is two ways that are really the same point. Longer assess questions are marked on knowledge, application and evaluation. For evaluation, Siffre's cave study supports an internal rhythm. But external cues such as light also matter, so it is not the whole story.

Worked example. Here is a real four mark question. Deandre is a nurse on night shifts. He feels very tired and cannot stay alert. At home he wakes every thirty minutes and only feels tired when it is time to go back to work. We explain two ways circadian rhythms account for this. There are two problems, so there are two ways. Problem one is tiredness at night. Problem two is broken sleep in the day. Way one. His body clock is still on a daytime pattern. So he is awake through the circadian trough in the early hours, when temperature and alertness are lowest. That is why he is so tired. Way two. When he gets home it is daylight. Light tells the body clock it is time to be awake. So his body signals wakefulness, and he keeps waking. He only feels sleepy when the cycle swings back in the evening. A tempting wrong route is to blame the ninety minute sleep cycle for the waking. But that is an ultradian rhythm, and the question asks for circadian. Here is the answer. Way one: awake in the early hours trough, he is very tired. Way two: daylight tells his body clock to be awake, so he wakes every thirty minutes. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The classic slip is mixing up circadian and ultradian. Circadian is about twenty-four hours. Ultradian is more than once in twenty-four hours, such as the ninety minute sleep cycle.

Your turn. Now you try. Describe how the sleep cycle can be classed as an ultradian rhythm. It is worth two marks. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. An ultradian rhythm repeats more than once in twenty-four hours. The sleep cycle lasts about ninety minutes and repeats four or five times a night. It moves through the non-REM stages and then REM. So it is ultradian. The common wrong answer says the sleep cycle takes twenty-four hours. That is the sleep-wake cycle, a circadian rhythm. It earns no marks here. So name the rhythm. Show the link. Give a second way if marks need it.
