Idea. Two parents. One says, I love you whatever happens. The other says, I love you when you bring home top marks. The children grow up feeling very differently about themselves. One feels safe and accepted. The other feels they must keep earning love. Rogers said people need positive regard. That means love, warmth and acceptance from others. Unconditional positive regard is given whatever you do or achieve. Conditional positive regard is given only when you meet other people's expectations. Conditional regard creates conditions of worth. These are standards a person absorbs and then judges themselves by, such as, I am only good if I get top marks. The effects are different. Self-esteem means how much you value yourself. Conditional regard can cause low self-esteem, anxiety and incongruence, which is a gap between how you see yourself and who you want to be. Unconditional regard supports high self-esteem and confidence. A question gives a situation with praise or love, or asks you to define a term. You decide which kind it is, define it, and give the effect on self-esteem. Short questions are worth two marks.

Method. Step one: decide whether the regard is conditional or unconditional. Does the love depend on achievement? Step two: define that type exactly. The trap is calling all praise unconditional. Step three: explain the effect on self-esteem or behaviour. That is the second mark. Do not just describe the regard and stop.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. It asks us to describe how conditional positive regard may affect an individual's self-esteem. There is no story here. It is two marks. Step one: define it. Conditional positive regard means a person only receives love or approval when they do what others expect, for example getting top grades. Step two: give the effect. Because they feel valued only when they succeed, they keep doubting whether they are good enough. This lowers their self-esteem. Without this step, you only earn half the marks. A tempting wrong route is to say conditional regard builds confidence, because praise feels good. Reject it. The praise depends on success, so confidence is fragile. Now write it out. Definition, then effect. One mark for the definition. One mark for the effect. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to call all praise unconditional. If the praise only comes for success, it is conditional. Unconditional regard means acceptance whatever you do.

Your turn. Now you try. Lorenzo often tells his daughter that she is perfect the way she is. She is confident and tries new things. Explain one reason why, using positive regard. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Lorenzo accepts his daughter the way she is, whatever she does. That is unconditional positive regard. So she has high self-esteem and does not fear failing, which makes her confident to try hard new activities. The common wrong answer just says she is confident because her dad praises her. That does not use the term and ignores why the praise matters. It loses the mark. So classify the regard. Define it. Then give the effect on self-esteem. Every answer in this topic follows those three steps, so practise them until they feel automatic.
