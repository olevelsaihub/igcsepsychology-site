Idea. Someone tells you a phone number. You say it over and over until you write it down. In nineteen sixty-eight, Atkinson and Shiffrin explained this with the Multi-store Model of Memory. A store is a place where information is held. In this model, information moves through three stores. Information first enters the sensory register. It holds the information for a very short time, in the same form as the sense that received it, such as a sight or a sound. If you do not pay attention to it, it is lost. Paying attention moves information from the sensory register into short-term memory, or STM. STM has limited capacity, about seven items, and a short duration, about fifteen to thirty seconds. Rehearsal means repeating information. It keeps information in STM and moves it into long-term memory, or LTM. LTM has an unlimited capacity and can last a lifetime. Retrieval brings information back from LTM to STM. Now evaluate. A strength is support from case studies like HM, and the primacy effect, where early words in a list are remembered. A weakness is that it is too simple. KF's case suggests STM has separate parts, and some things are remembered without rehearsal.

Method. Step one: identify which process the story shows: no attention, rehearsal, chunking or recall from LTM. Step two: state the part of the model that fits. Step three: apply it to the person, saying what they will remember or forget, and why. Learn the clues. No attention means the information is lost from the sensory register. Repeating over and over is rehearsal, which leads to LTM. Grouping items is chunking. Recalling is retrieval from LTM. The trap is saying attention moves information into LTM. It moves it into STM. Rehearsal is what moves it to LTM.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Jack reads three names of football players at a time, and his little brother says them back out loud. They do this for the whole squad, and repeat it every day for a week. We explain what Jack is likely to find, using the model. Two marks. First the clue. His brother says the names out loud, every day, for a week. Repeating information over and over is rehearsal. Step one: state the model. In the Multi-store Model, rehearsal passes information from short-term memory into long-term memory. Step two: predict. Because of the daily rehearsal, Jack is likely to find that his brother can remember most of the squad's names, held in long-term memory. A tempting wrong route is to say he will forget quickly, because STM only lasts seconds. Reject it. The rehearsal moves the names on, out of STM. Now write it out. The model, then the prediction. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying attention moves information into long-term memory. Attention moves it into short-term memory. Rehearsal moves it into long-term memory.

Your turn. Now you try. William talked to his friend while the teacher read out Spanish words, so he did not write them down. He did badly in the test. Explain why, using the Multi-store Model. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. William was talking, so he was not paying attention. In the model, information needs attention to move from the sensory register into STM. So the words were lost, and could not be rehearsed into long-term memory. The common wrong answer says he forgot because he was talking. That does not use the model. It names no store and no process, so it loses the mark. So spot the process, state the part of the model, and apply it to the person.
