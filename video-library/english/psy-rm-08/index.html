Idea. Do students who sleep more get better marks? To find out, you would not change anyone's sleep. You would just measure two things, hours of sleep and test marks, and see if they go together. That is a correlation. A correlation is a method that checks whether two co-variables are related. Co-variables are the two things measured. A scatter diagram plots a correlation. Each point is one participant, with one co-variable on each axis. A positive correlation means as one goes up, the other goes up. A negative correlation means as one goes up, the other goes down. No correlation means no clear pattern. Here is a negative example. The more homework tasks a student misses, the lower their maths test score. The co-variables are tasks missed and test score. As one rises, the other falls. One warning. A correlation does not show cause and effect. Ice cream sales and sunburn rise together. But ice cream does not cause sunburn. Hot weather is a third variable behind both. This type of question asks you to name the direction, read a value, or draw a conclusion from correlation data.

Method. Step one: name the two co-variables. A correlation is always between two measured variables. Step two: look at the direction of the pattern. Up means positive. Down means negative. Step three: describe the relationship using the variable names. Saying only negative may not earn the mark. The trap is saying one variable causes the other. A correlation only shows a relationship. Also do not call co-variables the IV and DV, because nothing is changed.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Savannah asks people how many hours of music they hear in a week, and how happy they are, from one to ten. People who heard under one hour scored two or below. People who heard over twelve hours usually scored nine or above. We explain one conclusion. It is worth two marks. Step one. Name the co-variables. They are hours of music listened to in a week, and happiness score. Both were measured. Savannah changed nothing. Step two. Find the direction. As the hours of music go up, the happiness score goes up. So this is a positive correlation. Step three. Justify with the data. People with under one hour scored two or below, and people with over twelve hours usually scored nine or above. That supports the conclusion. A tempting wrong route is to say music causes happiness. But Savannah only measured two things. Other things, such as free time, could be behind both. So say linked to. Now the exam-ready answer. Savannah can conclude there is a positive relationship between music and happiness. People who listened to under one hour scored two or below, but over twelve hours usually scored nine or above. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying a correlation proves one variable causes the other. A correlation only shows that the two are related.

Your turn. Now you try. Define what is meant by a correlation research method. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. A correlation research method measures two co-variables to see whether they are related. Nothing is changed. Each co-variable is just measured, for example hours of sleep and test marks. A common wrong answer says a correlation shows that one variable causes the other, or that the researcher changes one variable. Both are wrong. A correlation only checks a relationship, and nothing is changed, so that answer loses the mark. So name the co-variables. Find the direction. Describe it in context, and say linked, not caused.
