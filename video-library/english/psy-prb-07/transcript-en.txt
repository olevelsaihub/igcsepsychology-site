Idea. Doctors can treat some problems with medicine. A drug treatment is a medicine that changes how the brain or body works. Both depression and addiction can be treated with drugs, but the drugs are different. For depression, doctors use SSRIs, such as fluoxetine. Brain cells pass messages using chemicals called neurotransmitters. Serotonin is one. It crosses the synapse, the gap between neurons. Normally the sending neuron takes it back, which is called reuptake. SSRIs block reuptake, so more serotonin stays in the synapse to stimulate the next neuron. For addiction there are different drugs. Methadone replaces heroin, to reduce withdrawal. Disulfiram makes a person sick if they drink alcohol. Nicotine replacement reduces cravings when stopping smoking. Strengths: drug trials give research support, tablets are easy to take, and they can ease symptoms quickly enough for other therapy to start. Weaknesses: side effects, relapse if the person stops taking them, they treat symptoms but not the cause, and methadone can itself be addictive. A question gives a person, and asks how drugs could help, or for strengths and weaknesses. Choose a drug that fits, say how it works, and link it to them.

Method. Step one: check if it is depression or addiction, and choose a fitting drug. Step two: state how the drug works, its effect on the brain or body. Step three: link the change to the person's symptoms or situation. The traps are saying SSRIs add serotonin, when they block its reuptake. Using a depression drug for an addiction question. And giving side effects with no example, or two weaknesses that are the same point.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Claire takes a drug recreationally, and now has an addiction. We explain two weaknesses of using drugs to treat her. Four marks. Weakness one: relapse. Claire might stop taking the medication. Then her cravings return, and she could start using again. So the treatment only works if she keeps taking it every day. Weakness two: some addiction drugs, like methadone, are addictive themselves. Claire could become dependent on the treatment drug instead. That brings new health risks, and her addiction is not really solved. A tempting wrong route is to say drugs have side effects, and stop. That is vague. It has no example, and no link to Claire. Reject it. The exam answer. One mark for each weakness and one for each link to Claire. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is saying SSRIs add serotonin. They do not add any. They block reuptake, so the serotonin already there stays in the synapse longer.
