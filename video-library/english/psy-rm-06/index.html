Idea. At the doctor's you may fill in a form with tick boxes. Or the doctor may just ask you questions and talk. Researchers do the same. A questionnaire is a written set of questions. An interview is where the researcher asks the questions face to face or by phone. Both are self-report methods, because people report on themselves. Closed-ended questions have fixed answer options, such as tick boxes or a rating scale. They give quantitative data, which is numbers. Open-ended questions let people answer in their own words. They give qualitative data, which is descriptions in words. Interviews come in three kinds. A structured interview uses the same preset questions in the same order for everyone. A semi-structured interview has some set questions and freedom to ask follow-ups. An unstructured interview is more like a conversation about a topic, with no fixed questions. Self-report has weaknesses. People may give answers that make them look good. This is social desirability bias. They may also misunderstand a question. This type of question asks you to name, define or write a question or interview type, or explain why it suits a study.

Method. Step one: decide which format or question type is described. Fixed options mean closed or structured. Free answers mean open or unstructured. Step two: link it to the data it gives, numbers or words. Step three: apply your point to the topic of the study. For a twelve-mark evaluation, describe the method, apply it to the named study, give strengths and weaknesses, then a justified conclusion. The trap in short questions is a closed question with no answer options. That is not closed.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Jason will use a questionnaire to see if people of different ages use social media differently. We must write one open-ended and one closed-ended question for it. It is worth two marks. Step one. The closed question needs fixed answer options. Keep it on the topic, social media use. Give how often as tick boxes. Step two. The open question lets people answer in their own words. Ask why, or how. For example, why do you use social media? A tempting wrong route is to write the closed question with no options. How often do you use social media? Without tick boxes or a scale, it looks open. So always show the options. Now the exam-ready answer. Open: why do you use social media? Closed: how often do you use social media, with three tick boxes, under one hour, one to three hours, over three hours a day. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is calling open-ended a type of interview. Open and closed describe questions. Structured and unstructured describe interviews.

Your turn. Now you try. Jamie used a questionnaire with open-ended and closed-ended questions about personality and obedience. Describe one reason why Jamie may have used open-ended questions. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Open-ended questions let people answer in their own words. So Jamie would get detailed, qualitative data. For example, people could explain why they obeyed an authority figure. That gives a fuller picture of their personality. A common wrong answer says open-ended questions are quick and easy to compare. That describes closed questions. Open answers are different for each person, so they are harder to compare. That loses the mark. So find the format. Link it to its data. Apply it to the topic.
