Idea. Two babies in the same room. One stays calm when a loud toy goes off. The other cries every time. Before they have had any experiences, they already react differently. Temperament is an inborn, fairly stable style of reacting emotionally, such as being calm or easily upset. It is an internal influence on the self, because it comes from within the person. It affects how we react to events, and so affects self-esteem, which means how much we value ourselves. External influences come from experience and other people. Argyle suggested four: the reactions of others, comparing ourselves with others, social roles, and identifying with role models. Praise tends to raise self-esteem. Criticism, and unfavourable comparison with others, tend to lower it. A question describes someone and asks how internal or external influences shape their self. You pick the influence, define it, and show the effect.

Method. Step one: decide whether the influence is internal, which means temperament, or external, which means other people's reactions or comparison. Step two: define it. Step three: show how it raises or lowers this person's self-esteem or self-image. Self-esteem means how much they value themselves. The trap is using the same scenario detail for both of two ways.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Daniel works hard but gets low maths scores. His teacher criticises him, and he compares himself with his twin Zoe, who scores high. We must explain two ways external influences account for his low scores. Way one: the teacher's criticism. This is the reaction of others. Argyle said other people's reactions shape self-esteem. Criticism lowers Daniel's confidence, so he is less able to improve. Way two: his twin Zoe. This is comparing ourselves with others. Comparing with someone who does better can leave a poor self-image. Self-image means how you see yourself. Daniel believes he will never be good at maths, and this keeps his scores low. A tempting wrong route is to say Daniel was born with a low maths temperament. Reject it. Temperament is internal, and the question asks about external influences. Now write it out, one way at a time. Each way earns one mark for the point and one for the justification. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to list an influence without saying its effect on self-esteem. Naming the teacher or Zoe is not enough. You must say what it does to Daniel.

Your turn. Now you try. Natalie is cheerful, confident and positive about herself. Explain how her temperament may have influenced her self-esteem. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Temperament is an inborn emotional style. Natalie's is happy and cheerful. So she reacts to events positively and sees the best in herself and others. This helps her to feel confident and proud, which gives her high self-esteem. The common wrong answer says Natalie learned to be cheerful from her friends. That is an external influence, not temperament. Temperament is inborn, so the answer loses the mark. So decide internal or external. Define the influence. Show its effect on this person. If the question asks for two ways, use a different detail for each.
