Idea. You walk into a classroom and see a chair. You know at once that you can sit on it. You did not stop to think or remember anything. This is Gibson's direct theory of perception. Perception means how we make sense of what we see. Gibson said it is bottom-up, which means it starts from the light reaching the eyes. That light is the optic array. It has enough information, so we do not need past knowledge. Optic flow is the pattern of movement we see as we move. Near things rush past fast and far things drift slowly. An invariant is something that stays the same as we move, such as texture gradient. An affordance is what an object lets us do. In Gibson and Walk's visual cliff study of nineteen sixty, babies would not crawl onto the deep side. That suggests depth perception is partly inborn. But the theory cannot explain illusions or expectations, and it ignores emotion. Questions give you a person in a scene. You explain a Gibson idea for them, or you assess how well the theory fits.

Method. Step one: explain the Gibson idea, such as optic flow. Step two: apply it to a detail from the scenario. Step three, for assess questions: give a strength and a weakness, then a judgement. The trap is mixing Gibson up with Gregory, whose theory uses past knowledge. A nine mark assess question tests three skills equally. Aim for three points each of knowledge, application and evaluation. Then finish with a clear judgement.

Worked example. Here is a real two mark question. Malik is in a flight simulator. He can tell his direction and speed without moving. The objects in the video move at different speeds. We explain why, using optic flow. First, what optic flow is. When we move, near objects stream past us fast. Distant objects drift past slowly. The point we move towards stays still. The programmers make near objects move quickly and distant ones slowly. So Malik gets the same information as in real flight. The different speeds tell him distance, and the flow tells him his speed and direction. A tempting wrong route says Malik remembers what flying looks like. But that is past knowledge, which is Gregory's theory. The question says optic flow, which is Gibson's. Optic flow means near objects move past quickly and distant ones slowly. Programming the objects like this gives Malik the same flow as real flight. So he can judge his speed and direction. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The classic slip is getting optic flow backwards. Near things move faster, not slower. A fence beside the road flashes past, while far hills barely move.

Your turn. Now try a nine mark question. Kim walks through a park and sees a river and horses far away. Two people in sports clothes run towards her, but she is not worried. Assess Gibson's theory. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Describe: Gibson says the optic array holds enough information, so perception is direct. Apply: the river and horses look small and lack detail, so texture gradient tells Kim they are far away. As Kim walks, the path streams past her, which is optic flow. The runners grow larger as they come closer. Evaluate: the visual cliff study supports direct depth perception. But Kim is calm because the sports clothes suggest joggers. That needs past knowledge, which Gibson ignores. Judge: Gibson explains distance and movement well, but not why Kim was not worried. The common wrong answer only describes Gibson's theory. It never uses Kim's park, so it cannot reach the top level. So explain the idea. Apply it to the scene. Then judge how well it fits.
