Idea. Hold up your hands. Your brain has two halves too, called hemispheres, left and right. Lateralisation means the two hemispheres are specialised for different jobs, so they are not identical. The left hemisphere is mainly for language, which means speaking, reading and writing. It controls the right side of the body. The right hemisphere is mainly for spatial tasks, such as judging space and shapes, and for recognising faces and emotions. It controls the left side of the body. The corpus callosum is a thick bundle of nerve fibres that passes information between the two hemispheres. If it is damaged, the halves cannot share information easily. Some research suggests males are more lateralised, and females use both hemispheres more evenly. This may relate to males doing better on some spatial tasks, and females on some verbal tasks. Rilea and colleagues, in two thousand and five, found differences on some spatial tasks, but not on others. So the findings are mixed, and upbringing may matter too. A question names a hemisphere, or a damaged corpus callosum, or a task. You state the role, then predict the effect on the person.

Method. Step one: identify the hemisphere or structure involved. Step two: state its function, language on the left, spatial on the right, corpus callosum connects them. Step three: predict or explain the effect on the person. For sex differences, use research and say results are mixed. The traps are swapping left and right, forgetting that each side controls the opposite side of the body, stating sex differences as fact, and describing where the corpus callosum is instead of what it does.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Josh gives one female and one male a map reading task. They must find the quickest route from a castle to a cake shop. We explain one likely outcome, using research on sex differences. Two marks. Step one: recall the research. Rilea and colleagues found sex differences in lateralisation, and that males did better on some spatial tasks. Step two: apply it. Map reading is a spatial task. So the male is likely to plan a faster route, and reach the cake shop first. A tempting wrong route is to say the male wins because boys are cleverer. That is a claim with no research. Another wrong route is to say it will happen for certain. Reject both. The exam answer. Research, then the task. One mark for accurate research. One mark for applying it to the map task. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is swapping the hemispheres. Language is mainly on the left. Spatial tasks are mainly on the right. And each hemisphere controls the opposite side of the body.

Your turn. Now you try. Explain one problem Antoine may have. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. A stroke is when the blood supply to part of the brain is cut off, so that part is damaged. State the role. The left hemisphere is mainly responsible for language. Then give the problem. Antoine may find it hard to speak, read or write. A common wrong answer says Antoine may struggle with spatial tasks, like reading a map. That is the right hemisphere. It loses both marks. So identify the hemisphere, state its role, and predict the effect.
