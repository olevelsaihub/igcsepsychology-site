Idea. Why does your face go the same way as a baby's when you taste something horrible? Nobody taught either of you. Darwin thought the same about many expressions. In eighteen seventy-two, Darwin argued that expressions in humans and animals are innate, which means born with, and inherited, which means passed on from parents. They are not learned. Why did they evolve? Through natural selection. Animals that signalled well, with warnings or threats, were more likely to survive and have young. So they passed the signals on. Some expressions once had a practical job. This is a serviceable habit. Baring teeth was getting ready to bite. Now it works as a warning. Narrowed eyes focus on a threat. Wide eyes in fear take in more of the surroundings. Many expressions, like anger, fear and disgust, look similar across cultures and in other primates. That supports evolution. A question gives a scene and asks you to explain signals, using Darwin.

Method. Step one: pick a specific signal from the stem. Step two: say what job it does, like a warning, a threat, or focusing. Step three: link it to Darwin: innate, inherited, and helped survival. For each way, one mark is the signal and its purpose. One mark is the Darwin justification. The traps are saying expressions are learned, and using the same Darwin sentence twice.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. George and Edward argue about sport. George narrows his eyes without thinking, and later bares his teeth. We must explain two ways Darwin's theory can account for the non-verbal communication. Four marks. First choose two signals. George narrowed his eyes. George bared his teeth. Each one needs its job. Way one: narrowed eyes cut out the edges of vision, so George can concentrate. The stem says he did it instinctively. Darwin said such reactions are inherited, not learned. Way two: bared teeth. This warns Edward that George is ready to fight. Darwin called it a serviceable habit. Animals bare teeth before they bite. Those that warned rivals off survived. A tempting wrong route is to say George learned these faces by watching other people argue. Reject it. Darwin said expressions are inborn. The stem even says instinctively. The exam answer. Each way is the signal and its job, then Darwin. Each way earns one mark for the signal and its job, and one for the Darwin link. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is describing the behaviour without its purpose. Saying George bared his teeth earns little. Say it warned Edward off, and those who warned rivals off survived and passed that on.

That is natural selection, as Darwin said. A common wrong answer says expressions are learned from parents. That is the opposite of Darwin. He said they are innate and inherited. So, pick a signal.
