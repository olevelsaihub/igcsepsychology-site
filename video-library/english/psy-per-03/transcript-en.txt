Idea. Picture a friend walking away from you down a long corridor. The picture of your friend at the back of your eye gets smaller and smaller. But you do not think your friend is shrinking. You still see a normal sized person. This is visual constancy. Visual constancy means we see an object as staying the same size, shape or colour, even when the image in our eye changes. The image at the back of the eye is called the retinal image. Size constancy: a person walking away does not seem to shrink. Shape constancy: an opening door is still a rectangle. Colour constancy: a red apple looks red in dim or bright light. Do not mix it up with a visual illusion. A visual illusion is when what we see does not match the real object, so we are misled. An ambiguous figure can be seen two ways, one at a time. A distortion makes a shape look the wrong size. A fiction shows something that is not there. The question asks you to state, describe or explain one of these ideas. You say what it is, and then how it works.

Method. Step one: name the idea and say what it means. Step two: say how it works. For colour, the light changes but the brain allows for it. Step three: add a short example. The trap is forgetting the changing light. Keep constancy and illusion apart. In constancy the image in the eye changes, but we see the object correctly. In an illusion the object is seen wrongly.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question, worth two marks. It asks you to describe how visual constancies let people see an object as the same colour, even in different levels of light. Think of a banana. In sunshine, bright light bounces off it into your eye. In a dim room, much less light does. The image in your eye is different. Yet the banana still looks yellow. Mark one is for saying what it is. Visual constancy means an object's colour looks the same, even though the light reaching the eye changes. Mark two is for how it works. The brain allows for the lighting. It uses what it already knows about the real colour of the object, which is called past experience. A tempting wrong route is to say we just see the light that reaches the eye. If that were true, a banana would look brown in a dim room. It does not, because the brain allows for the light. Now the exam answer. Visual constancy means an object's colour looks the same even when the light changes. The brain allows for the lighting, using past experience. So a banana looks yellow in sun or dim light. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. Many students call constancy a trick. But a trick is an illusion. Constancy helps us see things correctly.
