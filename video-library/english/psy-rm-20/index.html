Idea. A bar chart shows separate categories, such as English or Sociology students. The bars have gaps between them. A histogram shows continuous data, such as time in minutes. Its bars touch. Every axis needs a label with units, and an even scale. A scatter diagram plots two measures for each person. Each person is one point. Time spent on homework goes on one axis, and test score on the other. A frequency table counts how often each category happens. It has a column for the categories, a tally column, and a total column. A distribution is the shape of the scores. In a normal distribution, the shape is a symmetrical bell, and the mean, median and mode are equal. In positive skew, most scores are low, with a tail to the right. In negative skew, most scores are high, with a tail to the left. This question type asks you to complete or draw a table or chart, or to name a distribution. Marks come for labels, a good scale, and accurate data.

Method. Step one: label the axis you are asked to complete, with units. Step two: choose an even scale that fits the biggest value. Step three: plot each bar or point exactly from the table. For distributions, look at where most scores bunch and where the tail points. The trap is an axis with numbers but no label. The label says what the numbers count.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Ricky tests whether English and Sociology students obey an instruction to complete their work. The paper prints a bar chart for us to complete. We must add the Sociology students' data, with a label and scale on the y-axis. It is worth three marks. Step one. Label and scale the y-axis. The y-axis counts students, so label it Number of students. The biggest value is sixteen, so use an even scale from nought to twenty, in steps of five. Step two. Draw the first Sociology bar. Fifteen students completed the work, so draw that bar up to fifteen. Step three. Draw the second Sociology bar. Ten students did not complete the work, so draw that bar up to ten. A tempting wrong route is an uneven scale, such as nought, five, ten, twenty, thirty. The gaps are different sizes, so the bars give a false picture. The scale must rise in equal steps. Now the exam-ready answer. Label the y-axis, Number of students. Use an even scale from nought to twenty in fives. Draw the Sociology bars to fifteen and ten. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is plotting a point on a scatter diagram the wrong way round. Check which measure goes across, on the x-axis, and which goes up, on the y-axis. Mixing them puts the point in the wrong place.

Your turn. Now you try. Kamira watches shoppers who find a lost item. She records what each passer-by does. Draw a frequency table that she could use. Include appropriate column and row headings. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The rows are the three behaviours Kamira recorded. The columns are the behaviour, a tally, and the total. Each passer-by gets a tally mark in the right row. A common wrong answer is a table with no headings, or with only one column. Another is a table with numbers but no names for the behaviours. The question says you must include column and row headings, so those marks are lost. So label the axis or the headings. Choose an even scale. Plot or record exactly.
