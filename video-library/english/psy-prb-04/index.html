Idea. You learned to talk, and to ride a bike, from the world around you. Learning theory says addiction is learned too, not inherited. It says we learn by watching other people, by rewards, and by links between things. Nurture means influences from the environment. Social learning means we watch role models, people we look up to, like parents, friends and celebrities. Then we imitate them. We copy even more if we see them rewarded. Operant conditioning means learning from the results of what we do. Positive reinforcement is a reward, like a high, so we repeat it. Negative reinforcement is when use removes something unpleasant, like stress or withdrawal, so we repeat it. Classical conditioning means learning by association. Places, people or objects get linked with the substance, and then trigger cravings by themselves. For strength, Bandura and colleagues, in nineteen sixty-one, showed children copy an adult's aggression towards a Bobo doll, an inflatable toy. For weakness, learning theory ignores biology, and not everyone who sees a model becomes addicted. Questions give a person and ask you to use these ideas.

Method. Step one: pick the learning idea that fits the scenario. Is it modelling, reward or association? Step two: name the model or the reward in the scenario. Step three: for evaluation, use Bandura as support and biology as a limit, each justified for the person. The traps are describing the Bobo doll without linking it to addiction, confusing positive and negative reinforcement, and saying nurture without explaining the learning.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Jeremie watched his father enjoy online auctions, and now has an addiction to them. We explain one strength and one weakness of using learning theory. Four marks. Strength: Bandura showed children copy adult role models. That supports the idea that Jeremie watched his father enjoy winning, and copied him once he was old enough. Weakness: the theory leaves out biology, such as genes or dopamine. Jeremie might have inherited a vulnerability from his father, which could explain his addiction as well as imitation. A tempting wrong route is to describe the Bobo doll in detail, and stop. That is just description. You must link the study to Jeremie. The exam answer. One mark for each point and one for each link. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is mixing up positive and negative reinforcement. Negative does not mean punishment. It means something unpleasant is taken away, like withdrawal.

Your turn. Now you try. Explain how learning theory could account for Aliyah's addiction. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. You can pick social learning. Aliyah's parents and sister use their phones all the time. They are her role models, so she copied them. Or pick reinforcement. She feels happy when messages arrive. That is positive reinforcement, so she keeps checking. One idea, linked to Aliyah. A common wrong answer says Aliyah is addicted because of nurture. That names the side, but not how she learned it. It loses the marks. So pick the learning idea, name the model or reward, and link it to the person.
