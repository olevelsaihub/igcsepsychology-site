Idea. Could you concentrate on a test if you were freezing cold, starving and had not slept? Probably not. First you need your basic needs met. Maslow built a theory of motivation on this idea. Motivation means what drives us to act. Maslow, nineteen forty-three, arranged human needs in a hierarchy, usually drawn as a pyramid. Starting from the bottom: physiological needs, safety, love and belonging, esteem, and self-actualisation. The rule is that lower needs must mostly be met before a person is motivated by higher ones. If your basic needs are not met, you cannot focus on the levels above. The bottom four are deficiency needs, which means they are lacking and push us to fix them. Self-actualisation is a growth need. It means becoming everything you are capable of being. A question gives a person with a problem and asks which need is unmet and how that changes their behaviour. Some also ask for a strength or a weakness of the theory.

Method. Step one: find which level is not being met. Food, water, warmth and sleep are physiological. Step two: state Maslow's rule that lower needs come before higher ones. Step three: link the unmet need to the person's changed behaviour. The trap is getting the order wrong, or naming a need without the rule.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Raymond's work has got worse. His heating broke, his house is cold, and he cannot sleep. We must explain why, using Maslow's hierarchy of needs. Step one: find the unmet need. Raymond is cold and cannot sleep. Warmth and sleep are physiological needs, the base of the hierarchy. Step two: state the rule. Maslow said basic needs must be met before a person can focus on anything higher. So Raymond is too tired and cold to focus on higher needs like work. A tempting wrong route is to say Raymond has an esteem problem because his work is poor. Reject it. The work is the result. The unmet need is the base level. Now write it out. Rule, then application. One mark for the rule. One mark for applying it. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to name a need but not give the rule. Just saying his physiological needs are not met earns little. You must say that lower needs must be met first.

Your turn. Now you try. Phoebe is divorced but still friends with her ex-husband, owns a successful business and is happy. Explain one reason why she has not yet reached self-actualisation, using Maslow. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Phoebe has recently divorced, so her love and belonging need may not be fully met. Maslow said lower needs must be met before self-actualisation. So Phoebe has yet to become everything she could be, such as giving more time to her charity. Both parts of the answer matter: the unmet level, and the rule. The common wrong answer says she has not reached self-actualisation because her business is not successful enough. But the story says it is successful. Use the details given, not an invented problem. So find the unmet level. State the rule. Link it to the person.
