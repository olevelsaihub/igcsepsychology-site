Idea. Imagine you stop being late for school because your phone was taken away for a week. What happens after a behaviour changes what you do next time. This is operant conditioning. Skinner said behaviour is shaped by its consequences. Reinforcement makes a behaviour more likely. Punishment makes it less likely. Now two small words. Positive means something is added. Negative means something is taken away. They do not mean good and bad. Reinforcement can be either kind, and so can punishment. In crime, stolen goods are a reward added, so theft is repeated. A fine is a penalty added. Losing a driving licence is a reward removed. One more pair. A primary reinforcer meets a basic need directly, like food, warmth or sleep. A secondary reinforcer, like money or a token, has no value on its own, but it can be swapped for a primary one. A question will describe a person and a consequence. It asks you to name the term, or explain how it changes what they do next.

Method. Step one: is something added or removed, pleasant or unpleasant? That decides the term. Step two: define it in one sentence. The trap is thinking positive means good. Step three: use a detail about the named person. Step four: say what happens to the behaviour in future. The definition earns one mark, and the application earns the other.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Tamara was caught speeding, went on a course, and was caught speeding again. We must explain how negative punishment could stop her. It is only two marks. First, take the term apart. Negative means something is taken away. Punishment means the behaviour becomes less likely. Next, ask what Tamara values. She values driving. So we take away her right to drive for a while. A tempting wrong answer is a fine, or another course. That adds a penalty, so it is positive punishment. Reject it. The key word is removed. Now write the exam answer. Define the term. Apply it to Tamara. Say the effect. One mark is for the definition. One mark is for applying it to Tamara. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is to think positive means good. Students say a fine is negative because it feels bad. But a fine is added, so it is positive punishment.

Your turn. Now you try. Niall broke a window and scratched a car. His parents took away his games console. Explain one way this might change his behaviour, using operant conditioning. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The console is taken away, so this is negative punishment: something Niall likes is removed. Because he loses something he values, he is less likely to throw stones or scratch cars again. That final sentence about the future is what makes the answer complete. The most common wrong answer says the console is a reward, or that this is negative reinforcement. But a console is not given. It is removed to stop bad behaviour, so it is punishment. So, decide added or removed. Define the term. Apply it to the named person. Then say what happens to their behaviour next.
