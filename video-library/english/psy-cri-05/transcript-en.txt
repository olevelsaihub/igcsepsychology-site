Idea. Think of a game that gives you points every time you finish a level. You can spend the points on a new costume. That is how some prisons try to change behaviour. A token economy uses operant conditioning, which means learning from rewards and punishments. Pro-social behaviour, which means helpful and positive behaviour, earns a token straight away. Tokens are secondary reinforcers. A reinforcer is anything that makes a behaviour more likely. Tokens have no value alone, but can be swapped for things people want, like food, privileges or visits. It works best when rules are clear, rewards are wanted, and tokens are given fairly and every time. It may fail if staff are unfair, rewards come late, or the behaviour stops when the tokens stop after release. Anger management is different. It is a type of cognitive behavioural therapy, which changes how people think and act. It teaches people to spot what triggers their anger and use calming skills such as breathing or counting. A question names a programme and a situation. You explain how it works, apply it, and judge if it would help.

Method. Step one: explain how the programme works in principle. Step two: use the details of the scenario. What is rewarded, what do tokens buy, and who is involved? Step three: judge success. Is the reward wanted, is it consistent, and does it last? Step four: end with a conclusion if the question says assess.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Jacob gives prisoners ten credit points for each day of pro-social behaviour. At one hundred points they can swap them for gym time or extra food. We must give one strength and one weakness. For the strength, look at how it was set up. It was agreed with the prisoners, and the points buy things they want, like gym time. Points are given every day, which is consistent. So pro-social behaviour is rewarded again and again, and it should be repeated. For the weakness, look at the numbers. A hundred points at ten a day takes ten days. That reward is delayed. Also, once prisoners leave, the tokens stop, so the behaviour may stop. A tempting wrong route is to call the tokens primary reinforcers. Reject it. A token has no value alone. It is a secondary reinforcer that can be swapped. Now write it out. Strength with a reason. Weakness with a reason. Two points and two reasons make four marks. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to explain operant conditioning in general, and never describe the programme. Always say what is rewarded and what the tokens buy.

Your turn. Now you try. Sadie has been in fights and must attend an anger management programme. Explain one strength of it for Sadie. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Anger management teaches people to recognise what triggers their anger and to use calming skills such as breathing or counting. Sadie's fights suggest anger is her problem, so over seven weeks she can learn to stay calm instead of lashing out. The common wrong answer says anger management punishes Sadie, so she will fear fighting. It does not punish. It teaches skills. So the answer describes the wrong idea and loses the mark.
