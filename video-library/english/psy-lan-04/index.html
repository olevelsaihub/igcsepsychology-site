Idea. You know your mum is cross before she says a word. Her face and body tell you. Non-verbal communication, or NVC, means sending messages without spoken or written words. There are four types to learn. Facial expressions. Eye contact. Posture. And gestures. The last two together are called body language. Facial expressions use the face, the mouth, eyes and eyebrows, to show emotion, like smiling or frowning. Eye contact is meeting another person's gaze. It can show interest, while looking away can show sadness or discomfort. Posture is how the body is held, such as open or crossed arms. Gestures are movements of the hands or body that carry meaning, such as pointing. Do not mix these two up. Signals can clash with words. When they do, people often trust the signal. A question describes behaviour, and asks you to name the type and say what emotion it shows.

Method. Step one: name the type of non-verbal communication. Step two: define it simply. Step three: quote the behaviour from the stem. Step four: say what emotion it shows. The definition earns one mark. Applying it to the behaviour earns the other. The traps are confusing posture with gestures, and giving a verbal example, like shouting.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Jamie sees his mother through the kitchen window. After a phone call she puts her hands on her head, points at his father and folds her arms. We must explain why Jamie thought she was annoyed, using body language. Two marks. Step one: define body language. It means using movements and the way we hold our body to show feelings, without words. Step two: pick out her actions. Hands on her head and pointing are gestures. Crossed arms are posture. Step three: say the emotion. These are closed, negative signals. They usually show frustration, so Jamie read her as annoyed. A tempting wrong route is to say Jamie heard her shouting. Reject it. He watched through a window, and shouting is verbal. The question asks for body language. The exam answer. Define. Quote her actions. Say the emotion. One mark for explaining body language. One mark for applying it to her actions. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is confusing posture with gestures. Students say posture is waving or pointing. That is a gesture. Posture is how the body is held.

Your turn. Now you try. Andrea gave Tim a present. His mouth turned down and he looked at the floor, but he said he liked it. Explain one reason Andrea did not believe him, using a concept. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Tim's mouth turned downwards. That is a facial expression, using the face to show emotion. It shows disappointment. His words said he liked the present, but his face said the opposite. People often trust the non-verbal signal, so Andrea did not believe him. A common wrong answer says Tim looked unhappy, so Andrea did not believe him. That names no concept. Always name the type, here facial expression, and say what emotion it shows. So, name the type. Define it. Quote the behaviour. Say the emotion.
