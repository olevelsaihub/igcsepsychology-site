Idea. Break a school rule and you might get a detention, a litter-picking job, or a meeting with the person you upset. Courts have choices like these too. They are all ways of dealing with crime. The big question is reoffending. Recidivism means a person offending again after being punished. Every punishment is judged by whether it reduces it. Prison is custodial. The offender loses their freedom and is removed from society. It is retributive, which means the punishment fits the crime. It protects the public and may deter people, but reoffending is high, and prisoners may learn more crime from each other. A community sentence is non-custodial. The offender stays in the community and does unpaid work or follows rules such as a curfew. They keep their job and family, but some people see it as a soft option. Restorative justice brings the victim and the offender together. The offender sees the harm they caused and tries to make amends. It can build empathy, but both sides must agree.

Method. Step one: name the punishment and define it. Step two: apply it to the offender. What exactly would they do or lose? The trap is mixing up community sentencing and restorative justice. Step three: link every strength or weakness to reoffending, because that is how these punishments are judged. In long answers, finish with a judgement.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Calvin caused a crash, was banned from driving, then crashed again while drinking and driving without a licence. He got six months in prison. We must give one strength and one weakness of prison for Calvin. For the strength, think about protection. While Calvin is locked up, he cannot get behind a wheel, so other road users are safe. Losing his freedom may also show him how serious his offence is. For the weakness, think about the cause. Calvin's problem is drinking. Prison punishes him but does not treat it. When he is released, he may drink and drive again. A tempting wrong route is to say prison will cure his drinking. Reject it. Prison is a punishment, not a treatment. Now write it out. Strength with a reason. Weakness with a reason. Two points and two reasons make four marks. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to mix up community sentencing and restorative justice. A community sentence is unpaid work. Restorative justice is meeting the victim.

Your turn. Now you try. Liam smashed windows and sprayed graffiti at a community centre. He has a community sentence. Explain how it could be used with Liam. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. A community sentence is a punishment served in the community, not in prison. Liam could be made to do unpaid work, such as cleaning off the graffiti and repairing the windows he damaged. He pays back the community and stays in his home area. The common wrong answer says Liam would meet the centre staff to say sorry. That is restorative justice, not a community sentence. It names the wrong punishment, so it loses the mark. So name the punishment. Define it. Then say what the offender would do.
