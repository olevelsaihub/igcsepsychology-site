Idea. Someone drops all their books in the school corridor. Do you stop to help? A bystander is a person who sees something happening, but is not directly involved. Bystander intervention means that a witness chooses to help. Helping like this is pro-social behaviour, which means positive behaviour that benefits others. Sometimes people fail to help because others are present. That is the bystander effect. Latané and Darley, in nineteen sixty-eight, explained it as diffusion of responsibility. The more people around, the less responsible each person feels, because they assume someone else will act. Factors fall into two kinds. A situational factor is about the situation around the person. A personal factor is about the person themselves. Always check which kind the question asks for. Cost of helping means people help less when it seems dangerous, embarrassing or time-consuming. Ambiguity means it is unclear if it is an emergency, so people wait to see what others do. Competence means having the skill to help. A question gives a scene and asks for one factor, linked to it.

Method. Step one: check whether the question asks for a personal or a situational factor. Step two: name the factor and say in one sentence how it affects helping. Step three: point to a detail in the scene that shows the factor at work. The traps are giving the wrong kind of factor, and naming a factor without explaining how it works.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Keira sees a younger boy being hurt at school, and walks past. We explain one situational factor that could account for this. Two marks. Step one: the detail. Lots of other students saw it too, and nobody did anything. That points to diffusion of responsibility. Step two: explain the factor, then link it. When many people see an incident, each person feels less personally responsible. Keira may have assumed that one of the other students, or a teacher, would deal with it. A tempting wrong route is to say Keira is shy. That is a personal factor, and the scene never says it. The question asks for a situational one. The exam answer. One mark for explaining the factor and one for linking it to the pupils watching Keira. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to name a factor without explaining how it works. Just writing, diffusion of responsibility, is only a label. Say what it does to the person.

Your turn. Now you try. Kareem sees an older woman fall down the stairs of a bus. Explain one personal factor that could affect whether Kareem helps. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. A personal factor is competence. If Kareem has first aid skills, he feels able to help, so he is more likely to. If he is a student with no training, he may be unsure what to do, so he might not help. Either way, we link the factor to Kareem. A common wrong answer is, lots of people were around. That is a situational factor, about the place. The question asks for a personal factor, so it earns nothing. So check the kind of factor, explain how it works, and link it to the scene.
