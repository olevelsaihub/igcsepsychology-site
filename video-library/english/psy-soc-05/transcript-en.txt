Idea. Think about a school uniform. Put one on, and do you act a little differently? Haney, Banks and Zimbardo, in nineteen seventy-three, tested this in the Stanford prison study. The aim was to see if brutality in prisons comes from the people, or from the prison situation itself. About seventy-five men answered a newspaper advert. Twenty-four healthy male college students were chosen. They were randomly made guards or prisoners. The mock prison was in a basement at Stanford University. Prisoners were arrested at home by real police. Guards wore khaki military-style uniforms and mirrored sunglasses, and carried whistles and batons. Prisoners wore a numbered smock, a stocking cap, and a chain on one ankle. Rules included three meals a day, three supervised toilet visits, and three roll-call line-ups every day. It was planned for two weeks, but stopped after six days. Guards became cruel, and several prisoners showed severe distress. The conclusion: the situation and the roles changed behaviour, helped by deindividuation. That means losing your personal identity through uniforms and anonymity. Questions ask you to use this finding.

Method. Step one: for recall questions, learn the numbers. Seventy-five answered, twenty-four were chosen, two weeks planned, six days actual. Step two: for scenario questions, recall the finding about roles, uniforms and deindividuation. Step three: apply it to the people in the question. The traps are saying the study lasted two weeks, and describing the study without applying it.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Casey records staff making sales calls, first in smart suits, then in casual clothes. We explain what Casey will find, using Haney, Banks and Zimbardo. Two marks. Step one: recall the finding. Ordinary students given guard or prisoner uniforms soon acted like their roles. Guards became bossy and aggressive, and prisoners became passive. Uniforms and roles can override personal identity. Step two: notice the contrast. Smart suits are the uniform of a sales professional. Casual clothes are not. So in suits, the staff should sound formal and businesslike. In casual clothes, they should sound more relaxed and chatty. A tempting wrong route is to describe the study, twenty-four students and six days, and stop. That is knowledge only. Use the finding to predict Casey's results. The exam answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to say the study lasted two weeks. Two weeks was the plan. It was stopped after six days, because the guards were cruel.

Your turn. Now you try. Junbin and friends wear masks at the park, then go without masks to a shopping centre. Explain why they may be less aggressive at the shopping centre. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. In the study, uniforms and sunglasses hid identity, which led to deindividuation, so people acted differently. At the park, the masks and hoods hid Junbin's group, so they lost personal identity and acted aggressively. At the shopping centre, without masks and with CCTV, they can be identified, so their own identity returns. A common wrong answer is, they were scared of being caught. That is common sense, not the study. It never mentions deindividuation, so it loses the marks. So recall the finding, name deindividuation, and apply it to the people.
