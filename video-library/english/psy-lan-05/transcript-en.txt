Idea. You sit on an empty bus. A stranger sits right beside you. It feels odd, although nobody spoke. We all keep an invisible bubble around ourselves. That bubble is personal space. It is the comfortable area of distance people keep around themselves. Proxemics, from Hall in nineteen sixty-six, is the study of how people use space when communicating. Hall described zones. Intimate distance is up to about forty-six centimetres, for partners and family. Personal distance is forty-six centimetres to one point two metres, for friends. Social distance is one point two to three point seven metres, for strangers and acquaintances. Public distance is beyond that. The closer the relationship, the smaller the distance we allow. And comfortable space varies between cultures. A question describes people moving closer or keeping away. It asks you to explain, using proxemics.

Method. Step one: state the idea of proxemics, with an approximate distance if you can. Step two: name the relationship between the people. Step three: explain the behaviour using that distance. One mark is for the proxemics idea. One mark is for applying it to the person. The traps are mixing up the distances, and not mentioning the relationship at all.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Mark is at a welcome evening on his first day at university. At first he is nervous and stands far from the others. By the end he stands close to several students. We must explain how proxemics may have influenced Mark. Two marks. Step one: compare the start with the end. At the start they were strangers, and he kept far away. By the end they were new friends, and he stood close. Step two: the idea. Proxemics says the space we need gets smaller as we get to know people. Step three: link it. Strangers get social distance, more than about one point two metres. Friends get personal distance. So Mark moved in as friendships formed. A tempting wrong route is to say only that Mark felt nervous, then braver. Reject it. That describes feelings, not space. The question asks about proxemics, so use distance and relationship. The exam answer. State proxemics. Then link it to Mark's two distances. One mark for the proxemics idea. One mark for applying it to Mark. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing up the distances. Students say friends stand at social distance. Social distance is for strangers. Friends use personal distance.

Your turn. Now you try. Kendra arrives at a bus stop. Two people sit at either end of a bench, with a small gap in the middle. She stays standing. Explain why, referring to proxemics. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Proxemics says we keep a comfortable distance that depends on the relationship. The two people are strangers, so Kendra would want to keep a social distance from them. Sitting in the small gap would put her much closer than that, inside the personal space of two strangers. That would feel uncomfortable, so she stood. A common wrong answer says Kendra was shy, or did not want to sit. That is only a feeling. There is no link to space or to the relationship, so the mark is lost. So, state the idea. Name the relationship. Explain the behaviour using distance.
