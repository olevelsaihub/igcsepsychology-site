Perceptual set: why we see what we expect

Idea. When you are very hungry on the way home from school, you notice every food shop and every smell. When you are not hungry, you hardly see them. The shops did not change. You did. This is perceptual set. Perceptual set is a readiness to perceive things in a certain way. It was named by Allport in nineteen fifty-five. Our mind is ready, or set, to notice some things more than others. There are four influences. Motivation means a need, such as hunger. Expectation means what we think will happen from the situation. Emotion means feelings, such as fear. Culture means the way of life and experiences of the group we grow up in. Here is research. Gilchrist and Nesberg, nineteen fifty-two, found hungry people rated food pictures as brighter. Bruner and Minturn, nineteen fifty-five, showed that the same symbol is read as a letter among letters and a number among numbers. Hudson, nineteen sixty, studied culture and depth in pictures. The question gives a person who perceived something. You say which influence was at work, then justify it with perceptual set.

Method. Step one: spot the influence and what the person perceived. Step two: justify with the concept. Say how that influence makes the mind ready to notice or read things a certain way. Name research if you can. The trap is the wrong influence, or only retelling the story. Use clue words. Hungry or thirsty means motivation. Frightened or happy means emotion. Context means expectation. Culture means the group's way of life.

Worked example. Here is a real two mark question. Students did not eat before arriving. Group 1 waited. Group 2 ate breakfast. Shown pictures with no food, Group 1 named more food-related images. We explain why, using influences on perceptual set. Look at the difference between the groups. Group 1 had not eaten, so they were hungry. Group 2 had just had breakfast. Mark one is the reason in context. Group 1 were hungry, so they were motivated to look for food. Food-like shapes stood out to them. Mark two is the justification. Motivation is an influence on perceptual set. A need such as hunger sets us to notice things linked to that need. Gilchrist and Nesberg found hungry people saw food pictures as brighter. A tempting wrong route is to say Group 1 saw food because food was shown. But the question says no food images were shown. Here is the answer. Group 1 were hungry, so motivated to find food. Motivation is an influence on perceptual set, because a need makes related things stand out. So they named more food-related images. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The classic slip is naming the wrong influence. Hunger is a bodily need, so it is motivation. It is not an emotion. The wrong label loses the justification mark.

Your turn. Now you try. Mark is afraid of the dark. He sees someone by the window and cries. It is really a coat. Explain one reason why his perception was inaccurate, using a concept. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Mark was frightened of the dark, so the influence is emotion. Emotion is an influence on perceptual set. Fear makes the mind ready to see threats, so a harmless coat looked like a person. That is why his perception was inaccurate. The common wrong answer says it was dark, so he could not see properly. That only retells the story. It does not use a concept, so the justification mark is lost. So spot the influence. Then justify with perceptual set.
