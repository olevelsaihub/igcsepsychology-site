Idea. Imagine someone collapses on a packed train. Do passengers help? Piliavin and colleagues, in nineteen sixty-nine, tested this. It was a field experiment, which means a study in a real-life setting where the researcher changes the variable. The setting was a New York subway train. Teams of four took part. A victim collapsed. A model would help if nobody else did. Two observers recorded what happened. The victim was male, and there were two types. The cane victim seemed ill but sober. The drunk victim smelled of alcohol and carried a bottle in a paper bag. Here are the results. The cane victim got spontaneous help on sixty-two of sixty-five trials. The drunk victim got help on nineteen of thirty-eight trials. That is about ninety-five per cent, against exactly fifty per cent. The model stepped in after about seventy seconds in the early condition, and about one hundred and fifty seconds in the late condition. About ninety per cent of first helpers were male. And there was no diffusion of responsibility. The authors suggested a cost-reward explanation, where people weigh the cost of helping against the reward.

Method. Step one: for recall questions, learn the exact numbers. Step two: for scenario questions, choose the finding that fits the person. Step three: apply it to predict what will happen. Step four: for evaluation, give a point and explain what it means for the scenario. The traps are mixing up the cane and drunk results, and saying Piliavin found diffusion of responsibility. They did not.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Justin has confederates in a shopping centre queue. One steals a purse. We explain what he is likely to find, using Piliavin. Two marks. Step one: spot the variable that matches Piliavin. The real participant is sometimes male and sometimes female. Piliavin found something about gender. Step two: recall the finding. About ninety per cent of the first helpers in Piliavin were male. So men stepped in far more readily than women. Step three: predict for Justin. Male real participants should react to the theft more often than female ones. For example, by challenging the thief or raising the alarm. A tempting wrong route is to say that in a crowd, nobody helps because of diffusion of responsibility. Piliavin found no diffusion. That is Latané and Darley. The exam answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to mix up the cane and drunk results. The cane victim got help almost every time. The drunk victim got help about half the time.
