Idea. Your dog wags its tail when you get home. That is communication. But can your dog tell you about last week? Humans and animals both communicate, but not in the same way. Communication means passing on messages. Humans and animals both use sounds, facial expressions and body signals to show needs, warnings and emotions. A dog whines to go out. A person waves. In nineteen eighty-three, Aitchison listed ten features of language to compare humans with animals. Four are found only in humans: semanticity, displacement, structure dependence and creativity. Semanticity means words have agreed meanings. Structure dependence means word order changes the meaning. Creativity means making endless new sentences. Turn-taking is shared. Displacement is the key one. It means talking about things that are not here and now, such as the past, the future or imagined events. A dog only reacts to what is happening now. A question gives an animal and a person, and asks for a difference or a similarity, using an Aitchison feature.

Method. Step one: choose one feature that fits the stem. Step two: say clearly whether humans, animals or both have it. Step three: link it to what the person says or the animal does. Each mark has a job. One is for the reason linked to the scene. One is for the Aitchison feature. The trap is saying animals cannot communicate at all. They can.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Harry tells his dog Betty they will go for a walk in the woods when the rain stops. Betty wags her tail. We must explain one reason Betty would not understand the plan, using Aitchison. Two marks. Step one: notice what Harry talks about. A walk later, a stick, squirrels. None of it is happening now. Step two: the reason. Betty only reacts to the sound of Harry's voice. She cannot follow a plan about something later. Step three: the feature. Displacement means talking about things not here and now. Only humans have it. A tempting wrong route is to say dogs cannot communicate at all. Reject it. Betty does communicate, by wagging. What she lacks is displacement. The exam answer. Betty's reaction, then the feature, then the link. One mark for the reason linked to Betty. One mark for the displacement justification. Common mistake. A common mistake is saying animals cannot communicate at all. Animals use sounds, expressions and body signals. They simply lack some features of human language.

Your turn. Now you try. Angela and Missy chat about their future plans and an imagined house. Explain, using Aitchison, one way their communication is different from how animals communicate. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Angela and Missy talk about the future, going to university, and an imagined house. That is displacement: talking about things not here and now. Aitchison says only humans have it. Animals react to the here and now. A common wrong answer says humans talk but animals only make noises. That names no feature. It also says animals do not communicate. Always name the Aitchison feature. So, choose a feature. Say who has it. Link it to the scene.
