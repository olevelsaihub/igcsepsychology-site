Idea. A phobia is a strong, lasting fear of one thing. Freud believed such fears come from hidden thoughts in the unconscious, the part of the mind we cannot see. The Little Hans case study is Freud's report on one boy with a phobia. In nineteen oh nine, Freud wrote about a boy called Hans, five years old when the case closed. Most facts came from letters by his father, because Freud met Hans only briefly. Hans feared horses, especially horses with blinkers and black around the mouth, and horses falling down. Freud said Hans wanted his mother to himself, and feared his father as a rival. This is the Oedipus complex. The fear moved onto horses. This is displacement, which means shifting a feeling onto a safer target. Blinkers looked like his father's glasses, and black round the mouth like a moustache. Hans also had dreams about losing his mother. The case is rich in detail. But it is one boy, told through his father, and Freud's meaning is only his opinion. A simpler idea is that Hans saw a horse fall in the street, and learned to fear horses by association. A question gives you a person and asks how well Little Hans explains their fear or dreams. A nine-mark assess question needs the case, a match, and an evaluation ending in a judgement.

Method. Step one: recall the case. Step two: match each detail to the person in the question. Step three: evaluate. Give similarities, then a simpler explanation, then the single case. Finish with a judgement. Here is the plan for a nine-mark answer. One paragraph for each skill, and a last line for your judgement. The trap is describing Little Hans and never mentioning the person.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Amy dreams her parents have left her in a shop. Barbara thinks the dream shows Amy's fear of living alone. We explain two ways Little Hans supports Barbara. Four marks. Way one. Hans had dreams about losing his mother, and Freud used them to show his real fear. So Amy's dream of being left could show her real fear of being alone. Way two. Freud said a fear can appear as a symbol. Hans's fear of his father appeared as a fear of horses. So Amy's fear of living alone may appear as a dream about a shop. A tempting wrong route is to retell the case, horses and blinkers, and stop. That is knowledge, but it does not support Barbara. The question says support, so link to Amy. The exam answer. Two ways, each with a point and a link. One mark for each Little Hans point and one for each link to Amy. Common mistake. The most common mistake is a wrong detail. Hans was afraid of horses, not dogs. And Freud said the fear was displaced from his father, not learned.
