Idea. Think of a football team. You could study each player's kicking alone, or watch how the whole team plays together. Psychologists argue about which gives a better explanation. Both ideas have names, and you need to know each one. Reductionism means explaining complex behaviour by breaking it down into smaller, simpler parts. It is like taking a clock apart to see how each gear works. Each small part is simpler than the whole clock. The Multi-store Model of memory is reductionist. It splits memory into three parts: the sensory register, short-term memory and long-term memory. Each store can then be studied on its own, for example how long short-term memory lasts. Holism means studying behaviour as a whole, because the whole is more than the sum of its parts. A holistic approach looks at how the parts interact. For memory, that means how emotion, culture and schemas affect what we recall, as in reconstructive memory. Each has strengths. Reductionism is easier to test scientifically but may oversimplify. Holism is more complete but harder to test. A question asks you to define a term, give an example, or describe the difference.

Method. Step one: define the term in one clear sentence. Step two: add an example from memory, or for a difference question, use the word whereas and define the other term. Each step is worth one mark. Examiners look for clear, full sentences. The trap is defining holism as just looking at everything. You must say the parts interact, and you must give an example if asked.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to define holism as looking at everything. That is too vague. Holism means studying the whole, and how the parts interact. For memory, emotion, culture and schemas all affect each other.
