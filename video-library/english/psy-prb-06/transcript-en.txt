Idea. Does therapy really work? Psychologists run studies to find out. Young, in two thousand and seven, tested whether CBT helps with internet addiction. That means being unable to control internet use. She worked at a centre in the USA. Young studied one hundred and fourteen clients with internet addiction. They had online CBT. After sessions three, eight and twelve, they filled in a client outcome questionnaire. Six months after therapy ended, they filled it in again. That later check is called a follow-up. The questionnaire measured five things. Motivation to quit. Managing time online. Relationships. Doing activities offline. And being able to stop using problem applications. The findings. The main problem was time spent online. Men's most problematic application was online pornography, and women's was chat rooms. Most clients improved by session three, and the improvement mostly lasted at the follow-up. So Young concluded CBT can be effective. Now the weaknesses. Clients report on themselves, which can be biased. There was no control group, so we cannot be sure it was CBT that helped. And the clients were all internet addicts in the USA. A question asks you to use Young for a new person.

Method. Step one: recall the exact detail, the sample, the sessions measured, the measures, or the findings. Step two: for a scenario, compare the person's addiction with Young's internet addicts. Step three: for weaknesses, use generalising or method, and justify each for the person. The traps are wrong session numbers or follow-up times, describing CBT with no mention of Young, and giving the same weakness twice in different words.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Henrietta has a gambling addiction and will have CBT. We explain two weaknesses of using Young to judge how well CBT will work for her. Four marks. Weakness one. Young's clients had an internet addiction, but Henrietta gambles at a casino. CBT might work differently for an in-person activity, so Young's results may not show how well it will work for her. Weakness two. Young's clients rated their own progress on a questionnaire. This is self-report, and clients may say they have improved to please the therapist. So Young may make CBT look more effective than it would really be for Henrietta. A tempting wrong route is to say most clients improved, so it is a good study. That is a strength. The question asks for weaknesses. The exam answer. One mark for each weakness and one for each link to Henrietta. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The most common mistake is giving the same weakness twice. Different addiction, and gambling is not internet addiction, are one idea. That earns only one weakness mark.
