Idea. How long can you keep a phone number in your head if you do not repeat it? Short-term memory, or STM, is the store that holds a small amount of information for a short time. In nineteen fifty-nine, Peterson and Peterson tested this with twenty-four psychology students. They heard a trigram, which is three consonants such as C H J. Then a three-digit number. They counted backwards from that number in threes or fours. This stopped rehearsal, which means repeating information to keep it. After a delay, a light told them to recall the trigram. The delays were three, six, nine, twelve, fifteen and eighteen seconds. After three seconds, recall was about eighty to ninety per cent. After eighteen seconds it was under ten per cent. So without rehearsal, STM fades in about eighteen seconds. A further experiment found that saying items aloud, called vocal rehearsal, gave better recall than silent rehearsal. A weakness is that trigrams are artificial, so the study lacks ecological validity. That means it does not match real-life memory. A question may ask for the procedure, a finding, or a weakness. Or it may describe a person and ask you to apply the finding.

Method. Step one: recall the procedure in order: trigram, three-digit number, count back, light, recall. Step two: state the key finding. Step three: link it to the person. Step four: for a weakness, compare the task with real life. Two marks usually means a finding and its link. The traps are saying participants learned words or numbers, and forgetting that counting backwards stopped rehearsal.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. On Saturday Chelsea silently repeated ten items on the way to the shop. On Sunday she said ten different items out loud. She remembered more on Sunday. We must explain why, using Peterson and Peterson. Two marks. Step one: find what is different. Only the way she repeated the items. Saturday silent, Sunday out loud. Step two: the finding. Peterson and Peterson found vocal rehearsal gave better recall than silent rehearsal. Step three: link it. Sunday was out loud, like vocal rehearsal. Saturday was silent. So she recalled more on Sunday. A tempting wrong route is to say the Sunday items were easier, or she was less tired. The stem says nothing like that. Reject it. Use what the stem gives. The exam answer. State the finding. Then say what Chelsea did on each day. One mark for the finding. One mark for linking it to Chelsea's two days. Common mistake. A common mistake is to get the procedure wrong. Students say participants learned words and recalled whenever they liked. In fact they heard a trigram, counted back, and recalled after three to eighteen seconds.
