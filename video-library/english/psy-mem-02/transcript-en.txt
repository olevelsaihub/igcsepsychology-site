Idea. Imagine remembering a phone number just long enough to dial it. A minute later, it is gone. That is short-term memory, or STM. It is the store that holds a small amount of information for a short time. Long-term memory, or LTM, holds information for much longer. Duration is how long information can be held in a store. STM lasts about fifteen to thirty seconds without rehearsal. Rehearsal means repeating information to keep it. LTM can last a lifetime. Capacity is how much information a store can hold. Miller, in nineteen fifty-six, found STM holds about seven items, plus or minus two. So that is five to nine items. LTM capacity is thought to be unlimited. Chunking means grouping items so more fit into STM. Eight single digits are eight items. But grouped as two years, they are only two chunks. Students often swap duration and capacity. Duration is time. Capacity is amount. A question may ask you to define one, give a figure, or predict how people will do on a task.

Method. Step one: decide whether the question is about duration or capacity. Step two: give the accurate figure or definition for the store asked about. Step three: if there is a scenario, use the figure to predict the result. Learn the figures together. STM duration is about fifteen to thirty seconds. STM capacity is seven plus or minus two items. LTM capacity is thought to be unlimited, and its duration can be a lifetime. The trap in a scenario is predicting everyone gets all ten digits. STM cannot hold that many. Most people fail beyond about seven to nine.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Nishka shows participants number lists that grow from four to ten numbers. They only move on if they get the last list right. We explain what she is likely to find, using STM capacity. Two marks. Here is Figure 3, Nishka's number lists. Trial one has four numbers: eight, one, five, nought. Each trial adds one more number. Trial seven has ten numbers. Step one: the figure. Miller found STM holds about seven items, plus or minus two. So most people can hold five to nine items. Now look at the lists. They grow from four numbers to ten. Notice where five to nine falls. The last list is past the limit. Step two: predict. Almost everyone recalls the four and five number lists. The number who succeed falls as the lists get longer. Hardly anyone recalls all ten. A tempting wrong route is to say everyone will remember all ten. Reject it. That ignores the capacity of STM. Now write it out. The capacity, then the prediction. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is swapping duration and capacity. Duration is how long, in seconds. Capacity is how much, in items.
