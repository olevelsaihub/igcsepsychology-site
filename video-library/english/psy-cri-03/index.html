Idea. Some people love fast rides and loud parties. Others prefer a quiet night in. Psychologists say these differences in personality can help explain crime. Personality means the traits that make you act in your own typical way. Eysenck, nineteen sixty-four, said personality is mostly inherited and comes from our nervous system. He described three dimensions: extraversion, neuroticism and psychoticism. Extraversion means being sociable, impulsive and craving excitement. Neuroticism means being anxious, moody and emotionally unstable. Psychoticism means being cold, hostile and lacking empathy. It is not a mental illness. Eysenck said offenders tend to score high on all three. He also said extraverts are harder to condition, so punishment teaches them less. That is why some people keep reoffending. A question gives a person's actions and asks how this theory explains them, or how well it does. So you must know each trait, and what it looks like in real behaviour.

Method. Step one: pick the dimension that fits the behaviour. Thrill seeking is extraversion. No remorse or aggression is psychoticism. Anxiety is neuroticism. Step two: say that these traits are inherited and make crime more likely. Step three: link the trait to something the person actually did. Step four: for evaluation, give research support, then a different explanation such as learning from friends or family.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Kylee bought an illegal drug, took it at a party, then bought more to sell. Her friends warned her, but she carried on. We must give one strength and one weakness of Eysenck's theory for her behaviour. For the strength, look at her actions. She took a drug at a party and ignored her friends' worries. That fits extraversion, which is impulsive excitement seeking, and psychoticism, which means not caring about others. For the weakness, ask what the theory leaves out. Kylee says selling is a quick way to make money. Money is a reward, and learning from rewards could explain her selling, without any inherited traits. A tempting wrong route is to say Kylee is psychotic and ill. Reject it. Psychoticism means cold, hostile and uncaring, not a mental illness. Now write it out. Strength with a reason. Weakness with a reason. Each point earns one mark, and each reason earns one more. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to say criminals are introverts. Eysenck said offenders score high on extraversion, neuroticism and psychoticism.

Your turn. Now you try. Jamal promised to behave, but a day later he punched a younger boy. Explain one reason why he continued to misbehave, using Eysenck. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Jamal breaks rules and fights, even after his promise at the meeting. That points to a high extravert. Eysenck said extraverts are impulsive and harder to condition, so punishment teaches them less. So Jamal did not learn from the meeting, and he carried on. The common wrong answer just says Jamal is aggressive. That names the behaviour but not a dimension of Eysenck's theory. Without a trait and its reason, it loses the mark. So pick the trait. Link it to the actions. Then say what it means for the person. If it is a strength and weakness question, add research support, or a different explanation.
