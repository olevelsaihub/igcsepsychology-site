Stereopsis: seeing depth with two eyes

Idea. Close one eye and try to touch a pencil tip with another pencil. It is much harder. Two eyes help you judge distance. Binocular vision means using two eyes with overlapping fields of view. Each eye sees the scene from a slightly different angle. The left eye sees a little more of the left side. The right eye sees a little more of the right. This difference is called retinal disparity. Stereopsis is the brain combining these two slightly different images into one three-dimensional image with depth. The bigger the difference between the two images, the closer the object. A far object gives two images that are almost the same. If one eye is damaged, or the eyes do not work together, stereopsis gets weaker. Depth must then come from one-eye cues. A question describes such a person and asks why their depth perception suffers.

Method. Step one: say each eye gets a slightly different image and the brain merges them. Step two: say this gives depth, a three-dimensional view. Step three: apply it to the person. Which image is missing or faulty, and what does that do to their depth? One mark is for the two-eye explanation. One mark is for linking the person's problem to losing depth. The traps are saying stereopsis needs one eye, and forgetting the two images are slightly different.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Katie has nerve damage that limits the movement of one eye. She struggles to perceive depth. We must explain why, referring to a visual cue. Two marks. Step one: the cue. Stereopsis. Each eye gets a slightly different image, and the brain merges them into one image with depth. Step two: apply it. Katie's damaged eye cannot move with the other, so the two eyes cannot focus on the same object. Her brain has no usable second image to merge. A tempting wrong route is to name a one-eye cue, such as linear perspective. Reject it. One-eye cues still work with a damaged eye, so they cannot explain why Katie struggles. The question needs two eyes: stereopsis. The exam answer. The cue first. Then Katie. One mark for the two-eye explanation. One mark for linking her damaged eye to losing depth. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is forgetting that the two images are slightly different. Students say both eyes see the same image. If they were the same, there would be no depth. The small difference is what the brain uses.

Answer. Stereopsis is the brain combining the two slightly different images from the two eyes into one three-dimensional image with depth. To earn the mark you need the two images, the fact that they differ, and the depth. The most common wrong answer says stereopsis is seeing in three dimensions, or judging depth with one eye. It misses the two slightly different images, and one eye cannot do it at all. So, say the two eyes give slightly different images. Say the brain merges them to give depth.
