A-Grade Plan · 58 types

Research methods: Calculating the range · Finding the mode · Finding the median · Types of data · Calculating fractions · Calculating percentages · Calculating ratios · Ethics in research · Tables, graphs and distributions · Sampling methods · Improving a study: who and where · Drawing conclusions from data · Improving a study: method and measures · Strengths and weaknesses of a study · Observations and case studies · Questionnaires and interviews

Development: Mindsets and praise (Dweck; Gunderson et al., 2013) · Piaget's stages of cognitive development · Willingham's learning theory

Memory: Reconstructive memory and the War of the Ghosts · Retrograde and anterograde amnesia · The Multi-store Model of Memory

Psychological problems: Young (2007): CBT for internet addiction · Genes and depression: Caspi et al. (2003)

The brain and neuropsychology: Visual agnosia, prosopagnosia and pre-frontal damage

Social influence: The Stanford prison study (Haney, Banks and Zimbardo) · Why people obey - and how to stop blind obedience · Conformity to majority influence

Criminal psychology (optional): Charlton et al. (2000): TV comes to St Helena · Prison, community sentences and restorative justice · Operant conditioning: rewards and punishments that shape crime · Eysenck's criminal personality · Social learning theory and the Bobo doll study

The self (optional): Maslow's hierarchy of needs · Self-concept: who you think you are · Rogers: positive regard and conditions of worth · Measuring personality: traits, scales and the 16PF · Van Houtte & Jarvis (1995): pets and children · Free will: Baumeister and Vohs & Schooler

Perception (optional): Monocular depth cues: judging distance with one eye · Perceptual set: why we see what we expect · Visual illusions and visual constancies · Gregory's constructivist theory of perception · Haber and Levin (2001): judging size and distance · Carmichael, Hogan and Walter (1932): words change what we see

Sleep and dreaming (optional): Sleep disorders: insomnia and narcolepsy · Activation Synthesis Theory of dreaming · Hormones and sleep: the pineal gland and melatonin · Bodily rhythms: circadian and ultradian · Zeitgebers, light and Siffre's cave study · Freud's theory of dreaming

Language, thought and communication (optional): Types of non-verbal communication · Human language vs animal communication · Yuki et al. (2007): eyes or mouth? · How language and thinking develop: Piaget vs Vygotsky · Does language shape how we see the world?

Extended answers across topics: Issues and debates essays (9 marks) · Using two areas of psychology to explain a scenario (9 marks)
