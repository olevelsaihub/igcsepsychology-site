Psychology / Explain: a point plus its justification, tied to the scenario

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Psychology · 1PS0

Explain: a point plus its justification, tied to the scenario

Explain a reason, a way, a strength, a weakness or an improvement, often using a named theory or study and a short scenario.

Command word: ExplainUsually 2-4 marksAbout 1-1.5 minutes per mark

What Pearson asks

Explain a reason, a way, a strength, a weakness or an improvement, often using a named theory or study and a short scenario.

How the marks are earned

For each point asked, one mark for identifying it (usually tied to the scenario) and one for a justification linked to it, using the named theory, study or a research reason.

Method

  1. Count how many points are asked (one way, two strengths, a strength and a weakness).

    Why: Marks come in pairs per point.

  2. Make the point using a detail from the stem.

    Why: Generic points often miss the identification mark.

  3. Add 'because' or 'which' and justify with the named theory, study or methodological reason.

    Why: The justification mark must link back to that same point.

  4. If the question says 'You must use ...', name it explicitly.

    Why: Without the named theory or study the justification is not credited.

Answer frame

[Point linked to scenario], because [theory/study/reason that justifies it]. (Repeat for each point.)

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Giving a justification that does not link to the point made.
  • Ignoring the scenario.
  • Not naming the theory or study the question requires.
  • For improvements, not saying what the improvement achieves.

June 2024 · Paper 2 · Q24(whole)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (whole).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Linear perspective is a depth cue in which parallel lines seem to meet at one vanishing point on the horizon, so things look further away. Hakim can draw the roads and the edges of buildings getting narrower as they head towards the horizon, which will make parts of his landscape look near and other parts far away.

Why it earns the marks

First sentence shows correct knowledge of what linear perspective is; the second applies it to Hakim's roads and buildings.
