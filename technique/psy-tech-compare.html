Psychology / Compare: a similarity and a difference

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Psychology · 1PS0

Compare: a similarity and a difference

Describe the difference between two linked terms (e.g. nature and nurture), or explain one similarity and one difference between two things in a scenario.

Command word: CompareUsually 2-4 marksAbout 1 minute per mark

What Pearson asks

Describe the difference between two linked terms (e.g. nature and nurture), or explain one similarity and one difference between two things in a scenario.

How the marks are earned

For a 2-mark difference, one mark for a clear contrast and one for developing what the second term means; for 4-mark similarity/difference parts, one mark for each point and one for each justification.

Method

  1. Say what the first term or thing does.

    Why: Sets up the contrast.

  2. Use a comparison word (whereas, unlike, both) to bring in the second.

    Why: Makes it a genuine comparison, not two separate descriptions.

  3. Develop the second term with a definition or example.

    Why: The second mark is for this development.

  4. For similarity plus difference questions, justify each with theory or the scenario.

    Why: Justification marks must link to each point.

Answer frame

[Term A] is ... whereas [Term B] is ..., because [Term B] ... (e.g. example). / Similarity: both ... because ... Difference: only ... because ...

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Defining both terms separately without any comparison.
  • Giving a similarity when a difference was asked.
  • Leaving the justification unlinked to the point made.

Worked illustration

November 2021 · Paper 2 · Q41(whole)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (whole).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Similarity: Emily and Terry both use sounds to get a message across, such as Emily saying 'walkies' and Terry whining or barking, because both humans and animals use signals that the other can understand. Difference: Emily is able to describe her working day, which is in the past and not present, but Terry can only react to what is happening now; Aitchison (1983) said displacement, talking about things not here and now, is found only in human language.

Why it earns the marks

Each of the two points is tied to the scenario (one mark each), and each is justified with a concept: shared signalling and Aitchison's displacement (one mark each).
