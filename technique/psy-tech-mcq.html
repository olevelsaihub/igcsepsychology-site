Multiple choice: rule out the wrong options

Select one option, often a study detail (sample size, number of stimuli, timings), a definition or the correct concept.

Command word: MCQUsually 1 markAbout 1 minute

What Pearson asks

Select one option, often a study detail (sample size, number of stimuli, timings), a definition or the correct concept.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the single correct letter; more than one mark on the grid gets no credit.

Method

  1. Read the stem and recall the answer before looking at the options.

    Why: Stops plausible distractors steering you.

  2. Cross out options you know are wrong.

    Why: Narrows the choice if unsure.

  3. Mark one box clearly; if changing, follow the paper's crossing-out instruction.

    Why: Ambiguous marks score zero.

Answer frame

One clearly marked box.

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Mixing up numbers from different studies.
  • Picking a reversed definition (e.g. world shapes language).
  • Marking two boxes.
